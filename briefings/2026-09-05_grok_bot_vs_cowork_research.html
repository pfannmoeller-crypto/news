The argument in brief

Two companies, two persistent agents, two ideas of what "coworker" should mean.

SpaceXAI's case for Grok Bot

The pitch is reach: a cloud-resident agent working any app or website, even with no API or MCP support, driving a real browser like a person. It learns by demonstration, no setup; Bots coordinate directly. Reviewers Lenny Rachitsky and Matt Shumer said the standout was work that felt finished, not a 90%-there draft — worth reading against the fact that Grok Bot itself ships labeled "beta," and xAI's launch leaned on internal testimonials rather than published benchmarks.

Anthropic's case for Claude Cowork

The pitch is trust: visibility and permission over raw reach. The user sees every file opened, approves before anything consequential, and a task started on desktop can be checked from mobile. Tom's Guide and MakeUseOf praised exactly this — plan-first, history kept local — as what made delegation feel safe.

The argument, walked through

Six claims, in order. C-scale: 5 primary source · 4 two independent sources · 3 single source · 2 aggregator only.

The deal chain behind Grok Bot Feb–Aug 2026

"SpaceX and xAI combined into a single company valued at $1.25 trillion" — Bloomberg/CNBC (as extracted)

SpaceX and xAI combined Feb 2, 2026 ($1.25T valuation), renamed SpaceXAI July 6. SpaceXAI separately acquired Cursor for $60B all-stock — option (Apr 21), agreement (Jun 16), closing (Aug 14) — hence Grok Bot bundled into Cursor's tiers, not standalone.

CheckTrue C 5/5 — multiple financial/trade outlets corroborate; see Sources.

The "premium-only" framing Aug 2026

"included with SuperGrok, SuperGrok Plus, and SuperGrok Heavy" — xAI launch post (as extracted)

Several outlets framed Grok Bot as gated to premium tiers only, implying a $120-300/mo floor. xAI's Aug 11 post says otherwise: entry-tier SuperGrok and Cursor Pro qualified day one; Aug 26 confirms all Cursor Pro/Teams plans do too. This piece's own draft repeated the gated framing first — calcifying on re-report.

CheckMisleading C 5/5 — unsupported by xAI's own two posts naming entry-tier availability.

Cowork's pricing, precisely checked today

Usually cited: Pro $17-20/mo, Max 5x $100/mo, Max 20x $200/mo, Team $20/seat/mo, Enterprise "custom." Incomplete: today's page shows two Team tiers — Standard $20, Premium $100/seat/mo — and Enterprise is seat price plus API usage, not flatly custom. One shared computer, every Bot Aug 2026

"isolate personalities and workspaces, not compute" — xAI documentation (as extracted)

Grok Bot gives every Bot on an account one shared cloud computer — one browser, filesystem, credential set. xAI's docs reportedly warn against treating separate Bots as a security boundary. Five reviewers (Garg, Pyke, Huang, Nugraha, Lenormand) converge: a payroll bot's login is reachable by a recruiting bot on the same account; audit trails are "coming," not shipped.

CheckTrue C 5/5 — five named reviewers document the same behavior, sourced to xAI's own documentation.

Cowork's audit-log gap, marketing lagging Mar–Sep 2026

"not yet captured in audit logs or Compliance API" — claude.com/product/cowork (as extracted)

A March 26 MintMCP analysis found Cowork activity "explicitly excluded" from Audit Logs, Compliance API, and Data Exports. Anthropic's Aug 11 blog post says the API "now covers Cowork" across desktop, web, mobile — but the marketing page, fetched today, still carries the old sentence verbatim. Anthropic's own marketing contradicts its own blog post on a claim compliance buyers rely on; separately, Anthropic's own materials still describe computer use — the fallback Cowork uses for apps with no direct integration — as a "research preview," not production-ready.

CheckTrue C 5/5 — gap real and documented (MintMCP); page/post contradiction observable today, not explained away.

Both pitches oversell supervision Aug 2026

"potentially destructive actions" — via TechRadar (as extracted)

Grok Bot's "message it like a colleague" pitch coexists with prose-based approval rules, a "model judging model" Auto Review, no dry-run mode, no spending caps (eesel.ai, kingy.ai). Cowork's "asks before acting" pitch coexists with Anthropic's own warning of "potentially destructive actions" including file deletion, plus prompt-injection risk. Neither is dishonest — both disclose it somewhere — neither headline carries the caveat equally.

CheckTrue — both C 4/5 — independently documented on both sides.

Stepping back

The error direction in this run. Two corrections, opposite ways: Claim 2 corrected an understatement ("premium-only" was too dismissive of Grok Bot's real availability); separately, this piece's first draft, from an AI-summarized Wikipedia fetch, overstated a causal link between Cursor's founder departures and the SpaceX deal, and was dropped rather than hedged into print. One error each way isn't a base rate — both reads needed checking.

Where it's contested

Other viewsThe "coworker" framing is itself contested: Forrester's Anthony McPartlin reportedly calls it "nonsense," rebranded automation with historically limited payoff. Boston University research (Prof. Emma Wiles, via MIT Tech Review) found framing AI output as a "coworker's" cut error-catching 18% and raised manager-escalation 44% — a risk in the word itself, applying to both. The Cursor/SpaceX deal drew developer anxiety about Grok becoming Cursor's default — anticipatory; nothing landed. One comparison (eigent.ai) isn't neutral — a competing vendor pitching itself as the "real" option. Some mention a third product, "OpenClaw" — not verified here.
