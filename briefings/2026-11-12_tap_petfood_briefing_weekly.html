Morocco opens its market to US rendered animal proteins for dog and cat food

The North American Renderers Association, the US trade group for companies that turn animal by-products into protein meals, announced on 10 November that Morocco has opened its market to US rendered animal protein for pet food. Morocco's food safety authority published the health certificate agreed with the US Department of Agriculture, giving US suppliers an official export route.

No US plant can ship yet: Morocco must first publish a list of approved US plants, and no such list was found.

Context & background. Morocco first set rules in mid-2024 for importing rendered animal meal for pet food only, requiring Moroccan pet food makers to apply for authorisation to use imported meal. The 2024 rules covered the principle; the November 2026 step is the missing practical piece, an agreed certificate that US exporters can sign.

The details. The association says it expects modest early volumes. It is working with the US embassy's agricultural office in Rabat and the US animal health inspection service on the plant list. It also wants approval for rendered proteins in Moroccan poultry and fish farm feed; a technical training for Moroccan inspectors with the American Feed Industry Association is planned for January 2027. These are the association's own statements about its plans. We could not open the original Moroccan notice, so the certificate itself was not checked.

Why it still matters. Rendered meals are a main protein source in dry pet food, and the story shows where access to supply is widening: a new export destination for US producers of these meals, not a new source for European pet food makers. The size of the opportunity is unknown, since Morocco's pet food market is small and the plant list decides who can sell.
