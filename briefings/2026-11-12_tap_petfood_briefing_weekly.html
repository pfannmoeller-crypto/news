Morocco opens its market to US rendered animal proteins for dog and cat food
Morocco has published a health certificate agreed with the US, giving US suppliers an official export route for animal-protein meals in pet food. No US plant can ship yet, because Morocco has not published a list of approved plants.
●●●●○ 4/5
The details

The North American Renderers Association (the US trade group for companies that turn animal by-products into protein meals) announced on 10 November that Morocco has opened its market to US rendered animal protein for pet food. Morocco’s food safety authority published the health certificate agreed with the US Department of Agriculture. Morocco first set rules in mid-2024 for importing rendered animal meal for pet food only, requiring Moroccan pet food makers to apply for authorisation to use imported meal; the November 2026 step is the missing practical piece, a certificate US exporters can sign. The association expects modest early volumes. It is working with the US embassy’s agricultural office in Rabat and the US animal health inspection service on the plant list, and wants approval for rendered proteins in Moroccan poultry and fish farm feed. A technical training for Moroccan inspectors with the American Feed Industry Association is planned for January 2027. These are the association’s own statements about its plans. We could not open the original Moroccan notice, so the certificate itself was not checked.

The other reading

The opportunity may be small: Morocco’s pet food market is small and the plant list decides who can sell. This is a new export destination for US producers of these meals, not a new source for European pet food makers.

What to watch

Publication of Morocco’s approved US plant list; inspector training in January 2027.
