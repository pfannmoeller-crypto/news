LEADUS CAPACITY30 Jul
US pet food construction spending totals $1.5 billion in current and planned projects, led by Nestlé Purina, J.M. Smucker and United Petfood capacity add-ons
A report from Industrial Info Resources (IIR), an industrial-construction intelligence firm, tracks $1.5 billion in current and future US pet food-related construction and maintenance activity, including more than $275 million in capital projects already underway, per PetfoodIndustry's 30 July coverage. Nestlé Purina is adding an 80,000-square-foot expansion at its Fort Dodge, Iowa plant to enable Fancy Feast wet-food production; J.M. Smucker (Meow Mix, Milk-Bone) is expanding its Topeka, Kansas plant with a 28,000-square-foot addition; and United Petfood Producers USA — which makes private-label and store-brand recipes — has a new warehouse, distribution facility and dryer addition underway at its Mishawaka, Indiana plant, its first US factory.
chevron_rightRead more
Context & background

Industrial Info Resources is a industrial-project data and construction-intelligence provider that tracks capital spending across manufacturing sectors; its pet food-specific report was covered by PetfoodIndustry on 30 July, four days before this edition's standard 7-day window opens, which is why Production investments is explicitly widened to 11 Jul–10 Aug this cycle rather than left at the standard 7 days. J.M. Smucker's Topeka expansion had been mentioned only in passing, without figures, in this briefing's first (22 July) edition; this is the first time this series has reported it with specifics, sourced to IIR's data rather than to Smucker directly.

The details

Current capital projects (>$275 million underway): Nestlé Purina's Fort Dodge, Iowa expansion — the company's primary US site for Friskies cat food — broke ground in summer 2025 and is scheduled for completion in the first half of 2027, adding a dedicated Fancy Feast production line plus auxiliary processing and canning equipment. J.M. Smucker's Topeka, Kansas addition began construction this past spring and is expected to finish in late 2027 ($205 million, per PetfoodIndustry's earlier reporting on the project). United Petfood Producers USA's Mishawaka, Indiana plant — acquired from Wellness Pet in 2024 as the company's US market entry — is adding a warehouse/distribution facility and a dryer-facility addition. Beyond current spending, IIR is tracking 64 further future capital pet food projects worth a combined $1.2 billion, plus more than a dozen planned upgrades. Minnesota has the most future projects (nine), including Barrett Petfood Innovations' Little Falls plant (building expansions, packaging-line additions, a new wastewater treatment facility) and Tuffy's Pet Foods' Perham plant (dry-line upgrade, bagging/palletizing replacement, plus a 66,000-square-foot storage facility at another site).

Context

This lands directly alongside two threads this briefing has already tracked: the IRS's new "qualified production property" factory tax deduction (covered 2 August), which lowers the after-tax cost of exactly this kind of new US construction, and PetfoodIndustry's own recurring caution that pet food manufacturing capacity is outpacing demand growth. A $1.2 billion pipeline of future projects layered on top of $275 million already under construction is the clearest quantification yet of the scale of that capacity buildout, though it does not resolve the open question of whether demand will absorb it.

Watch-Items

(1) Whether IIR's tracked future projects convert into confirmed public groundbreaking announcements. (2) Whether Minnesota's nine-project concentration becomes a recognized regional manufacturing story. (3) Whether any future project announcement explicitly cites the new QPP tax deduction as a factor.

FRANCE3 Aug
France's pet food industry produced 1.84 million tonnes worth €4.8 billion in 2025 and invested €225 million in R&D and production capacity, its trade body reports
FACCO (Fédération des Fabricants d'Aliments pour Chiens, Chats, Oiseaux et autres animaux familiers), the French pet food industry association, released its 2026 market report showing local manufacturers produced around 1.844 million metric tons of pet food in 2025, worth roughly €4.8 billion (US$5.5 billion); about 52% was sold domestically and the rest exported. To upgrade and expand manufacturing capacity, FACCO's member companies invested around €225 million (US$260 million) in R&D and production facilities during the year.
chevron_rightRead more
Context & background

FACCO is the recognized French national trade body for pet food manufacturers, comparable in function to APPA (American Pet Products Association) in the US or FEDIAF at the EU level. Its annual market report, covering the prior calendar year, was reported by PetfoodIndustry's Jaroslaw Adamowski on 3 August alongside two other European items (Burgess Pet Care's acquisition of Bunny Nature and Musti Group's Swedish retail reshuffle, both covered separately in Brand moves below).

The details

2025 French pet food output: ~1.844 million metric tons, ~€4.8 billion (US$5.5 billion) in production value; 52% sold in the French market, 48% exported. Capex: ~€225 million (US$260 million) invested in R&D and manufacturing-facility upgrades/expansion across the year. Retail channel mix: supermarket chains remain dominant at 68% of cat food sales and 51% of dog food sales; pet-food specialty stores take 23% (cat) and 34% (dog); online sales are 21% (cat) and 28% (dog).

Context

France's €225 million aggregate 2025 capex, spread across FACCO's full domestic membership, is a useful counterweight to the single-site, multi-hundred-million-euro commitments this briefing usually tracks from multinationals (Nestlé Purina's CHF 520 million Mantova plant, for instance). It suggests the production-investment landscape has two distinct textures running in parallel: a handful of very large greenfield commitments from global players, and a much larger number of smaller, incremental capacity and R&D investments across a national industry's existing footprint — both real, but not directly comparable in scale.

Watch-Items

(1) Whether France's 48% export share shifts as EU trade dynamics (EU-Mercosur, EU-India, covered in earlier editions) take effect. (2) Whether next year's FACCO report shows the €225 million capex figure rising in response to the tax and machinery-cost trends tracked elsewhere in this briefing. (3) Whether other national trade bodies (Germany's IVH, for instance) publish comparable 2025 output data for cross-country comparison.

LEADSMALL-ANIMAL NUTRITION3 Aug
UK's Burgess Pet Care buys Germany's Bunny Nature, calling it the company's largest international deal to date
Yorkshire-based Burgess Pet Care has acquired Bunny Nature, a small-animal nutrition brand based in Melle, Germany, for an undisclosed sum. Burgess said the deal is "our largest international deal to date" and grows its international business "five-fold overnight." Bunny Nature will continue as a distinct brand, with its leadership team and customer relationships unchanged, according to both companies' statements.
chevron_rightRead more
Context & background

Burgess Pet Care describes itself as an independent, family-run business based in Thornton-le-Dale, Yorkshire, and "one of the UK's leading animal food manufacturers, catering for the country's top nine most popular animals" — dogs, cats, rabbits, guinea pigs, chinchillas, hamsters, gerbils, rats and ferrets. Bunny Nature, headquartered in Melle in Germany's Lower Saxony, makes nutrition for rabbits, guinea pigs, chinchillas and other small rodents, plus snacks, hay and accessories; the brand describes itself as founded "out of love for animals."

The details

Deal value not disclosed by either company. Burgess's own statement: "We have acquired Bunny Nature, a well-established German small animal nutrition brand with strong international reach... This marks our largest international deal to date and a major step forward in our global growth plans — growing our international business five-fold overnight." Bunny Nature continues operating as a distinct brand under its existing leadership and customer relationships, per both companies.

Context

This is a small-mammal and exotic-pet nutrition consolidation move, distinct from the dog/cat-dominated M&A this briefing usually tracks (Assisi Pet Care/Forthglade, Outward Hound/H.I.G. Capital, United Petfood/SmartPetPro). It is a reminder that "petfood M&A" as a category extends beyond dog and cat food into a less-covered adjacent niche — small-animal nutrition — that rarely surfaces in this briefing's usual sourcing.

Watch-Items

(1) Whether Burgess discloses a deal value in subsequent reporting. (2) Whether the "five-fold" international-growth claim gets a quantified baseline anywhere else. (3) Whether this catalyzes further consolidation specifically in the small-animal/exotic-pet nutrition category.

PREMIUMIZATION / ENRICHMENT6 Aug
The Farmer's Dog acquires enrichment-products maker Woof, pushing beyond food into canine wellness
Human-grade dog food company The Farmer's Dog has entered a definitive agreement to acquire Woof, maker of refillable dog enrichment devices including the Pupsicle. Financial terms were not disclosed; the deal is expected to close in 2026, subject to customary closing conditions. Woof, founded in 2019, will operate as an independent division of The Farmer's Dog, which says it has served more than 1 billion meals since its founding.
chevron_rightRead more
Context & background

The Farmer's Dog is a US direct-to-consumer, human-grade fresh dog food company. Woof created what the companies describe as a category of "refillable wellness systems" for dogs. CEO and co-founder Jonathan Regev: "Nutrition is the foundation of a long, healthy life, but enrichment and play are also essential. We've admired what the Woof team has built, and together we'll create even more ways to improve dogs' lives every single day." Woof founder Daniel Haarburger: "From enrichment devices to wellness solutions, we've dedicated ourselves to bringing real innovation to life... we're excited to join an amazing partner in The Farmer's Dog to set an even higher bar for the pet industry."

The details

Structure: definitive agreement, not yet closed; expected to close in 2026 subject to customary conditions. Woof will remain an independent division rather than being folded directly into The Farmer's Dog's core food business. Financial terms undisclosed by either party.

Context

This is a third distinct pattern of capital moving toward premiumization, alongside the PE buy-and-build model (Outward Hound/H.I.G. Capital, covered earlier) and the legacy-CPG internal-reallocation model (General Mills' fiscal-2027 pet strategy, covered 2 August). Here, a venture-backed, direct-to-consumer fresh-food company is diversifying via bolt-on acquisition into an adjacent hardware category — enrichment and wellness devices — rather than extending its own food SKUs or being acquired itself.

Watch-Items

(1) Whether a deal value surfaces once the transaction closes. (2) Whether Woof retains independent retail/DTC distribution or gets folded into The Farmer's Dog's subscription model. (3) Whether other human-grade or fresh dog food challengers follow with similar adjacent-category acquisitions.

Finland's Musti Group takes over Sweden's Gaston pet stores from grocery retailer ICA, opens talks on a longer-term retail partnership
Arken Zoo, Musti Group's Swedish pet-retail chain, is taking over three Gaston-branded pet stores (Uppsala, Västerås, Norrköping) from Swedish grocery retailer ICA, which launched Gaston as a pilot concept in October 2024 and, after a strategic review, decided not to continue operating it. Analysis

Four reads from a cycle split between two working sections and two genuine gaps

  1. Two consecutive empty PE & M&A cycles, even after widening to 30 days, is a pattern worth naming rather than a coincidence to smooth over. The 2 August edition's PE & M&A section was empty; this one is too, despite a wider search window. On a beat this narrow, that could mean genuine deal-flow quiet, or it could mean this briefing's sourcing (trade press and public deal announcements) is structurally slower to surface PE activity than it is for strategic brand M&A — worth watching whether a wider set of sources (PE Hub, Mergermarket press coverage) changes that picture in future editions.
  2. Production investments this cycle is landscape intelligence, not a single event. Neither item is a groundbreaking-ceremony story — one is an aggregated construction-spending report, the other a national trade body's annual output figures. Both are genuine, publicly sourced signal about where capital and capacity are moving, but they read differently than the single-plant announcements (Mantova, Vafo's Poland site) this briefing has typically led with.
  3. Brand moves keeps finding new adjacent spaces to consolidate. This cycle's three items — a small-animal-nutrition roll-up, a human-grade dog food company's move into enrichment hardware, and a grocery retailer handing pet-store concepts to a pure-play pet retailer — all sit outside the dog/cat dry-and-wet-food core this briefing usually tracks under this heading. That is a genuine expansion of what counts as "petfood brand M&A," not padding.
  4. The raw-material gap this cycle is a different kind of empty than the PE & M&A gap. It is not "no activity" — Peru's fishmeal squeeze is real and ongoing — but "no fresh, publicly sourced development this specific week" on top of a story this series has already substantially covered. Distinguishing those two kinds of gap (nothing happening vs. nothing new to report) matters for reading this section honestly over time.
