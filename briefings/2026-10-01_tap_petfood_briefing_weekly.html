Peru has still not set a catch limit for its second anchovy season, after cancelling the first

Fishmeal is ground, dried fish used as a protein ingredient, and its price tends to rise when Peru, which normally supplies about a fifth of the world's fishmeal, catches less. On 20 August Peru's government cancelled the first anchovy fishing season of 2026 in its main fishing region on the north-central coast, saying ocean conditions were unfavourable. (A season is a legal fishing window with a total catch limit.) It has not announced a limit for the second season, which normally opens in November.

El Niño is a periodic warming of Pacific waters that drives anchovies away from Peru's fishing grounds. On 14 September Peru's national El Niño study commission (ENFEN) said a coastal El Niño of “extraordinary” strength is the most likely outcome through January 2027 and could last until mid-autumn 2027.

Context & background.Peru's main anchovy region has two seasons a year, each with a total catch limit set after a fish survey by the Peruvian Sea Institute (IMARPE), the state ocean-science agency. The first 2026 season opened in April with a limit of 1,914,049 tonnes, the lowest in a decade apart from 2023, when an earlier El Niño led to the whole season being cancelled. Peru halts fishing when too many young fish (juveniles) are caught, to protect the stock. On 12 May it suspended fishing for 15 days and extended the stop to 10 June, because more than half of the fish caught were juveniles. About 451,000 tonnes, 23.6% of the limit, had been landed by 27 May (Aquaculture Magazine). The government then cancelled the season on 20 August.

The details.Last year's timing suggests when this year's decision might come. In 2025 Peru first allowed a small starter amount, a provisional limit of 500,000 tonnes announced on 1 November. Fishing opened on 7 November, and after a survey on 4–6 November the limit was raised to a final 1.63 million tonnes, announced on 12 November (Gestión, a Peruvian business paper; The Fish Site, an aquaculture news site). The same sequence this year would put the decision in the first half of November. The southern region already has a smaller allowance of 251,000 tonnes for 1 July to 31 December, which does not replace the main one. The Marine Ingredients Organisation (IFFO), the trade body for fishmeal and fish oil producers, reported world fishmeal output 28% lower in the first quarter of 2026 and 38% lower in March alone, across 12 reporting countries that make about 40% of the world's fishmeal.

Why it still matters.Fishmeal is a protein ingredient for pet food and fish feed, so lower Peruvian catches mean less supply for makers. Peru's central bank, which projected a 6.6% contraction of the country's fishing sector for 2026, expects the sharpest effects in the November-to-February second season (Aquaculture Magazine). The El Niño commission's September outlook covers the same months. Not yet known: the date of the institute's survey, the size of any starter limit, and whether a second season opens at all.

INSECT PROTEIN29 Sep 2026

Two insect-protein producers say their carbon footprint falls as plants get bigger

Insect protein is a possible substitute for fishmeal in pet food. Innovafeed and Protix, two companies that raise black soldier fly larvae and turn them into protein for animal feed, published a paper arguing that test plants overstate the carbon footprint (greenhouse-gas emissions per kilogram of product) of full-size production. This is the companies' own claim.

The authors cite Innovafeed's own figures: emissions per kilogram of protein fell fivefold between 2022 and 2025.

Context & background.Black soldier fly larvae are raised on organic by-products and processed into protein meal and fat. Studies of total emissions from start to finish (life cycle assessments) put insect protein between 0.5 and 77 kg of carbon dioxide equivalent per kilogram of protein; carbon dioxide equivalent counts all greenhouse gases as the matching amount of carbon dioxide. The paper says much of that spread comes from the size of plant measured: at laboratory or test scale, energy use, climate control and supplying the larvae's food are not yet efficient.

The details.Innovafeed builds next to industrial partners, recovers their waste heat and uses wet by-products from a neighbouring starch plant as food for the larvae. The authors say earlier research found this cut the footprint of the insect meal by 80% compared with a plant not sited next to a partner. They also compare insect meal with fishmeal and a processed soy protein used in feed, using a global feed footprint database, and say many conventional proteins score higher than full-size insect meal, though values vary by species, origin and processing. Innovafeed's target is 0.6 kg per kilogram by 2030.

Why it still matters.The authors argue that soy needs land and that fishmeal depends on wild fish stocks exposed to climate swings; that is their case for insects as an alternative. Not stated: volumes, prices or pet food uses.

FACTORY INVESTMENTHUNGARY30 Sep 2026

Nestlé's Purina finishes a $530 million expansion of its Hungarian pet food plant

Purina, Nestlé's pet food brand, has completed the five-year expansion of its plant in Bük, Hungary. It added three new production facilities (factory buildings) and a robotic warehouse holding 50,000 pallets, and raised the plant's capacity, the most it can make in a year, by more than 100,000 tonnes to as much as 500,000 tonnes.

The investment was 193 billion Hungarian forint, about $530 million.

Context & background.Nestlé bought the Bük factory in 1998 and has invested 325 billion forint (about $893 million) in it since; the 193 billion forint is the latest five-year programme within that total. The plant is described as one of Nestlé's largest pet food plants in Europe and as Hungary's largest food-industry development.

The details.About 1,500 Hungarian suppliers provide raw materials, packaging and services worth roughly 75 billion forint (about $206 million) a year. The head of Nestlé's Hungarian business said at the opening that the investment supports local jobs, farming and small businesses. Not stated: what food the new lines make or how much of the new capacity is in use. Other Purina builds reported by Petfood Industry, a pet food trade publication: a $550 million plant in Batavia, Ohio, opened on 31 August, and a 520 million Swiss franc combined site in Mantova, Italy, for super-premium (top-price) wet food, due to start in 2029.

Why it still matters.Capacity is what a plant can make, not what it does make; no figure for actual use is given. Brand-owned plants of this size are competition for contract manufacturers, the firms that make pet food for other brands or supermarkets, and for private-label producers, who make supermarkets' own-brand food.

COMPANY PURCHASEPRIVATE LABEL22 Sep 2026

Belgian pet food maker United Petfood agrees to buy a plant in Arkansas, its third in North America

United Petfood, a Belgian company that makes pet food sold under retailers' and brands' own labels (private label), signed a definitive agreement (a binding contract) on 22 September to buy a dry pet food factory in Decatur, Arkansas, from Wellness Pet Company. The two companies also signed a long-term supply agreement, so they stay trading partners. The price was not disclosed.

Decatur is United's third North American plant, after Mishawaka, Indiana, which it also bought from Wellness in 2024, and Drummondville, Quebec.

Context & background.United Petfood makes dry and wet food, biscuits and snacks for retailers and brands. It has plants in the UK, France and Poland, exports to more than 110 countries and employs over 3,500 people. In March 2026 it also joined Lider Petfood, a Turkish company, in buying a dry food factory in Kırklareli, Turkey, from Maya Company Pet Food.

The details.The deal covers the factory only, not Wellness as a company, and Wellness stays a trading partner through the supply agreement. United's chief executive said the site gives “additional capacity and flexibility” for customers. Not stated: Wellness's comment or when the sale completes.

Why it still matters.United has now bought four factories since 2024: Mishawaka (2024), Kırklareli (March 2026, jointly with Lider Petfood), Drummondville (April 2026) and Decatur, across the US, Canada and Turkey. All four are purchases of existing factories rather than new builds.

BRANDSPRIVATE LABEL30 Sep 2026

Lidl, the discount supermarket chain, merges its own-brand pet food into one brand with three quality levels

Lidl is rolling out “Max & Mila's World” in all its stores: one own-brand label for all its dog and cat food (private label: products sold under a retailer's own name), in three quality levels called Basic, Advanced Care and Gourmet. Gourmet is grain-free, meaning made without cereals.

Lidl says the recipes are 100% natural, with no added sugar or artificial colours, and follow the guidelines of the European Pet Food Industry Federation (FEDIAF), the European pet food industry's trade federation.
