ProductionTOP10 Nov
Mars puts £161 million into its Melton Mowbray plant, the birthplace of Whiskas and Pedigree
Mars will modernise its oldest pet care factory in England through 2031 and make it the main UK plant for pouched pet food. The money pays for new pouch lines and AI quality cameras.
●●●○○ 3/5
The details

Mars announced a £161 million (about US$216 million) programme at its Melton Mowbray factory, which opened in 1951. By 2031 the site becomes the company’s primary UK plant for pouched (single-serve wet food) pet food. It gets pouch lines able to use recyclable single-material packaging and AI-powered cameras that spot quality defects earlier and cut waste. More than 30 skilled jobs are planned in the first year, and the apprentice group grows to 33 in 2027. The factory sends 95% of its output to UK households and 5% to other European markets. Mars says the project lifts its UK manufacturing investment to £661 million since 2021 across snacks, food and pet care.

What to watch

Mars’ UK hiring and the first new pouch lines; no dates were given.

ProductionTOP10 Nov
Mars adds a $49 million line in Ohio so its Temptations lickable treats are made in the US
Mars is spending $49 million at its Columbus wet pet food plant to start US production of Temptations Lickable Treats. Capacity is more than 400 million pouches a year.
●●●○○ 3/5
The details

Mars announced on 10 November a $49 million investment at its Columbus, Ohio plant for new mixing and filling equipment. The plant will be able to make more than 400 million pouches of Temptations Lickable Treats a year; Mars has put $162 million into the site since 2022. The project sits inside a larger Mars statement: a planned $1.4 billion for US manufacturing and operations in 2027, which the company calls its largest single-year amount, and more than $9 billion since 2017. The same announcement lists $60 million for two frozen-snack lines in Salt Lake City, which is human food, not pet food.

What to watch

Mars’ 2027 spending plan; no start date for the Columbus line was given.

ProductionTOP9 Nov
Argentine farm group Cosechas Argentinas is building a dry pet food plant, due late 2026 or early 2027
The agribusiness (grain storage, soybean processing, animal feed) is entering dry pet food with a plant that makes 6 tonnes an hour. It also plans to make food for other brands.
●●●○○ 3/5
The details

Petfood Industry published the story on 9 November 2026, three days before the previous edition. The plant is in San Andrés de Giles, Buenos Aires province, with extrusion (cooking and shaping kibble), drying, grinding and coating equipment. Starting capacity is 6 metric tonnes an hour. Sales start through farm-supply shops, then vets, pet shops and supermarkets, and the plant will also make food for other brands (contract manufacturing). The trade writer notes Argentina already has many producers, so the plant adds competition more than it fills a gap. No investment amount was given. Start-up is stated as late 2026 or early 2027, not as a firm date.

The other reading

The author says the market is not short of capacity: another producer is a sign of confidence in recovery, not of a supply shortage.

What to watch

Whether the plant starts by early 2027; no amount or opening date has been published.

Production10 Nov
Citizens Pet adds 40,000 square feet at its Fresno plant and six senior hires
The California maker of air-dried dog food and treats now has more than 125,000 square feet in Fresno. It is expanding to make more of its own brands and to take on contract and store-brand work.
●●●●○ 4/5
The details

Citizens Pet announced on 10 November a 40,000-square-foot addition to its Fresno plant, for its Plato, Unconditional and Furry Republic brands. It also named six leaders: a chief commercial officer, a chief marketing officer, and heads of pet-specialty sales, supply chain and engineering, plus a former pet-food chief executive as adviser. Entrepreneur Avrum Elmakis bought a majority stake in November 2025 to grow contract manufacturing, private-label (store-brand) production and product development. The air-dried dog food Unconditional launched in September 2026 and sells through the pet retailers PetSmart, Petco and Pet Supplies Plus. No investment amount was disclosed.

What to watch

The first contract or private-label customers; none named.

Production11 Nov
Mexican fresh pet food maker Pet’s Table now runs a 6,000-square-metre plant near Mexico City
The home-delivery company has grown from a small kitchen to a plant with about 200 staff, shipping to 24 Mexican states. Trade press reads it as fresh food growing alongside kibble.
●●●○○ 3/5
The details

Petfood Industry (11 November) describes Pet’s Table, founded in 2020, as making freshly cooked and frozen pet food in Naucalpan. Reported figures, from press sources: nearly 200 employees, 24 states, more than 800,000 orders. Sales rose sixfold in 2023, tripled in 2024 and doubled in 2025, with operating profit reached, but volumes and revenue are not disclosed. Customers can buy a full fresh diet or a plan that mixes fresh with ordinary food. The article says frozen delivery costs more than dry food, so the open question is whether repeat orders cover the costs of refrigerated shipping and of winning customers. No new investment amount is given.

What to watch

Whether the company discloses volumes or new funding.

Tractor Supply puts pet food on Uber from nearly 2,500 US stores
The farm and rural-living retailer will deliver pet food and animal care goods through the Uber, Uber Eats and Postmates apps. It extends fast delivery to small towns and suburbs.
●●●○○ 3/5
The details

Uber and Tractor Supply announced a nationwide partnership on 13 November. Customers pick a store in the app, fill a basket and track the order, as with a restaurant meal. Besides pet food and animal care, the range covers lawn, garden and tools. The point for pet food makers is channel, not product: a large rural chain becomes available for same-day delivery where big-city delivery services rarely reach. No sales figures, delivery fees or launch date by region were published, and nothing says which pet food brands benefit.

What to watch

Whether other rural chains follow; no date announced.

Editor’s view

  1. The money is going into wet and treat formats. Both Mars projects are pouches: wet food in England, lickable treats in Ohio. Pouches need dedicated filling lines, which is why they get capital first.
  2. Small plants are being built to serve other brands. Citizens Pet and Cosechas Argentinas both say they will take contract work, a sign that owners of brands without their own plant are a market in themselves.
  3. Most plant news is from just before this week. Five plant stories are dated 9–11 November and carry their true dates; this week itself, 12–16 November, brought one retail story and no deal.
