DISASTER201613 Nov (universal time) · 14 Nov (New Zealand)

A magnitude 7.8 earthquake tore across the northeast of New Zealand’s South Island

At two minutes past midnight on Monday 14 November 2016 New Zealand time (11:02 on 13 November in universal time), a magnitude 7.8 earthquake began near Waiau in North Canterbury and ruptured towards the coast at Kaikōura. Two people died, one in Kaikōura and one at Mount Lyford.

The shaking lasted about two minutes and set off landslides that cut the town off by road and rail.

Context & background. New Zealand sits on the boundary between two tectonic plates and has a long record of strong earthquakes; the Christchurch earthquake of February 2011, far to the south, killed 185 people. This one struck in the middle of the night in a thinly populated region, which is a large part of why the death toll stayed low for such a strong event.

The details. Earthquake scientists were struck by how many separate faults broke in a single event. The rupture did not follow one clean line but jumped from fault to fault, which GeoNet, the national geological monitoring service, and later research papers documented in detail. Early reports gave a lower magnitude of 7.5; the figure of 7.8 is what later analyses settled on. The two-death figure comes from the encyclopedia and the Australian disaster-resilience institute.

Why it still matters. The quake is now a standard case study for planners, because it shows that hazard maps built around one fault at a time can understate what a real rupture does. It also showed how fragile a single coastal road and rail line is: the region’s transport link was out for a long time while engineers dealt with the slopes above it.

POLITICS201615 Nov 2016

Hong Kong’s High Court removed two elected lawmakers over how they took their oath

On 15 November 2016 Justice Thomas Au ruled that Sixtus “Baggio” Leung and Yau Wai-ching, elected to Hong Kong’s Legislative Council six weeks earlier, had “declined” to take their oaths properly and must be disqualified. Both had pronounced China in a way judged to insult it, and had displayed a banner saying Hong Kong is not China.

The ruling came eight days after China’s top legislative committee issued its own reading of the Basic Law, Hong Kong’s mini-constitution.

Context & background. Hong Kong has run since 1997 under a “one country, two systems” arrangement that promised it a high degree of autonomy. After the 2014 street protests, a small group of younger “localist” candidates won seats in September 2016. At the swearing-in the two used the oath to make a political statement.

The details. The committee in Beijing issued its interpretation of Article 104 of the Basic Law on 7 November, while the court case was still pending. It said an oath-taker who does not follow the prescribed wording, or who takes the oath in a way that is not sincere or solemn, must be disqualified. A research report for the US Congress notes that the High Court’s November 2016 decision rested only on Hong Kong’s own oaths law, whereas a July 2017 decision that removed four more lawmakers relied heavily on the Beijing interpretation. Leung and Yau lost their first appeal on 30 November 2016, and on 26 August 2017 the Court of Final Appeal refused to hear the case.

Why it still matters. It was the first of several disqualifications that thinned out the opposition in the Legislative Council, and it showed that Beijing could settle a local legal question by issuing its own interpretation of the law. Debates over Hong Kong’s autonomy still start from this episode.

SCIENCE201614 Nov 2016

The Moon came closer to Earth than at any full moon since January 1948

On 14 November 2016 the full moon fell within about two hours of its closest point to Earth, a so-called supermoon. NASA called it the closest full moon of the 21st century up to then; the Moon was roughly 356,500 kilometres away.

Observers around the world reported a moon about 14% bigger and 30% brighter than an ordinary full moon.

Context & background. The Moon’s orbit is an ellipse, so its distance from Earth varies by roughly 50,000 kilometres over each month. “Supermoon” is a popular label rather than a formal astronomical term, and it is used when a full moon coincides with the near end of that orbit.

The details. What made 2016 unusual was the timing: the Moon turned full almost at the same moment as its nearest approach, so the effect was close to the maximum possible for the modern era. The size and brightness figures are press-quoted and depend on the comparison chosen; a casual viewer could hardly see the difference without a reference. The NASA figures here are as reported by science outlets quoting NASA.

Why it still matters. It is a clear example of a rare-sounding event that follows predictable orbital mechanics, and a reminder that the apparent difference to the eye is smaller than the headlines suggest. The next comparable event is in 2034.

DISASTER200112 Nov 2001

American Airlines Flight 587 broke up over Queens two months after 11 September, killing 265 people

On Monday 12 November 2001 an Airbus A300 bound for the Dominican Republic lost its tail minutes after taking off from John F. Kennedy International Airport in New York and crashed into the Belle Harbor neighbourhood of Queens. All 260 people aboard and five on the ground died.

In a city still digging out from the 11 September attacks, many first feared terrorism. The investigators found a different cause.

Context & background. The jet had followed a large Boeing 747 off the runway. The crash came at a moment of heightened alarm in the US, two months after the attacks, which is why early official statements concentrated on ruling out a bomb or hijacking.

The details. The National Transportation Safety Board’s 2004 report found that the first officer made unnecessary and excessive rudder pedal movements, which overloaded the vertical stabilizer, the tail fin, until it separated. The board’s performance study found the loads came from those pedal inputs and not from turbulence left by the 747 ahead. It also named two contributing factors: the sensitive design of the Airbus rudder control system, and parts of the airline’s pilot training programme.

Why it still matters. The accident led to changes in how pilots are trained to use the rudder, a pedal-operated control that is meant to be used gently, and drew attention to how control systems interact with human reflexes. It remains one of the deadliest accidents on US soil.

CONFLICT200113 Nov 2001

The Taliban slipped out of Kabul overnight and opposition troops took the Afghan capital

On Tuesday 13 November 2001 the Taliban left Kabul under cover of darkness without a fight, heading east towards their stronghold of Kandahar. Fighters of the Northern Alliance, an anti-Taliban coalition, moved in at dawn and during the day, to scenes of residents cheering and honking horns, according to Associated Press reports.

It came just four days after the alliance had taken the northern city of Mazar-i-Sharif.

Context & background. The US-led campaign began on 7 October 2001 after the Taliban refused to hand over al-Qaeda leader Osama bin Laden. American air power backed the Northern Alliance’s ground forces, who had long held a northern pocket of the country.

The details. President George W. Bush had urged the alliance to stay out of Kabul until a broad-based government could be formed, but the Taliban’s sudden withdrawal left a vacuum. Opposition-trained police took over security in the capital and carried out house-to-house searches, while many alliance troops stayed outside the city. The encyclopedia’s day pages list the event under both 12 and 14 November; the news-agency reports are unambiguous that it was 13 November.

Why it still matters. The speed of the collapse surprised many observers and set up the UN-sponsored talks held near Bonn at the end of the month, which laid out the path to an interim Afghan government. The rest of the story, two decades of foreign presence and the Taliban’s return in 2021, is where the consequences of this week were felt.

BUSINESS200115 Nov 2001

Microsoft launched the Xbox in North America, taking on Sony and Nintendo in the living room

The Xbox went on sale in North America on Thursday 15 November 2001 at a price of $299, with the shooting game Halo: Combat Evolved as its headline title. It was Microsoft’s first video-game console, and reached Australia, Europe and Japan in 2002.

It sold about 1.5 million units in North America before the end of 2001, according to one reference source.

Context & background. Sony’s PlayStation 2 had launched a year earlier and dominated the market. Microsoft, best known for Windows and Office, was entering a hardware business with little experience of it.

The details. Microsoft’s own press releases confirm that the 15 November date was announced in September and that the game line-up was set in early November. The $299 price comes from Microsoft’s pre-launch estimate of $299.99; the 1.5 million figure comes from a single reference source and is not confirmed by company data.

Why it still matters. The Xbox gave Microsoft a lasting place in gaming: Halo became a long-running series and the console’s online service pioneered features now taken for granted. The Xbox brand is still a core Microsoft business twenty-five years on.

SCIENCE200114 Nov 2001

A magnitude 7.8 earthquake on the Tibetan Plateau ruptured a fault for roughly 400 kilometres

At 09:26 universal time on 14 November 2001 a magnitude 7.8 earthquake struck a remote stretch of northern Tibet. It was the strongest earthquake in China in about five decades, but in an almost empty region.

Scientists later showed that the break travelled faster than the speed of the seismic waves around it, a so-called supershear rupture.

Context & background. The earthquake is known as the Kunlun or Kokoxili earthquake after the fault and the area. Few people live there, so the human toll was small and the main legacy is scientific.

The details. A 2003 study by Michel Bouchon and Martin Vallée first reported the supershear speed, and a 2004 paper by Antolik and colleagues put the rupture at about 400 kilometres along the western Kunlun fault and the speed at about 3.6 kilometres per second. Sources differ on length: the encyclopedia cites around 450 kilometres for the surface break. A supershear rupture is like a sonic boom in rock: it concentrates energy along the fault line.

Why it still matters. Before this event supershear rupture was mostly a theoretical idea. It is now regarded as a real behaviour that matters for estimating how strongly the ground shakes near long faults, such as those in California and Turkey.

POLITICS197615 Nov 1976

Quebec elected a government committed to independence for the first time

On 15 November 1976 the Parti Québécois, led by René Lévesque, won 71 of the 110 seats in Quebec’s provincial assembly. It beat the governing Liberals of Robert Bourassa, who fell to 26 seats from 102.

Lévesque became premier of a province that is home to most of Canada’s French speakers.

Context & background. The party had lost in 1970 and 1973. Its programme was “sovereignty-association”, political independence with an economic union with the rest of Canada, and it promised to put the question to a referendum rather than declare independence on winning.

The details. The vote share of about 41% was well short of a majority, but the voting system turned it into a large majority of seats. Lévesque’s government passed the French-language law known as Bill 101 the next year and held the sovereignty referendum in 1980, which the “no” side won. Bill 101 and the referendum were not part of this week and are mentioned only as consequences.

Why it still matters. The 1976 vote made independence a serious option in Canadian politics. A second referendum in 1995 was lost by a very narrow margin, and the question of Quebec’s status still shapes Canadian elections.

CONFLICT197615 Nov 1976

A Syrian-led Arab force moved into Beirut and stopped the main fighting of Lebanon’s civil war

On Monday 15 November 1976 troops of the Arab Deterrent Force, most of them Syrian, entered Beirut peacefully after months of civil war. Syria had first sent about 6,000 troops into Lebanon on 6 June 1976.

The force had been created at an Arab League summit in Riyadh in October and was meant to enforce a ceasefire.

Context & background. Lebanon’s civil war began in 1975 between Christian and Muslim-Palestinian forces. Syria intervened in 1976 at the request of Christian groups facing defeat, then was drawn into a settlement that made its army the main power in the country.

The details. Sources disagree on the date. One month-by-month chronicle of 1976 places a Syrian column in Beirut on 10 November; the Arab Deterrent Force article, a United Press International report published the following day, and photo archives place the entry on Monday 15 November. We use 15 November and rate the item 3/5 because the sources conflict and no official record settles it.

Why it still matters. The civil war lasted until 1990 and Syrian troops stayed in Lebanon until 2005, so this week marks the start of almost three decades of Syrian military presence.

POLITICS197611 Nov 1976

India’s upper house passed the sweeping 42nd Amendment during the Emergency

On 11 November 1976 the Rajya Sabha, the upper house of India’s parliament, passed the Constitution (Forty-second Amendment) bill, after the Lok Sabha, the lower house, had approved it on 2 November. India was then under the Emergency, declared by Prime Minister Indira Gandhi in June 1975.

The amendment rewrote large parts of the constitution and is often called the “mini-constitution”.

Context & background. Under the Emergency, civil liberties were suspended, the press was censored and many opposition leaders were jailed. Parliament, with the opposition largely absent or imprisoned, could pass changes that would have been contested in normal times.

The details. The amendment added the words “socialist”, “secular” and “integrity” to the preamble and added a list of fundamental duties for citizens. It also reduced courts’ power to review laws. One source gives 10 November for the Rajya Sabha adopting the Lok Sabha’s changes and 11 November for final passage; most sources give the 11th, and the vote counts reported by one chronicle are unconfirmed.

Why it still matters. After the Emergency ended in March 1977 the new government reversed several of the amendment’s provisions, but the preamble words and the fundamental duties remain in the constitution and are still debated in India today.

Editor’s view

Two threads run through the week honestly. In 2016 and 1976 a ruling or an election turned a local dispute into a lasting constitutional question, in Hong Kong and in Quebec, and in both cases the larger fight came years later. And both earthquakes, in New Zealand and Tibet, taught scientists more about how faults behave than their small death tolls would suggest. The weakest item is the 1976 Beirut date, where the sources genuinely disagree.
