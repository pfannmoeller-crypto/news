Fresh Iran strikes send oil past $95, but stocks close the week at highs

Last edition closed on Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole surprise still working through prices. This window opened with a second front: US forces struck Iranian targets near the Strait of Hormuz on back-to-back sessions (Monday 31 August, Tuesday 1 September), knocking the Dow down more than 400 points and sending Brent toward $95 as Hormuz throughput collapsed to roughly a fifth of its pre-war level. Equities clawed almost all of it back by Friday on a Goldilocks jobs report, a dovish turn from Fed Governor Christopher Waller, and blowout AI-capex earnings from Broadcom, Snowflake and Hewlett Packard Enterprise — while gold whipsawed through a 3.6% one-day rally and gave up 1.7% on the week regardless, and bitcoin ended almost exactly where it started.

US strikes on Iranian targets near the Strait of Hormuz on Monday 31 August and again Tuesday 1 September knocked the Dow down more than 400 points and dragged the S&P 500 and Nasdaq roughly 0.7–0.9% lower each day, as Brent jumped over 4% both sessions. The rout didn't last: by Friday's close the S&P 500 stood at 7,747.71 (+1.1% day, +0.47% on the week), the Nasdaq at 26,584.06 (+1.4% day, +0.69% wk) and the Dow at 53,686.11 (+1.2% day, +0.24% wk), lifted by a solid August jobs report, a dovish signal from Fed Governor Christopher Waller and beats from Snowflake and Hewlett Packard Enterprise on top of Broadcom's AI-capex guide (earnings section, below).

Fed · jobs reportFri 4 Sep

August payrolls beat easily, and Waller signals he could support a hold

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported nonfarm payrolls up 162,000 in August, well above the roughly 53,000–55,000 economists had forecast, with unemployment unchanged at 4.1% and June-July revised up a combined 55,000 jobs. Hours before the release, Fed Governor Christopher Waller said he could support holding rates steady "unless inflation data shows a major surprise," a notably softer line than Warsh's Jackson Hole warning last edition flagged as the week's defining shock — and stocks, gold and bitcoin all caught a bid on the combination even as Polymarket's own Fed-hike pricing kept drifting higher on the week.

Iran · HormuzMon 31 Aug – Fri 4 Sep

Brent settles near $95 as fresh US strikes cut Hormuz flow to a fifth of normal

US forces struck Iranian targets near the Strait of Hormuz on Monday 31 August and again Tuesday 1 September, reversing the "breakthrough" this briefing flagged last edition and sending Brent from $90.55 (28 August) past $93, then to a six-week high near $96 intraday before settling at $95.04 on Friday 4 September — up roughly 5% on the week. Strait throughput has fallen to about 4.9 million barrels a day, down 77% from a pre-war 21.6 million (counted in vessel transits rather than barrels, the Geopolitics briefing's tracker puts 30 August at roughly 7% of the pre-crisis daily count — the two measures differ, both point the same way), and the EIA has extended its disruption forecast into the fourth quarter even as OPEC+ signalled no change to October policy at a Sunday Vienna meeting. Full detail on the strikes and the diplomatic picture is in this edition's Geopolitics briefing.

Context & background: two editions ago this briefing tracked Hormuz-driven oil to a record $91.84, before it reversed to $88.58 on talks hopes and then ticked back toward $90 last edition on renewed uncertainty. This window resolves that ambiguity firmly in the tightening direction — the "breakthrough" narrative is dead for now, and the throughput collapse is a hard operational fact rather than a headline framing choice.

The details: the 77% Hormuz throughput drop is the more structurally important number than the daily Brent print — it means the channel has been running at roughly a fifth of capacity for long enough that "tanker operators, insurers, and the refineries on the receiving end of those cargoes have had months to absorb the reality," per Eastern Herald's reporting, rather than reacting to a fresh shock. The EIA's upward revision to its Middle East shut-in production estimate, extending into Q4, is the forward-looking confirmation that this is not expected to resolve quickly.

Why it still matters: OPEC+'s own room to respond is constrained — the group completed its planned production-cut rollback through September, but actual supply lags official targets by 300,000-500,000 barrels a day, and Sunday's meeting produced no policy change. A market pricing 30.5% odds of WTI reaching $100 this month (odds board, above) reflects a real, growing possibility rather than tail-risk noise.

lightbulbEditor's view

A geopolitical shock and a hawkish Fed drift both hit this week — and markets absorbed both

1. Wall Street is now treating war-risk and Fed-risk as separable shocks. A 400-point Dow drop from fresh Iran strikes and a continued hawkish drift in rate-hike pricing (odds board, above) both landed this week, and equities still closed at fresh highs — a genuinely different kind of resilience than last edition's single-shock whipsaw.

2. Oil's de-escalation story from two editions ago is fully dead. Hormuz throughput at a fifth of its pre-war level is a structural fact, not a headline framing choice, and the market is now pricing real (30.5%) odds of WTI reaching $100 this month.

3. Gold's safe-haven role is being overridden by the rates story. A war in the Gulf would ordinarily support gold; instead it fell on hike bets and only rallied on dovish Fed talk, ending the week down 1.7% and more than 21% off its January peak.

4. The jobs report and Waller's comments point the same direction, but Polymarket's own pricing does not fully agree. A strong payroll print plus dovish Fed talk lifted risk assets, yet the hike-by-September and zero-cuts-in-2026 contracts both drifted higher on the week — worth watching whether that tension resolves before 16 September.

5. Europe is not one trade. The DAX fell nearly 2% this week even as Wall Street recovered — the mirror image of last edition, when the DAX rose the same week US indices wobbled on Warsh.

6. AI-capex earnings keep clearing a rising bar, but rewards are getting stingier. Broadcom tripled profit and grew AI revenue 221% and still traded flat on a guidance figure a few hundred million dollars light — a valuation the market is now pricing for near-perfection.

This was a two-shock week — a geopolitical escalation and a continued, if softening, hawkish Fed drift — and equities absorbed both by Friday even as gold and the DAX did not fully follow. Next edition should open by checking whether Hormuz throughput deteriorates further, whether the CLARITY Act's 15 September vote actually happens, and how the 16 September FOMC decision resolves the tension between this week's data (dovish) and Polymarket's own pricing (still drifting hawkish).
