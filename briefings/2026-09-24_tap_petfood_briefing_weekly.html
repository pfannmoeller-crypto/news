Pet Food Briefing · 18–24 September 2026

A private-equity handover and a $2 million capacity raise mark a quiet week for pet food

It was a genuinely quiet week for pet food news, and this briefing says so plainly rather than padding it out with older material. Three of the four areas it covers produced dated, public, single-thread stories — a small Indian pet food brand's raise to expand production capacity, the sale of a pet-supplement manufacturer from one private-equity firm to another (private equity: investment firms that buy companies outright, improve or grow them, and then sell them on), and a premium brand's exclusive entry into Italy — while raw-material inputs produced nothing new at all, even after the standard 30-day widening was tried for the two areas that qualify for it. None of the three stories below shares a single source with either of the others, and none leans on the kind of one-outlet analysis piece that supplied every figure in the private-equity section of the 19 September edition.

LEADCAPACITY9 Sep 2026 · 30-day window

India's Lickicious raises $2 million to build its first dedicated pet food plant

Lickicious, an Indian pet food brand founded in 2024, has raised ₹19 crore — a crore is ten million, so this is roughly $2.0 million — through a mix of equity (selling a stake in the company) and institutional debt (a loan from banks or other financial institutions). Prath Ventures, an Indian investment firm, was the lead investor — meaning it put in the largest share and organized the deal — with additional money from the founders of Indian appliance maker Atomberg and other industry figures. The company will use the funds to build a 60,000-square-foot manufacturing and distribution facility — roughly the size of a football field — its first dedicated production site, as it shifts from selling only online to also stocking physical and other retail channels.

It is a small raise compared with other pet food investment news, but a concretely dated one: the deal was finalized and announced this week, and the money is earmarked specifically for physical production capacity rather than marketing or working capital.

Context & background. Lickicious is run by Nuvexo Wellness Private Limited and was co-founded in 2024 by Shashwat Sahai and Chandan Jha as a digital-first brand — one that sold directly to pet owners online rather than through a manufacturing footprint of its own. That model can work at small scale without owning a factory, but it caps how far a brand can grow once demand outpaces what contract manufacturers — outside factories that make products for a brand to sell under its own name — can reliably supply, which is the gap this raise is meant to close.

The details. Beyond the new facility, the company says the capital will also go toward research and development, supply-chain investment, and expanding its product range into new formats, species and sales channels — moving it toward what the company calls an "omnichannel" model that combines its existing online business with physical retail. Co-founder Shashwat Sahai framed the goal in blunt terms: the company wants to become one of India's three largest pet food companies within a decade, and Prath Ventures' Harmanpreet Singh said the Indian market is shifting toward brands that can prove product quality and trustworthiness rather than compete on marketing alone.

Why it still matters. On its own, a $2 million raise for one facility in India is a minor data point next to the hundreds of millions multinational manufacturers are spending on plants in Thailand or Brazil. What makes it worth including is that it is the same underlying story at a different scale: investors are willing to fund dedicated pet food production capacity even for a two-year-old, domestically focused brand, in a market where most of the multinational capacity spending tracked this year has been aimed at supplying from the outside rather than serving directly.

EXIT— one investment firm selling to another1 Sep 2026 · 30-day window

Blue Sea Capital buys pet-supplement maker CompletePet from a rival private-equity firm

Blue Sea Capital, a private-equity firm, has become the majority owner of CompletePet, a Miami, Florida-based company that formulates and manufactures pet wellness products — soft chews, topical liquids, sprays, oils and powders — for other pet brands, with an additional facility in Milton, Vermont. Blue Sea bought the company from Align Capital Partners, the private-equity firm that had owned it since 2021; neither side disclosed a price. CompletePet's existing management team is staying on, putting some of its own money back into the company and keeping a stake in it alongside the new owner.

It is a sale from one private-equity firm to another — in industry terms, a secondary buyout — rather than a sale to an operating pet food company in the same industry (what the industry calls a "strategic buyer"), and it closed on 1 September 2026.

Context & background. CompletePet traces back to 2005, when the Martinez family founded it as Custom Veterinary Services; Align Capital Partners acquired the business in March 2021 in partnership with chief executive officer Worth Turner, who has continued running it since. In 2025, under Align's ownership, CompletePet merged with Green Mountain Animal, another contract manufacturer of pet supplements, which is how the company ended up with production sites in both Florida and Vermont.

The details. Turner said in a statement that the company is "excited to partner with Blue Sea Capital to accelerate our next phase of growth" in the pet wellness category. Blue Sea's J.R. Davis described the firm's approach as partnering with "exceptional management teams leading best-in-class companies" — language the firm used specifically in a healthcare-investing context, which is a reminder that private-equity buyers increasingly treat pet supplement manufacturing as adjacent to human wellness and healthcare investing rather than as a pet food deal in the traditional sense.

Why it still matters — and what is not known. This fits a pattern common in private equity: buy a company to serve as a base (a "platform"), add smaller companies to it over time ("buy-and-build"), then sell the combined business (the "exit"). But there is a gap here: no financial terms were disclosed, so there is no way to judge deal size or the multiple paid (how many times the company's annual profit the buyer paid for it), and only one trade-press outlet has reported it as of this edition. It says something about continued investor appetite for pet supplement manufacturing that a private-equity owner is selling to another private-equity owner rather than to an operating company in the same industry — but the scale of that appetite is not measurable from what has been made public.

LAUNCH22 Sep 2026

Nulo enters Italy exclusively through Arcaplanet, with production moved local

Nulo, a premium dog and cat food brand, has launched its full range in Italy exclusively through Arcaplanet, the country's largest pet-specialty retail chain, both in its stores and online. Rather than exporting product made elsewhere, Nulo says the Italian range is manufactured locally by Italian production partners — a choice it linked to Italian pet owners' preference for products with visible local origin, alongside the usual freshness and logistics reasons for producing closer to the customer.

The Italian range spans oven-baked kibble, freeze-dried raw formulas, wet foods, broths and pouches for both dogs and cats, built around Nulo's usual approach of high animal protein and low carbohydrate content with added probiotics (beneficial bacteria that support gut health) and omega-3 fatty acids.

Context & background. Premiumisation — trading buyers up to higher-priced, higher-claimed-quality products — remains the main way pet food brands drive growth, and Italy is a mature, competitive European market for an international premium brand to enter. A single-retailer exclusive, rather than a broad multi-retailer rollout, is a lower-risk way for a challenger brand to test a new country before committing to wider distribution.

The details. On the dog side, the range covers five product lines — freeze-dried raw, baked-and-coated kibble, a "raw medley" kibble, a "limited-ingredient-plus" kibble, plus meaty pouches and pâtés. The cat range mirrors that structure with baked-and-coated kibble, raw medley, "signature" stews, "silky" mousses and comparable pouch formats. No financial terms, volume commitments or exclusivity length were disclosed.

Why it still matters — and what is not yet known. Local manufacturing plus single-retailer exclusivity is a distinct strategy for entering a new market, compared with the straightforward export deal most brand entries into a new country use, and it is worth watching whether Nulo — or other premium challengers — repeat it elsewhere in Europe. Only one trade outlet has reported the move so far, and with no sales or volume figures disclosed, there is no public way yet to judge how significant a commitment this is for either company.

Editor's view

A quiet week that is honestly quiet, not padded to look busier

  1. Capacity investment is not only a multinational story. Lickicious's $2 million raise is tiny next to Nestlé's $196 million Thailand commitment reported in the 19 September edition, but it is the same underlying pattern — investors funding dedicated pet food production capacity — showing up at a completely different scale and in a different market.
  2. A sale from one private-equity firm to another, not to an operating company in the industry. Blue Sea Capital buying CompletePet from Align Capital Partners is a straightforward ownership handover inside the private-equity world rather than a pet food company expanding into supplements — worth noting precisely because no financial terms were disclosed, which limits what can be concluded from it.
  3. Three cards, three separate outlets, with no single outlet's reporting collected and repackaged by another to do the heavy lifting. Unlike the 19 September edition, which drew all four of its cards' figures from one trade-press analysis, this week's cards are corroborated across PetfoodIndustry and Pet Food Processing (production investment), Pet Food Processing alone (private equity), and PetfoodIndustry alone (brand movements) — a narrower spread of evidence per card, which is why two of the three C-Scores sit at 3/5 rather than higher.

This edition was researched using public web searches across all four scope areas, with production investment and private equity & acquisitions widened to the 30-day window after the primary week produced nothing in either area; source pages were then read directly for the figures cited above.
