TeslaTOP13–15 Nov
Tesla opened invite-only Cybercab orders at $29,990, but the car will not move without a $299 monthly fee
On 13 November Tesla began taking orders for its two-seat driverless Cybercab, with deliveries targeted for December. Buyers pay a $1,000 deposit, then $299 a month, and may drive only inside Tesla’s robotaxi zones.
●●●○○ 3/5
The details

The Cybercab has no steering wheel or pedals, is built at Tesla’s Texas factory and has run in limited robotaxi service in Austin since September. Per Teslarati’s (a Tesla fan site) reading of Tesla’s order agreements: the first month of the subscription is included; use is personal only, not for ride-hailing; Tesla can shrink the allowed area; resale needs written permission, with damages of at least $50,000 for unauthorised sales; three safety “strikeouts” trigger a one-week suspension of self-driving. Delivery is offered in Austin, Dallas, Fort Worth, Houston and San Antonio, one car per buyer. Musk replied on X that the fee is “too high” and should be about $115 a month, and that the car will be available nationwide as regulators approve. The YouTuber Marques Brownlee, who bet he would shave his head if Tesla sold a Cybercab under $30,000 before 2027, ordered one but says he has not lost: none has been delivered.

The other reading

Electrek, a news site often critical of Tesla, headlines the subscription terms “insane: $299/mo or it doesn’t move”; we saw only that headline. Tesla said on its third-quarter call that most Cybercabs will stay Tesla-owned.

What to watch

First customer deliveries, estimated for December; whether Tesla cuts the monthly fee; Brownlee’s own deadline of 31 December.

TeslaTOP12–13 Nov
Bloomberg says Tesla and SpaceX could announce a record merger in December, with China the open problem
On 12 November Bloomberg reported, citing unnamed people, that both boards are negotiating terms and an announcement could come in December or slip to early 2027. Neither company has commented.
●●●○○ 3/5
The details

SpaceX is valued at about $1.9 trillion after its June stock-market debut and Tesla at about $1.5 trillion, so a deal would be the largest on record. Open points reported: how many SpaceX shares Tesla owners would receive, and how to keep Tesla’s large China factory business apart from SpaceX’s US government and defence contracts. Tesla needs approval from half of all its shares; a 2025 board proposal won only 39.4 percent. Both companies formed committees of independent directors on 20 October; Musk said he is a “conflicted party” and stays out. The 3 December annual meeting has three items: re-electing Musk, chair Robyn Denholm and the co-founder Jeffrey Straubel, an advisory pay vote and the auditor. There is no merger vote, so a deal would need a separate shareholder meeting. Reports add that Democrats, who won Congress in the US midterm elections, pledged to examine Musk’s businesses.

The other reading

Electrek calls the independent committees a formality. It notes Musk owns about 20 percent of Tesla but about 85 percent of SpaceX’s votes, and that his 2025 pay award pays out faster if the company changes hands. That is Electrek’s analysis, not a finding.

What to watch

Tesla’s annual meeting on 3 December; any deal announcement in December.

Electrek Teslarati

Why 3/5: one underlying source (Bloomberg, unnamed people), relayed by two outlets; the Bloomberg article and any company filing were not opened.

Tesla13 Nov
Tesla now sells only the $109,990 Cyberbeast Cybertruck, two days after extending the line to late summer 2027
Electrek found that on 13 November Tesla’s US ordering page let buyers pick only the top Cyberbeast version, at $109,990, up $10,000 in about a week. Musk had said on 11 November that production runs through the end of summer 2027.
●●●○○ 3/5
The details

The Dual Motor ($82,490) and Premium AWD ($92,490) versions are greyed out on the page with no announcement. The Cyberbeast has gone from $99,990 to $102,490 to $109,990; the entry price was $69,990 in early August, so the cheapest truck you can order costs 57 percent more. Electrek saw no new Cybertrucks in inventory within 200 miles of Los Angeles. It estimates Tesla delivered 7,000 to 8,000 in the third quarter, about 30,000 a year on a line built for more than 125,000. Musk said on 9 November that orders “far exceed” production and that Tesla would have to return deposits to many people. Tesla has published no end date; the answer on the Cybertruck end date is still Musk’s posts, not a filing.

The other reading

Musk’s stated reason for the extension is working through the order backlog (Teslarati, 11 November). Electrek reads the backlog as an end-of-life rush Tesla created by announcing the end.

What to watch

Whether Tesla names an end date at the 3 December meeting, and whether the cheaper pre-orders are built.

SpaceX 2 stories

SpaceXTOP13–14 Nov
SpaceX caught Starship’s upper stage for the first time, but lost the booster on the same flight
Starship’s 15th test flight lifted off on 13 November. The upper stage returned from orbit and was caught by the launch tower’s arms on 14 November; the booster aborted its catch and was destroyed over the Gulf of Mexico.
●●●●● 5/5
The details

Starship is SpaceX’s fully reusable rocket in two parts, the Super Heavy booster and the Ship upper stage. Flight 15 left Starbase, SpaceX’s Texas launch site, on 13 November and released 26 next-generation Starlink V3 satellites (SpaceX’s satellite-internet service); three carried cameras that checked the Ship’s heat shield. After about 22.5 hours and 14 orbits, Ship 42 settled into the tower’s “chopstick” arms at 4:20 p.m. US Eastern time on 14 November, the first catch of an orbital rocket’s upper stage. The booster never reached the tower: it aborted its own catch and its safety system destroyed it just above the water. Musk wrote that it ran short of fuel because its engines slightly underperformed, and that the fix is a newer version of its Raptor engine (V3.2) plus software that cuts the engines about one second earlier. Teslarati reports that the FAA (the US aviation regulator) granted the licence on launch day, which settles the open item from the last edition. No Ship has yet been reused, and refuelling a Ship in orbit is still untested.

The other reading

SpaceX spokesman Dan Huot first blamed a guidance problem, then said on 14 November that the guidance system worked and triggered the abort; SpaceX was “still digging into the root cause” (the underlying reason) (Space.com). The fuel explanation is Musk’s own post, relayed by Teslarati.

What to watch

SpaceX has not given a date for Flight 16 or published the booster’s underlying cause as of 16 November. Musk has said he expects weekly Starship launches in the first half of 2027.

SpaceX12 Nov
SpaceX put a $50 billion price on a computing campus filed under another name; local votes come 9 December
On 12 November SpaceX said its planned Arizona computing campus would cost about $50 billion, and over $74 billion with a Texas site. The Arizona application was filed under the name Tombstone Resources.
●●●○○ 3/5
The details

The campus sits in Cochise County, about 80 miles east of Tucson, on 747 acres with up to nine buildings. SpaceX plans four phases of about 1.5 gigawatts (a measure of electric power) each, 6 in total, with a large battery and $3.6 billion of grid upgrades; an Arizona Republic analysis puts that at about 2.5 times the power of all data centres in Maricopa County combined. It would sit beside a coal and gas plant that SpaceX says it could expand. SpaceX told residents that putting its name on the filing “draws attention”. A petition against the plan has about 2,400 signatures. Separately, the city commission of Starbase (SpaceX’s Texas launch town) votes on a property-tax break for a $24 billion gas plant and computing site. SpaceX said it ended the third quarter with 1.7 gigawatts of computing capacity.

The other reading

SpaceX says the campus would use dry cooling with almost no groundwater and could cut local cooperative electricity bills by up to 20 percent. Opponents cite protected sandhill cranes at the nearby dry lake.

What to watch

Written comments to Cochise County are due 27 November; both the Arizona planning hearing (Bisbee) and the Starbase vote fall on 9 December.

Editor’s view

  1. The ship catch is real; the cadence is not yet. One caught ship and one lost booster show the hardest piece works, but nothing has been flown twice quickly, and Musk’s weekly launches are a 2027 promise.
  2. The $29,990 price comes with strings. The fee, driving-zone limit and resale terms mean Tesla keeps control of the car. Musk calling the fee too high suggests it will change.
  3. The merger is a report, not an event. It rests on one news agency’s unnamed sources; the 3 December meeting has no merger vote, so look for a filing.
  4. The Cybertruck end date is still talk. The price rises and pulled versions are what Tesla did; the 2027 date is what Musk said.
