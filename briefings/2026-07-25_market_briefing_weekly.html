The AI-spending verdict finally landed — and it was a "no": Alphabet and Tesla lost hundreds of billions in value in the Nasdaq's worst session in a month, while Houthi tankers strikes sent Brent above $100 for the first time since May.

This was a short, dense week. Wednesday's edition closed with markets waiting on Alphabet and Tesla; Thursday delivered the answer, and it wasn't the one bulls wanted. Both companies beat revenue estimates, but Alphabet raised full-year capex to $205B (from a $195B estimate) and Tesla's adjusted EPS of $0.33 missed the $0.51 estimate — enough to send Alphabet down 7% and Tesla down roughly 14.4%, dragging the Nasdaq to its steepest one-day drop in weeks and pulling the memory-chip rally this briefing covered last week into reverse. Overnight, the geopolitical story that has shadowed markets all month also broke worse: Houthi missile and drone strikes on two Saudi oil tankers in the Red Sea pushed Brent above $100/barrel for the first time since May, even as the 10-day Hormuz ceasefire proposal flagged last week went nowhere. Friday brought partial stabilization — Apple rallied 3.5% on its comparatively frugal AI strategy, Intel jumped on a genuine earnings beat, oil retreated from its peak, and Frankfurt's DAX closed back above 25,000 — but the week ends with Nasdaq down roughly 2% since last Friday and the "spending versus returns" question this briefing has tracked for a month now answered, at least for now, in the market's favor of caution.

Big TechWed 22 Jul (report) → Thu 23 Jul (reaction)

Alphabet and Tesla report — and the market delivers its verdict: beats aren't enough anymore

Both companies beat on revenue. Alphabet posted $119.8B revenue (vs. $117B est.) and $2.26 EPS (vs. $2.24 est.), with Google Cloud at $24.8B (beat) and search revenue up 17%. Tesla posted $28.2B revenue (vs. $25.7B est.) and record deliveries above 480,000 vehicles, but adjusted EPS of $0.33 missed the $0.51 estimate and gross margin slipped to 16.8% from 17.2% a year earlier. Neither beat mattered: Alphabet fell 7% and Tesla dropped roughly 14.4% on Thursday, as Alphabet's decision to raise full-year capex to $205B (from a $195B estimate, including $44.9B for cloud infrastructure alone) read as exactly the wrong signal to a market that, per this edition's own framing last week, has moved from "pricing in promise to pricing in execution."

Kontext & Vorgeschichte: Last week's briefing called this "the first 'Magnificent Seven' test since the AI-capex scare began" and quoted LPL Financial's framing directly: "The market is moving from pricing in promise to pricing in execution... investors should focus less on who is spending the most and more on who is generating measurable returns." It also flagged that Alphabet had raised $80B in equity in June specifically to fund its capex plan — an unusual move for a company with Alphabet's free cash flow — and that Warren Buffett had personally built Berkshire's $31B Alphabet stake while warning the AI giants are "playing a game they don't want to play." Tesla entered the print already the week's most sold-off Magnificent Seven name, down roughly 3% the prior Monday alone on Iran-driven risk-off flows.

Die Details: The headline numbers were genuinely strong on a standalone basis — Alphabet's revenue and EPS both beat, Google Cloud beat, search grew 17%, and Tesla delivered a record 480,000-plus vehicles while also beating revenue estimates. What moved the stocks was the capex line and the margin line, not the top line: Alphabet's raise to $205B guidance (versus a $195B Street estimate, itself already elevated) came with $44.9B earmarked for cloud infrastructure alone, and management offered no correspondingly concrete new monetization metric to offset it. Tesla's adjusted EPS of $0.33 against a $0.51 estimate was a genuine, unambiguous miss, and gross margin compression to 16.8% from 17.2% undercut the delivery-record narrative. The market's reaction — Alphabet −7%, Tesla −14.4% — was disproportionate to the magnitude of the misses in dollar terms, which is itself informative: this is the thin-positioning dynamic flagged in last week's Goldman hedge-fund data playing out in real time, where light institutional exposure to tech means earnings surprises (in either direction) move stocks harder than they would in a normally-positioned market.

Einordnung: This is the cleanest possible resolution of the question this briefing has been tracking for a month: is AI infrastructure spending being read as evidence of demand, or as a red flag on returns? For the two highest-profile reporters of the cycle so far, the market chose the latter reading, even where the underlying business numbers (Alphabet's beats, Tesla's delivery record) were solid. That is a meaningfully different outcome than the "healthy reset, not deterioration" framing that dominated Tuesday's memory-chip rally just two days earlier, and it directly undercuts that framing's staying power — see the memory-stock reversal story below, which happened the very next session. The AMD/Microsoft Helios story from last week's edition, read as demand-side evidence, and this week's Alphabet/Tesla reaction, read as spending-fatigue evidence, are not strictly contradictory — hyperscaler infrastructure deals and single-company capex guidance are different signals — but the market is currently unable or unwilling to hold both ideas at once, and this week it swung hard toward the skeptical reading.

Watch-Items: Whether Meta and Microsoft, reporting in the days ahead, face the same "beat isn't enough" bar or get more benefit of the doubt given different capex trajectories. Whether Alphabet's Gemini 3.5 Pro — flagged last week as running behind schedule — shows any monetization progress in coming weeks that could repair sentiment. Tesla's delivery and margin trajectory into Q3. And whether Friday's partial stabilization (see Apple and Intel below) represents the market drawing a distinction between "spending with a return story" and "spending without one," rather than a blanket AI-capex reversal.

Last week's memory-chip rally reverses hard: SK Hynix and Micron sink 6%, SanDisk drops 9% on a Korea-led selloff

The stocks remain dramatically up on the year regardless — Micron +227%, SanDisk +526%, Western Digital +209% YTD — consistent with profit-taking after an extraordinary run rather than a reversal of the underlying demand thesis.

Intel's Q2 beat is real — 25% revenue growth, upbeat Q3 guide — but the market barely blinked

Intel posted $16.13B revenue, up 25% year-over-year and 12% above the $14.45B consensus, with non-GAAP EPS of $0.42 against a $0.22 estimate. Intel is up 178% year-to-date.

ECB holds at 2.25% as expected — but Lagarde reveals a split council and markets now price a September hike

The European Central Bank left its main rate at 2.25%, in line with expectations. President Christine Lagarde disclosed that some governors had questioned whether a further rate increase was already justified, and said the council was "not giving any" advance commitment on future moves — language read as leaving the door open rather than closing it. On inflation, Lagarde noted "second-round inflation effects have yet to show up in data," suggesting the ECB does not yet see a wage-price spiral requiring immediate tightening despite the energy shock covered below. Iran · Red SeaThu 23 – Fri 24 Jul

Oil tops $100 for the first time since May as Houthis strike two Saudi tankers in the Red Sea — then retreats as fast as it spiked

The 10-day Hormuz ceasefire this briefing flagged last week as a live possibility did not materialize; instead, the conflict widened. Yemen's Houthis struck two Saudi oil tankers — the Encelia and Layla — with missiles and drones in the Red Sea on Thursday, citing violations of their declared blockade of Saudi ports; a fire broke out on at least one vessel, though all crew were reported safe. Brent spiked to roughly $101.10 (+6.45%), its highest in eight weeks and matching its late-May peak; WTI hit ~$91.49 (+5.37%). Five additional Saudi tankers had already diverted course earlier in the week, with cargoes bound for China and India rerouted — significant given Saudi Arabia has redirected more than 70% of its Gulf exports through Red Sea routes in recent months. The spike proved as sharp as it was short-lived: by Friday, Brent had eased back to ~$97.6 and WTI to ~$90.1 (−2.25%), helping lift European equities and easing some of the FOMC-adjacent inflation anxiety covered above. This briefing's companion geopolitics edition carries the full military and diplomatic detail.

Kontext & Vorgeschichte: Last week's briefing left the Houthi threat against Saudi shipping as a freshly-opened "second front," distinct from the Hormuz disruption tied to the direct US-Iran conflict, and explicitly noted "the two threats are not fully substitutable and a resolution of one does not resolve the other." That warning proved accurate within days: rather than the mediators' 10-day ceasefire proposal advancing, the Red Sea front escalated into direct tanker strikes, while the Hormuz/Iran story continued in parallel (this week marking, per one tracker, close to the two-week mark of the renewed escalation).

Die Details: The Encelia and Layla strikes were confirmed by the Saudi Press Agency, which called them "a violation of international laws and conventions that guarantee the safety of commercial vessels." Oil's move captured a genuine structural risk, not just a headline spike: Saudi Arabia has been routing more than 70% of its Gulf exports through Red Sea corridors specifically to avoid Hormuz risk, meaning a credible threat to Red Sea shipping removes the alternate route that had been partially insulating Saudi exports from the Hormuz conflict. Oil's five consecutive up-sessions through Thursday und the jump to $101.10 (Brent) reflected that logic. The Friday retreat to $97.6 tracked easing of the most acute fear (no further strikes reported, diverted tankers apparently finding alternate paths) rather than any resolution of the underlying threat.

Einordnung: This week is the clearest illustration yet of the pattern this briefing flagged as the defining oil dynamic of the month: a market pricing a conflict that neither resolves nor escalates cleanly, moving in sharp bursts on individual incidents rather than settling into a stable risk premium. The read-through to equities and rates is direct — Friday's oil retreat helped DAX and other European indices post gains even as Wall Street digested the Alphabet/Tesla fallout, and the FOMC section above shows how tightly Fed hike-odds are now tracking the oil tape on a session-by-session basis. Unlike a month ago, when this briefing could point to a single chokepoint (Hormuz) as the transmission channel, there are now two independent geographic risks (Hormuz and the Red Sea/Bab el-Mandeb corridor) that can each move oil on their own, which structurally raises the floor under volatility even during weeks with encouraging headlines.

Watch-Items: Whether the Houthis follow through on further Red Sea strikes or this proves an isolated incident. The fate of the 10-day Hormuz ceasefire proposal, last reported as under discussion but not confirmed accepted by either side. Confirmed Hormuz transit volumes and Bab el-Mandeb diversion data as the two chokepoints' combined effect on actual physical crude flows. And whether the July CPI print, due mid-August, shows this week's price action feeding into headline inflation the way May's spike did.

Regulationwk of 20–24 Jul

CLARITY Act clears Senate Banking Committee 15–9, heads to a full floor vote — but ethics provisions could still delay it

The crypto industry's push for a federal regulatory framework advanced a concrete step: the CLARITY Act cleared the Senate Banking Committee on a 15–9 vote and is now headed to the full Senate floor, having already passed the House. It still needs a 60-vote Senate majority, House-Senate reconciliation, and a presidential signature. The bill would delineate SEC/CFTC authority over digital assets and, per recent reporting, would bar sitting presidents and other officials from issuing or holding crypto tokens until 2029 — an ethics provision that has drawn scrutiny and could still delay passage. Prediction markets put the odds of the act being signed into law in 2026 at roughly 32.5% as of midweek, reflecting real but far from certain momentum.

lightbulbEditor's view

The market answered its own question this week — spending alone no longer buys a pass

1. Alphabet and Tesla beat on revenue and were punished anyway. That is the single cleanest data point this briefing has produced in a month of asking whether AI capex reads as demand or as risk. A $205B capex raise without a matching new monetization story cost Alphabet 7% in a session; a genuine delivery record couldn't save Tesla from a 14.4% drop on an EPS miss and margin compression.

2. The memory-chip rally this briefing covered last week didn't survive the week. SK Hynix, Micron and SanDisk gave back most of Tuesday's gains by Friday on a Korea-linked selloff, confirming the suspicion flagged in last edition's own analysis: that bounce leaned more on short-covering than on fresh conviction.

3. Apple and Intel show the market isn't punishing tech broadly — it's punishing unconvincing capex specifically. Apple rallied 3.5% for spending less on AI infrastructure than peers; Intel rallied on a genuine 25%-revenue-growth beat-and-raise. The bar has shifted from "are you spending" to "does the spending or the report tell a believable story," and that bar is now being applied unevenly but consistently.

4. Oil's two-front risk is structurally higher than a month ago. Houthi strikes on Saudi tankers opened a second chokepoint (Red Sea/Bab el-Mandeb) independent of the Hormuz/Iran conflict, meaning oil can now spike on developments in either theater. Brent's round trip from $91 to $101 to $97.6 within days shows how little of the current premium is stable.

5. The Fed faces a genuinely split market for the first time this cycle. Polymarket (80% hold) and CME FedWatch (~64% hold) disagree by double digits heading into Tuesday's meeting — a gap this briefing hasn't seen this wide before, and one the oil price will likely keep moving right up to the decision.

6. Bitcoin's decoupling from oil-driven risk-off flows continued. It held near $65,000 through a week that saw the Nasdaq drop 2% and oil spike past $100 — though the broken ETF-inflow streak and rising Treasury yields are the first concrete cracks in that resilience this briefing has recorded.

The wildcard heading into next week: Tuesday's FOMC lands the same week Meta, Microsoft, Amazon and Apple report — meaning the market gets a second, larger round of the exact test Alphabet and Tesla just failed, against a Fed decision that is unusually uncertain even by this month's standards. This edition's read is that positioning, not conviction, will keep driving outsized moves in both directions until one of those two events breaks the pattern.
