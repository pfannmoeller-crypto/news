Tame CPI sends the S&P to a record, then oil's Hormuz surge drags it back within a week

Last edition closed on a short, thin session cut short by an oil headline and Intel's stock sale, with Wednesday's CPI print flagged as the week's real test. It landed almost exactly as forecast — and for two sessions the market treated it that way, pushing the S&P 500 to a fresh record close on CPI day and again by Friday. Then the ground shifted back: July's retail sales posted their sharpest drop in over a year, the interim US-Iran memorandum on the Strait of Hormuz quietly expired without renewal, and a fresh incident at Hormuz pushed Brent crude above $91 for the first time this briefing has recorded — enough to pull the S&P back to 7,703 by Tuesday. Underneath both moves, Applied Materials and Coherent posted clean, AI-demand-driven beats, and the Fed's own rate-path signals started disagreeing with themselves: CME's FedWatch tool cut September hike odds in half on the CPI print, while Polymarket's own contract on the same meeting has actually drifted the other way.

Macro · CPIWed 12 Aug

July CPI lands exactly at consensus, and the Fed's September hike odds get cut nearly in half overnight

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported July headline CPI up 0.1% month-on-month and 3.4% year-on-year — down from June's 3.5% — with core CPI (ex food and energy) up 0.2% m/m and 2.5% y/y, down from June's 2.6%. Every figure matched the Dow Jones consensus forecast exactly. Shelter costs drove roughly two-thirds of the monthly increase; energy fell 1.5% for the month even though it remains 14.7% higher than a year ago. Treasury yields fell across the board on the release, and per CNBC, CME Group's FedWatch tool cut the implied probability of a September rate hike to 42%, from roughly 65% beforehand.

Context & background: last edition flagged this print as the direct test of the case three dissenting Fed regional presidents (Hammack, Kashkari, Logan) made at the 29 July FOMC meeting, where they voted for a 25-basis-point hike against a 9-3 majority to hold. Pre-CPI, their public dissent had pushed September hike odds as high as 57-65% on some trackers — an unusually hawkish position for a Fed that had just held rates for a fifth straight meeting.

The details: a print that matches consensus exactly, on both the headline and core readings, is about as clean a "no surprise" data point as this briefing has recorded — which is precisely why the market's reaction (record equities, falling yields, halved hike odds) was as large as it was. The gap between energy's -1.5% monthly move and its +14.7% annual level is a reminder that oil's swings (below) are still working their way through CPI arithmetic with a lag, even in a month oil fell.

Why it still matters: Polymarket's own "Fed decision in September" contract has not followed CME FedWatch's post-CPI move — its hike-odds price actually rose week-over-week (odds board, above), from 38% to 45%. That divergence between two venues pricing the same binary event, right after the same data release, is unusual and worth tracking into September; either one market is slower to update or traders are weighting the CPI print differently from how CME's model does.

Macro · PPI & retail salesThu 13 – Fri 14 Aug

Producer prices stay flat for the month and retail sales post their sharpest drop in over a year — both undershooting forecasts

July's Producer Price Index for final demand was unchanged month-on-month, below the 0.2% rise economists expected, and up 4.7% year-on-year versus a 4.9% forecast — energy prices fell 3.1% and food fell 0.9%, per the Bureau of Labor Statistics. The next day, Census Bureau data showed July retail and food-services sales of $763.6 billion, down 0.6% from June and the sharpest monthly drop since May 2025, sharply missing the roughly +0.1% consensus; motor-vehicle sales fell 1.8% and nonstore (largely online) retailers fell 2.2%, while clothing and health/personal-care stores both posted gains.

Context & background: these two prints landed one day apart and tell a slightly different story from Wednesday's CPI: where CPI showed prices behaving exactly as expected, both PPI and retail sales undershot their forecasts — PPI on the disinflation side (good news for the Fed's case to hold or cut), retail sales on the demand side (a genuine growth-scare data point, not just a pricing one).

The details: the retail-sales miss is broad-based rather than concentrated in one volatile category — motor vehicles and nonstore/online retail led the decline, while services-adjacent categories (food services, health/personal care) still grew, a split consistent with consumers pulling back on discretionary big-ticket and online purchases specifically rather than spending broadly less. The three-month trailing figure (May-July, +6.3% y/y) is still solidly positive, which is the main reason this single month's -0.6% didn't trigger a larger equity reaction the same day.

Why it still matters: a soft PPI plus a weak retail print is exactly the combination that would typically strengthen the case for the Fed's doves over its three hawkish dissenters — yet Polymarket's hike-odds contract moved the opposite direction this week (odds board, above). That's the second data point this edition has flagging a gap between what the incoming data would seem to justify and where at least one prediction market is actually pricing the September meeting.

Iran · Hormuzthrough Wed 19 Aug

Oil pushes through $91 for the first time this briefing has recorded, as the interim Hormuz deal expires and a fresh incident hits a tanker

Brent crude, which had already jumped to $89.60 early Wednesday 12 August on dueling US-Iran claims over control of the Strait, kept climbing through the week to trade above $91 by Wednesday 19 August — a level oilprice.com's live pricing attributes to an "unidentified projectile" strike on a vessel transiting Hormuz. The June memorandum of understanding that had briefly reopened Hormuz to commercial shipping formally expired Monday 17 August without renewal; President Trump said he was not interested in extending it, while separately claiming the US has "total control" of the strait — a claim Iran directly disputes, and one ship-tracking data does not clearly support, per oilprice.com's reporting that Hormuz traffic sank to just six vessels on one recent Monday.

Context & background: last edition's Hormuz card ended on Iran's hardened precondition — lift the blockade before any reopening — and a newly disclosed June memorandum that had already collapsed once over shipping-route disputes. This window supplies the next chapter: that same memorandum's formal expiry, unrenewed, on 17 August, plus a second round of physical escalation (the projectile strike) rather than just rhetorical hardening.

BitcoinWed 12 – Fri 14 Aug

Bitcoin barely moves on a third straight tame CPI print, a sign its inflation-day correlation trade has cooled

Bitcoin opened Wednesday 12 August at $63,547.05, roughly flat to slightly down from Tuesday, rose about 1% toward $64,200 ahead of the CPI release, then dipped from about $64,400 to $64,080 in a brief knee-jerk reaction before stabilizing — a less-than-1% net move on the day, per CoinDesk and Yahoo Finance. Per cryptonews.net's analysis, this was the third consecutive month a major US inflation print produced under 1% Bitcoin movement, and Deribit's CPI-day options premium has fallen from roughly 25% above baseline in early 2025 to under 5% in August 2026 — traders are no longer pricing much CPI-driven volatility into Bitcoin at all. A secondary tracker (SpinDepth) puts Bitcoin easing further, to around $62,700, by Friday 14 August; this briefing did not confirm a fresher print beyond that date.

lightbulbEditor's view

Inflation data stopped being the market's problem this week; oil and its own follow-through became the problem instead

1. Tame data, thin follow-through. CPI and PPI both landed soft or exactly at consensus, and the S&P still round-tripped from a fresh record on Friday to a 0.5% loss by Tuesday. The data cooperated; the market's conviction still didn't hold for a full week.

2. Retail sales' sharp miss barely dented the tape mid-week. A -0.6% print, the worst since May 2025, landed the same morning the S&P hit a fresh intraday high. For now, this market is trading off inflation prints, not growth prints.

3. Oil re-escalated on its own timeline, independent of the inflation data. The June Hormuz memorandum's quiet expiry on 17 August and a fresh reported strike on a transiting vessel pushed Brent through $91 — undoing, within a week, the dovish read this same briefing gave Wednesday's tame CPI.

4. AI-capex-linked earnings keep beating, cleanly. Applied Materials and Coherent both blew past estimates on AI-infrastructure demand — equipment orders and optical connectivity respectively — a second and third confirmation, after Intel's stock sale last edition, that the AI buildout remains this market's steadiest growth story even as the broader macro picture gets choppier.

5. The Fed's own signals disagree with each other. CME's FedWatch tool cut September hike odds to 42% right after CPI, while Polymarket's "Fed decision" contract moved the opposite direction week-over-week (38% → 45% on a hike). Two venues, same event, same data, diverging reads — worth resolving one way or the other before 16 September.

6. Bitcoin's inflation-day correlation keeps fading. A third straight CPI print produced under 1% Bitcoin movement, and Deribit's own options pricing confirms traders no longer expect crypto to swing hard on US inflation data — a genuine structural change from where this briefing found it a year of coverage ago.

The 16 September FOMC decision is now the single date this briefing is building toward from every direction at once — CME and Polymarket already disagree about which way it leans, Hormuz shows no sign of resolving before then, and two straight editions have now shown how quickly a quiet macro week can be overturned by an oil headline.
