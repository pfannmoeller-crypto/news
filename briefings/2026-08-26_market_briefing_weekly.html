Last edition closed with the S&P handing back a full week of gains to a fresh Hormuz-driven oil spike, and the 16 September FOMC meeting flagged as the market's next resolution date. Chips & memoryMon 24 – Tue 25 Aug

Memory and storage names lead a broad tech pullback into Nvidia's earnings and the PCE print

The S&P 500 opened the week lower on Monday 24 August, with CNBC's live markets coverage attributing the drag specifically to "a sell-off in chip stocks." By Tuesday 25 August the index closed at 7,652.86, down 0.3%, while the Nasdaq fell a sharper 0.8% to 25,980.19 as technology posted the worst sector performance of the day, down 1.8%. The damage was concentrated in memory and storage names rather than the AI-compute leaders: Micron fell 5.8%, SanDisk 6.5%, Seagate 6.5% and Broadcom 2.6%, while investors rotated into Consumer Staples (+1.7%) and Financials (+1.3%). The Dow, less exposed to the group, still closed up 0.3% at 53,417.16, extending a three-session win streak that Treasury-yield relief (below) had helped support.

Kevin Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote as Fed Chair becomes this edition's central catalyst

Kevin Warsh, confirmed as Fed Chair on 22 May 2026 by the narrowest Senate margin (54-45) in the role's history, delivers his first Jackson Hole keynote as chair on Friday 28 August at roughly 10am ET — one day after this window closes but squarely the event every other story in this edition is bracketing. Warsh, a known inflation hawk, has already broken with recent practice by withholding the Fed's rate-path "dot plot" projections, the first such withholding since the dot plot's 2012 introduction. Per Bank of America fund-manager survey data, 69% expect a neutral tone from the speech; per prediction-market pricing (odds board, above), traders have moved hard toward a September hold since this briefing last checked, even as the July FOMC's fractured 9-3 vote — three hawkish dissenters citing persistent inflation — argued the opposite direction only a month ago.

Treasury · bond marketWed 19 – Fri 21 Aug

Bessent's surprise bond buyback cools 19-year-high yields for 48 hours, then the relief trade unwinds

Treasury Secretary Bessent announced an upscaled, doubled buyback operation for longer-dated government debt on Wednesday 19 August, sending yields lower that day per CNBC's coverage — a move Bloomberg's reporting on the same story characterized as, at best, a "circuit breaker" for a broader global bond slump rather than a fix. It worked only briefly: by Thursday 20 August yields were already edging back higher as traders digested the plan, and by Friday 21 August CNBC reported the "rally fizzles out," with longer-dated yields rising back to erase the Wednesday decline entirely. The 30-year Treasury yield remained near 19-year highs around 5.27% through the episode, per Tech Times' Jackson Hole preview.

Brent crude, which closed last edition at a record $91.84 on a fresh reported strike near the Strait of Hormuz, had fallen to $88.58 by this edition's compile — down 3.9% — while WTI eased to $81.01, down 1.6%. Oilprice.com's live coverage attributes the reversal directly to renewed "Iran negotiation hopes," a sharp turn from last window's escalation narrative. The physical picture underneath the price move is mixed rather than clearly resolving: satellite imagery reportedly showed seven tankers loading Iraqi crude simultaneously even as India's crude import bill climbed on elevated Hormuz shipping rates, and the US Strategic Petroleum Reserve was separately reported sinking toward its operational minimum.

6. Two binary events now sit on top of each other at this window's close. Nvidia's Q2 report and Warsh's Jackson Hole keynote land within 18 hours of each other, one testing AI-capex demand and the other testing the new Fed chair's reaction function — genuinely different questions that the market has, for now, priced as if only one of them matters.

Nothing this edition can score as resolved actually resolved this window — the two events it is built around both land after this edition's own publish time. That is itself the story: markets spent 20-26 August positioning for a Thursday-Friday pair of catalysts rather than reacting to one that had already happened, and next edition should open by scoring both.
