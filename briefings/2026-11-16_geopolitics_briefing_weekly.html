UkraineTOP13–15 Nov
The US now offers Ukraine a NATO path if it leaves Donbas, Zelensky says; Kyiv says no decision yet
Zelensky said US envoys asked whether Ukraine would withdraw from Donbas, the eastern industrial region, if it joined NATO (the Western military alliance). He said nothing is concrete and that he would put any deal to Ukrainians.
●●●○○ 3/5
The details

On 13 November Zelensky told journalists the Americans raised the question for the first time at the Abu Dhabi talks: “What if Ukraine joins NATO? Would Ukraine withdraw from Donbas?” He described a wider package: guarantees from NATO and the EU (European Union), a strong army with funding, and a reconstruction plan. He had asked for guarantees like Article 5 (the alliance’s mutual-defence clause) as a minimum; Washington said something like Articles 4 and 5, with no US troops on Ukrainian soil. Any deal would come “without any recognition of the occupied territories as Russian”. This is Zelensky’s account of private talks; Washington has not published the offer. Jared Kushner, a US envoy, said on 14 November that “several new ideas” are being explored.

The other reading

Ian Lesser of the German Marshall Fund (a US think tank) told Al Jazeera the NATO element looks “very doubtful” because it is hard to imagine Moscow agreeing, and some NATO members may object.

What to watch

Whether the White House confirms the offer, and whether Moscow answers it. The Kremlin has long said NATO membership for Ukraine would bring “a harsh response”.

US envoys saw Putin in Moscow; the next step is Russia–Europe contact and a third round, with no date
Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, President Trump’s envoys, met Putin on 12 November, then briefed Zelensky in Dublin. Kushner says Washington now wants direct Russia–Europe contact and is preparing the agenda for a next round of three-way talks.
●●●●○ 4/5
The details

After the Abu Dhabi talks of 10–11 November, the two US envoys met Putin in Moscow on Thursday 12 November and Zelensky in Dublin, ahead of a meeting of European leaders there on 13 November. Kushner wrote on X on 14 November that the focus is “facilitating direct engagement between Moscow and European partners and preparing the agenda for the next round of trilateral talks”, and that NATO remains a “critical pillar” of European security. No Kremlin account of the Putin meeting was in the sources we read, and no date for the next round was given. Our last edition listed the envoys’ Moscow visit as an open item; the visit happened and produced the two proposals described in the neighbouring stories.

What to watch

A date and venue for the third round; any Russia–Europe meeting; the Kremlin’s own account of the 12 November talks.

Ukraine12–13 Nov
Putin proposes a “humanitarian mission” in Kostiantynivka; Zelensky calls it a ruse to take the city
Zelensky says Putin gave the US envoys a plan for a temporary humanitarian mission in the embattled city of Kostiantynivka. He argues a pause would let Russian troops move into better positions, and that Kramatorsk and Sloviansk would be next.
●●●○○ 3/5
The details

Zelensky said on 13 November the proposal reached him through the US negotiators, who told him “we understand”. He called it “an absolutely primitive move, because otherwise he has no way of taking this city”, and said Ukrainian civilians remain in Kostiantynivka while fighting goes on inside it. Ukraine passed the US side a list of seven or eight blockaded settlements, such as villages near Oleshky, where civilians including children remain; Russia proposed nothing for them. Presidential adviser Dmytro Lytvyn pointed to an earlier Russian-proposed humanitarian operation in Kherson Oblast which he said was used to rotate troops under cover of a pause. Ukraine denies Russia controls the city.

What to watch

Whether the US pushes the idea into the next round, and whether Russia names any other settlement for a humanitarian pause.

Russia & US13–19 Nov
Trump, Xi and Putin meet in Shenzhen on 19 November, but the White House says Ukraine is not on the agenda
The three leaders will hold a first-ever three-way meeting at the APEC summit (the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation forum). A US official says the topic is nuclear arms and Iran; the Kremlin says Ukraine will still come up in the Trump–Putin talks.
●●●○○ 3/5
The details

The White House confirmed the meeting on 13 November and China’s Foreign Ministry confirmed it on 16 November, for 19 November in Shenzhen. A senior US official told the Associated Press the main subject is nuclear non-proliferation, with Iran also planned and Ukraine not on the agenda; no new agreements are expected. The last US–Russia nuclear arms treaty expired in February 2026. Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said on 12 November that the settlement of the war will be discussed in the Trump–Putin meeting. Ukraine’s foreign minister Andrii Sybiha said the summit would only “encourage Putin to continue this war”. Kyiv and the Europeans will not be in the room.

What to watch

18–19 November: the APEC summit; any Trump–Putin side meeting and whatever each side says Ukraine was about.

Ukraine14–15 Nov
Russia and Ukraine keep hitting each other’s energy and industry while the talks drag on
Russia fired 132 long-range drones overnight on 15 November, 78 of them jet-powered; Ukraine says strikes killed 4 and injured 69 over a day. Ukraine hit a Tatarstan refinery complex, an airfield in Astrakhan and an oil site in Samara.
●●●○○ 3/5
The details

Ukraine’s Air Force counted 132 attack drones overnight into 15 November, including 78 jet-powered ones, with a “mass attack” on Odesa Oblast; officials reported 4 dead and 69 injured across Ukraine in 24 hours. Ukraine’s General Staff said it struck petrochemical and refining plants in Nizhnekamsk, Tatarstan, plus a helicopter maker, and the Akhtubinsk airfield in Astrakhan, a test centre for new aircraft and weapons. A fire was also reported at an oil station near Prosvet in Samara Oblast, about 950 kilometres from Ukrainian-held ground. Zelensky said Russian drones get through to power substations partly because speed limits on SpaceX’s Starshield (military satellite internet) remain in force on the front line; Elon Musk has suggested the US military is behind the limits. All figures are the parties’ own.

What to watch

Winter energy damage in Ukraine, and any Russian response to the refinery strikes.

Kyiv Independent · Odesa attack Kyiv Independent · Nizhnekamsk

Iran & the Gulf 1 story

IranTOP13–16 Nov
A third US aircraft carrier reaches the Gulf as Iran fires at a US radar in Bahrain and mines hit tankers
The IRGC (Iran’s Revolutionary Guards) says it sent six drones at a US radar in Bahrain on 15 November, the second claim in three days. Tankers are hitting sea mines in the Strait of Hormuz, and the carrier Theodore Roosevelt has arrived.
●●●○○ 3/5
The details

The IRGC claimed on 13 November to have destroyed a US early-warning radar in Bahrain; CENTCOM (the US military’s regional command) denied it and said it shot down two drones. On 15 November the IRGC said it launched six drones at the radar at Riffa Air Base, and Bahrain sounded sirens; the US had not commented in the text we read. Two tankers struck mines on 14 November, and the IRGC says they used an “unauthorised” route with tracking switched off. Parliament speaker and chief negotiator Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf says Iran sent seven conditions through Qatar, including lifting the US blockade and sanctions, and awaits a US answer. On 16 November Al Jazeera reported the Theodore Roosevelt joining two others in the region.

The other reading

CENTCOM said no damage or injuries followed the 13 November incident, and has not confirmed the 15 November claim.

What to watch

A US reply to the seven conditions; any US or Israeli strike, which Tehran says it would answer at once.

Sources

Al Jazeera · Bahrain radar Al Jazeera · live blog

Why 3/5: one outlet (citing Reuters, CENTCOM and Iranian agencies); the claims of both sides are unverified; search for other outlets returned only older items

Israel11–13 Nov
Israel’s caretaker government can legally order a strike on Iran, but its own military sees no urgent need
Israeli media said the security cabinet ordered the army to step up preparations for an attack on Iran. The US command in the region called such reports “mere speculation”, and Israel’s army reportedly told Netanyahu there is no urgent need.
●●●○○ 3/5
The details

KAN (Israel’s public broadcaster) reported on 11 November that the cabinet’s directives “could lead it to carry out activities that could trigger a war”. A US military official was quoted by KAN saying Washington had told Israel to be ready for a new round; CENTCOM dismissed that as “mere speculation”. Channel 13 reported that the military assessment, given to both Netanyahu and Gadi Eisenkot (the opposition leader asked to form a government) on 11 November, is that there is no urgent need. Legally, experts told Al Jazeera that a caretaker government (one that stays in office until a new one forms) keeps war powers; a court-made “duty of restraint” on irreversible moves has a security exception. The opposition demands no strike unless critical. Netanyahu said on 14 November a unity government could “eliminate the Iranian threat once and for all”.

What to watch

Whether any strike precedes a new Israeli government, due by 6 December at the earliest deadline.

Al Jazeera · caretaker powers Al Jazeera · unity talks

Israel13–16 Nov
Eisenkot trails the 61 seats he needs and Netanyahu offers unity, as a police-raid affair erupts
Eisenkot’s bloc has 48 of 120 seats, Netanyahu’s 53, and Arab-led parties 19. Netanyahu’s unity offer was called a “bluff”. A report says his office sent police against Arab-party members on election eve.
●●●●○ 4/5
The details

Israel’s President Isaac Herzog gave Eisenkot until 6 December, extendable by 14 days to 20 December. His bloc passed a Knesset (parliament) committee vote 63–50 with both Arab-led factions, and the three together hold 67, but opposition leaders Avigdor Lieberman and Naftali Bennett reject relying on them. The likeliest route, experts say, is a deal with the ultra-Orthodox parties Shas and United Torah Judaism, one faction of which seeks a law exempting seminary students from military service. On 15 November Channel 12 reported that Netanyahu’s chief of staff steered about 14 police officers to raid three Ra’am (an Arab party) members’ homes without a warrant on 26 October; all three were released. Eisenkot demanded an investigation; Likud (Netanyahu’s party) called it “outrageous incitement”. Eisenkot confirmed his party is talking with Likud about a shared government.

The other reading

Likud calls Eisenkot’s response incitement; a former Israeli negotiator, Daniel Levy, says taking meetings is mostly spin and leverage-seeking.

What to watch

6 December: Eisenkot’s first deadline; the Haredi (ultra-Orthodox) exemption law.

Sanctions 2 stories

Sanctions13–15 Nov
EU naval forces board a suspected Russian shadow-fleet tanker in the Mediterranean
The EU’s Operation Irini boarded the oil tanker Midag on 13 November and confirmed it was sailing under a false flag. EU foreign-policy chief Kaja Kallas said the bloc will keep disrupting the “shadow fleet” at sea.
●●●○○ 3/5
The details

Kallas said on 15 November that Irini, the EU’s Mediterranean mission that monitors arms and oil trafficking, boarded the crude tanker under Article 110 of the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea, which allows inspection of ships under certain conditions. The shadow fleet is the network of ageing tankers Moscow uses to sell oil around sanctions. The EU boarded another suspected tanker in August. The latest full EU sanctions package we found, the 21st, was adopted on 21 July and added 51 entities tied to Russia’s military industry and suspended the oil price-cap adjustment for a year; we found no new package in the window. The boarding is an enforcement step, not a new law.

What to watch

A 22nd package; what happens to the tanker and its cargo.

Sources

Kyiv Independent · tanker boarding Euronews · US waiver (context)

Why 3/5: single outlet relaying Kallas’s own post; the 21st package detail is from a search summary

Sanctions9 Oct–14 Nov
The US eased sanctions around Russian diesel and Lukoil, and Kyiv counts it as pressure on Ukraine
Trump agreed a Russian diesel supply deal, issued a licence for it and waived new sanctions; the US also extended a Lukoil waiver to 25 April 2027. Reuters says US buyers are unlikely to take the diesel.
●●○○○ 2/5
The details

A deal announced in early October set Russian diesel shipments to US and global markets at 300,000 tonnes at once, 500,000 in November and 1 million after that; US Treasury General License 135 (a formal permission) of 9 October lifted US sanctions on the diesel. A week later Trump signed an order setting but at once waiving sweeping new tariffs and sanctions under the Graham sanctions law. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent says “no sanctions were lifted” and cites a 60-day review. The Kyiv Independent on 13 November described the diesel deal, the waivers and pressure on Kyiv to stop refinery strikes as part of a “pivot toward Russia”. This is from search summaries and a news-site summary, not the legal texts.

The other reading

Bessent argues the waiver gave Russia no real relief; Democrats and some Republicans call it a surrender.

What to watch

The 60-day review; any bill in Congress to block the waiver.

Sources

Kyiv Independent · context paragraph Euronews · Lukoil waiver

Why 2/5: headlines and summaries only; the Reuters 11 November item was read through a search summary

Europe’s hidden war 2 stories

Hidden warTOP14–15 Nov
Estonia will close its Russian border crossings after a Russian-ordered arson attempt on a drone maker
Eight people were detained after an attempted arson at Threod Systems, which Estonia says Russian military intelligence ordered. Foreign minister Margus Tsahkna said the crossings at Narva, Luhamaa and Koidula will close from 1 December.
●●●●○ 4/5
The details

The attempted attack came in the early hours of 14 November on Threod Systems, a company making reconnaissance and attack drones. Estonia says the GRU (Russia’s military intelligence) ordered it. Eight arrests followed; Latvia’s interior minister said several “Latvian citizens and non-citizens” were among those held. Tsahkna said closing the crossings will cut Russia’s ability to run hostile operations in Estonia and help stop sanctions evasion; he gave no length of closure. Estonia has accused Russia of an earlier arson attack on a Ukraine-supplying defence firm, in an August case announced on 29 September. Moscow had not commented in the text we read. An Estonian lawmaker, Marko Mihkelson, called closing the border an “asymmetric” answer to a “shadow war”.

What to watch

How long the closure lasts; whether Latvia or Finland follow; a Russian reply.

Sources

Al Jazeera / Reuters · border closure Kyiv Independent · Threod Systems

Why 4/5: two outlets agree; the GRU attribution is Estonia’s own and Russia has not answered

Hidden war13–14 Nov
Three merchant ships were hit by drones off Romania’s coast in two days; nobody has said whose
A Ukraine-bound cargo ship was hit on 13 November and two more vessels caught fire on 14 November near Sulina. Romania opened an investigation and sent F-16 jets up, but has not named who launched the drones.
●●●○○ 3/5
The details

Romanian officials said the first ship was about 12 kilometres off the coast near Sfântu Gheorghe, heading for Sulina; a drone damaged its cargo crane and no one was hurt. On the evening of 14 November two more vessels were struck, about four nautical miles from Sulina, and both caught fire, which the crews put out. Defence minister Ștefan-Radu Oprea confirmed an investigation on 14 November; two F-16s were scrambled after an aerial target was detected east of Snake Island, and drone alerts were issued in Tulcea County. Earlier ship strikes: 28 October in Bulgarian waters and near Constanța. Romania is a NATO member and borders Ukraine on the Danube. Attribution is not established.

What to watch

Romania’s findings; whether NATO consults on Article 4 (the alliance’s consultation clause).

Editor’s view

  1. Washington is moving the territorial argument, not just the talks. The NATO-for-Donbas idea and the Kostiantynivka proposal both reached Zelensky through US envoys, and neither has a public US text. Until one does, treat both as Kyiv’s version.
  2. The Shenzhen summit sets the next window. With Ukraine kept off the agenda and Europe absent, any Russia–US understanding on 19 November would arrive before Kyiv and the Europeans are at the table. Our Markets briefing covers oil and Hormuz prices.
  3. Iran is drifting toward a trigger. Two radar claims in three days, mines, a third carrier and Israeli strike reports fit a stalemate that is getting less stable, while both Washington and Israeli generals publicly play down an attack.
  4. Europe’s answers to sabotage are getting physical. Closing a border and boarding tankers are state actions, not police work, and both came within three days.
