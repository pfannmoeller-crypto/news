Briefing summary — seventh edition

Packaging costs ease as PET resin drops again while European gas pushes past €62/MWh

This cycle's clearest story is packaging and energy moving in opposite directions again, for calmer reasons than the aluminum-versus-pork split the 11 August edition carried: North American PET bottle resin is falling for a second summer month as feedstock costs ease, even as European gas pushes past €62/MWh and closes in on double its year-ago level. Protein inputs bring one genuinely new deal — Ever After Foods' acquisition of Belgium's Fishway, the first cultivated-protein consolidation this briefing has tracked with premium pet food named as an initial commercial target — plus fresher, if still not current, data on two previously flagged gaps: Peru's fishmeal crisis, still setting records into June with no anchovy-season reopening date, and rabbit meat, priced in public data for the first time in this briefing's run. Industry side carries one dated, named transaction: VAFO Group's acquisition of German direct-to-consumer brand Pets Deli. Two threads from the 11 August edition carry no fresh public update this cycle and are deliberately not repeated: African swine fever's grip on EU pork, and the record US aluminum premium.

CULTIVATED PROTEIN / M&A18 Aug 2026

Ever After Foods buys Belgium's Fishway to build a cultivated-protein platform for premium pet food

Israeli cultivated-meat manufacturer Ever After Foods (EAF) acquired Fishway, a Belgium-based aquatic cell-line and animal-component-free media developer, adding its scientific team to EAF's industrial protein platform. Announced 18 August alongside an additional $2 million in strategic investment, the deal names cultivated functional protein ingredients for premium pet food as one of the platform's initial commercial programs — the first cultivated-protein consolidation this briefing has tracked with pet food named explicitly as a target market.

Context & background. EAF and equipment maker Bühler announced a cultivated-meat scale-up collaboration in February 2025; this briefing's sister trade press has separately noted that cultivated protein "has faced an assortment of challenges over the last few years," making a platform-consolidation deal — buying capability rather than promising a breakthrough — a notably different signal than the funding-round announcements this sector has typically produced.

The details. EAF's platform combines bioprocess engineering with its own edible-scaffold technology (PlantriX) and an "Edible Packed-Bed" production system; Fishway brings aquatic cell lines and animal-component-free media expertise developed in Belgium, which the companies say shortens development cycles from biological innovation through to industrial-scale production. Fishway's team joins EAF, Belgium remains a development site, and Fishway's own shareholders continue to support the combined platform — terms of the core transaction were not disclosed, only the accompanying $2 million raise.

Why it still matters. This is a platform-capability acquisition, not a product launch — there is no named pet food customer or commercial timeline yet. Worth watching for whether EAF names a first pet food partner or discloses pricing for its cultivated protein ingredient, which would be the first concrete cost comparison against the rendered and insect-protein alternatives already tracked in this briefing.

Super-prime Peruvian fishmeal traded above $2,700 per metric tonne CFR China in April–May 2026, and Chinese fishmeal prices logged a fifth consecutive weekly record by week 23 (early June), as Peru's "mega ban" — a 15-day fishing suspension imposed 27 May and since extended — kept anchovy boats tied up with no reopening date announced. RABBITJun 2026 · first coverage, gap closed

Rabbit meat gets its first public price print in this briefing: $8.04/kg US retail, with disease risk on the supply side

US rabbit meat is priced at $8.04/kg ($3.65/lb) retail and $5.43/kg wholesale as of June 2026, per aggregator Selina Wamucii's UN Comtrade-derived tracking — the first public rabbit-protein pricing data this briefing has located, closing the gap explicitly flagged in the 11 August edition. US export unit values rose 113% month-on-month, though from a small base; Spain, Hungary and France lead global rabbit-meat exports. Recurring outbreaks of Rabbit Haemorrhagic Disease (RHD), a highly fatal viral disease with no reliable treatment, are named as the main recurring supply risk.

LEADRESIN10–19 Aug 2026

PET resin prices post an unusual second summer drop as feedstock costs ease

North American bottle-grade PET (polyethylene terephthalate) resin is trending flat to lower through August 2026 — an unusual pattern for the peak summer beverage season — as falling paraxylene, PTA (purified terephthalic acid) and MEG (monoethylene glycol) feedstock costs outweigh seasonal demand. No August price-increase initiative had been announced as of 10 August. This reverses the roughly 32% resin cost rise the 11 August edition reported behind American Packaging Corp's Iowa pouch-capacity expansion.

Context & background. The 11 August edition reported PE/PET resin costs up roughly 32% across three hikes in March 2026 — the input-cost headwind American Packaging Corp's $30 million Story City, Iowa expansion was betting demand growth could outrun. Five months later, the same input category is retreating, at least in North America.

The details. The mechanism is feedstock, not demand: June's North American paraxylene contract settled at $0.73/lb, five cents below May, with PTA following lower and spot MEG down roughly two cents/lb. Buyers are covering only near-term requirements rather than rebuilding inventory, which reinforces the soft market — sellers compete for open orders while buyers wait for clearer signals. Recycled-PET bale prices in the US South and Southeast eased in parallel, from 7–8¢/lb in July to 6–7¢/lb in August, though Midwest and California bale prices remain structurally lower on logistics and processing-capacity differences.

Why it still matters. The reporting explicitly cautions that the absence of an August increase does not guarantee stable September pricing, and the picture is regional: China, the US, Germany, India and Brazil all logged higher second-quarter PET values before this North American summer softening, so this reads as a North America-specific correction rather than a global resin reprieve. Worth checking whether any pet food packaging buyer discloses resin-driven cost relief, mirroring the cost-pass-through question this briefing has already raised for aluminum.

GAS / TTF17–18 Aug 2026

European gas pushes past €62/MWh, now up nearly 99% year-on-year, as Gulf LNG disruption persists

TTF (Title Transfer Facility, Europe's Dutch benchmark gas price) settled near €62.50/MWh on 17–18 August 2026 — a fresh three-week high, up from the €58.44/MWh the 11 August edition reported a week earlier. Continued disruption to Gulf LNG (liquefied natural gas) supply amid stalled US-Iran negotiations is compounding with below-average European storage levels and heatwave-driven power demand across Southern and Central Europe, which is diverting gas from storage injection into generation at exactly the time of year storage should be filling fastest.

Context & background. The 11 August edition already carried Uniper's €50–60/MWh forecast band and Commerzbank's €50/MWh year-end call, both explicitly conditioned on Strait of Hormuz shipping risk persisting. This print sits at the top of that band, not yet outside the range either bank sketched out.

The details. What is new this cycle is the demand side: heatwaves across Southern and Central Europe are pulling gas into power generation right when storage should be filling fastest ahead of winter, adding a second pressure source alongside the ongoing Gulf LNG disruption. The two forces compound rather than substitute for each other — a resolution on either front alone (a Hormuz de-escalation or cooler autumn weather) would only partially unwind the current premium.

Why it still matters. Gas is not a pet food raw material, but it is a direct cost input for extrusion, drying and rendering — the same logic that puts aluminum and resin pricing in the Packaging section. European storage-refill data through autumn, named as the swing factor in the prior edition, remains the indicator to watch; a further escalation in Gulf shipping risk is the more consequential near-term catalyst.

Analysis

Three reads from a cycle that closed old gaps more than it opened new ones

  1. Packaging and energy inputs are diverging again, but this time both sides are calmer. A record aluminum premium against a swine-fever price collapse was last edition's sharp opposite-direction story; this cycle's version — PET resin easing on soft feedstock costs while gas grinds toward a fresh high on Gulf disruption and heatwave demand — is a gentler version of the same pattern, worth watching as a recurring structural feature of this beat rather than a one-off.
  2. Two of this cycle's three protein cards are explicitly about closing gaps this briefing itself created by flagging them. Fishmeal's staleness and rabbit's total absence were both named as open questions in the 11 August edition; neither is now current-to-the-week, but both are closer to it, which is the honest middle ground between padding with old material and repeating "no news."
  3. The Ever After Foods/Fishway deal and the VAFO/Pets Deli deal are structurally different kinds of consolidation worth telling apart. One is capability-buying in an unproven category (cultivated protein) with no named pet food customer yet; the other is scale-buying in a proven one (D2C pet food retail) with disclosed revenue on both sides. Both are real, publicly announced, and both are the kind of concrete, dated transaction the 11 August edition's trend-level M&A read was waiting on.
