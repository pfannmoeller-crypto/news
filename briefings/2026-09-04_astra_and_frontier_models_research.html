Astra, and the frontier around it

OpenAI's Astra launched publicly on September 3, 2026, as the first model any lab has assigned its own "Critical" cyber-capability tier. It landed inside a frontier that had not stood still: Anthropic's Fable 5.1 shipped two days earlier and sits atop the one leaderboard every source here cites (a single aggregator snapshot, C 3/5); Google, xAI, Meta, Microsoft and a cluster of Chinese open-weight labs are each staking a claim to some part of "best." This is a snapshot of what each lab says about itself, what the handful of independent evaluators who checked in time found, and where the two accounts do not agree — as the record stood on September 4, 2026.

The picture in brief

Each lab's own claim about itself, at full strength, before any checking — no verdicts here, those come later.

  • OpenAI: Astra, launched September 3, is the first model OpenAI has designated at its own "Critical" cyber-capability tier — and, in Greg Brockman's words, "our most intelligent and, also very importantly, our most aligned model yet." (TechCrunch, C4)
  • Anthropic: Fable 5.1, launched two days earlier on September 1 alongside the smaller Mythos 5.1, sits at #1 on the Artificial Analysis index snapshot cited throughout this piece.
  • Google: Gemini 3.5 Pro carries a 2-million-token context window and a four-level "Deep Think" effort setting; a 3.8 Pro/Ultra tier is discussed but UNVERIFIED here.
  • xAI: Grok 4.6 is positioned as the cost-efficiency frontier of the closed tier, at $2/$6 per million tokens under 200K context — with Grok 4.7 targeted "within weeks" and Grok 5 before the end of 2026.
  • Meta: Muse Spark is a closed family — a press cluster describes a pivot away from open weights at the frontier — even as Llama 4 Scout and Maverick remain openly licensed one tier down.
  • The Chinese open labs: GLM-5.3, Kimi K3, Qwen3.8 and DeepSeek V4 each claim frontier-adjacent capability at roughly 10–50x lower list prices than the closed leaders, and currently hold the top five slots on the open side of the index cited here.
  • Nvidia: Nemotron 4 is pitched as the US open-weight answer to a closed, expensive frontier — a >1 trillion-parameter model backed by a $7 billion compute budget through fiscal 2028. It has not been released.

Astra, walked through

OpenAI's own claims, in the order the company made them. Checks and other views sit under the claim they check. Confidence scale carried over from the underlying reports: C5 primary source · C4 corroborated press · C3 single credible source or aggregator · C2 weak/secondary · C1 rumor-only.

The road to launch

A White House official told Axios that OpenAI "voluntarily informed the administration of their plans to delay" (Aug 7, C4–5). An RL training pause followed, roughly August 7–18, described in OpenAI's own post "Pacing model development in an era of cyber-critical capabilities" (C5) and corroborated by Axios (Aug 18, C4). On September 1, OpenAI's "Path to Astra" post announced the model's first Critical-tier cyber designation (C5); public launch followed September 3, covered by TechCrunch, CNBC, NBC and Fortune (C4). The name itself is contested: OpenAI and TechCrunch call it "Astra"; other outlets refer to it as "GPT-6 Astra."

Rollout and price

Access opened first to Daybreak defensive-cyber partners, then rolled to Pro, Plus, Enterprise, Business and API access over the following week (C4). Pricing is reported at $10/$50 per million tokens (Vellum and FourWeekMBA, both citing Artificial Analysis, C3).

What OpenAI says it can do

OpenAI's own claims: computer and browser automation, zero-day identification, software engineering, terminal use, and codebase analysis.

OpenAI's own benchmark numbers

All figures in this block are OpenAI's own (C5): 100% on ExploitBench; an internal "Port 20" red-team exercise that found high-severity V8 vulnerabilities including 2 zero-days; a Cyber Jailbreak Refusal rate of 91.5% against 59% for Sol (GPT-5.6); a Honeypot unauthorized-attempt rate of 0% against 56% for Sol; and 98.6–99.9% on ARC-AGI-3 through OpenAI's own harness. GPQA Diamond is reported at 96.0.

Greg Brockman: "Our most intelligent and, also very importantly, our most aligned model yet." (TechCrunch, C4)
OpenAI: "It is the first model we are designating at this level, and requires stronger safeguards than any prior release."

ARC Prize's own caution: even saturating the benchmark "would not represent proof of achieving AGI." For scale, Opus 5 scored 30.2% on the same public set per Vellum.

"Persistent-Astra"

Secondary commentary (Zvi and others) has used a "Persistent-Astra" label — describing some form of Astra continuing to run or reason across sessions in ways prior models reportedly did not. Separately, a July 19 security incident is reported to have involved "a different internal model from the Astra family, but not [the public] Astra itself."

The safety story

Jakub Pachocki (OpenAI): chain-of-thought monitoring is "fragile" — and, in a separate fragment as reported, "unfortunately trending in a negative direction." (the-decoder, C4; two reported fragments, contiguity not confirmed)

Astra is reported to use a form of recurrent depth, or "opaque recurrence," in its reasoning — part of what is driving the monitoring concern.

UK AISI: opaque reasoning could "severely undermine current oversight methods." (C4)

A joint statement from researchers at OpenAI, Anthropic and Google on latent reasoning was also reported (C4).

Dianne Penn (Anthropic): the approach is "deliberately more conservative." (Axios, C5)
Michael Dalton (OpenAI): the company is "consciously slowing down research to enhance security." (Axios, C5)

OpenAI's own framing: the largest planned RL run "remains paused pending smaller-scale assessments" (C5). "Critical" tier is defined, per OpenAI, as the ability to produce functional zero-day exploits against hardened systems without human intervention.

Governance: voluntary or pressured?

A White House official's "voluntarily informed" framing (Axios, Aug 7, C4) sits opposite a broader narrative, visible in some coverage, that reads the delay as government pressure; this piece did not find sourcing strong enough to settle which framing is more accurate. The administration is reported to be developing a pre-release evaluation framework, with details unclear. A separate claim that Altman gave a Capitol Hill demo could not be verified (C2). A Cursor pricing dispute in this period is a separate, all-models business disagreement, not specific to Astra (C3).

Other viewsThe headline framing split cleanly on the same numbers. Axios and Fortune ran with "may represent AGI"; FourWeekMBA's read of the same index snapshot called Astra "a flat-capability release at a premium price" (C3). A pre-launch narrative (Patel) describing Astra as "trained but withheld" was overtaken by the actual September 3 launch and should be read as historical, not current.

The safety story — a Critical-tier designation, a paused RL run, a chorus of named researchers worried about opaque reasoning — is the best-sourced part of Astra's launch. The capability story is the least resolved: OpenAI's own harness and ARC Prize's neutral one disagree by more than 35 points, and on the one index every source here cites, Astra ties the model it replaced.

The closed frontier

Per-lab claims and independently observed figures, with confidence scores carried over from the underlying reports.

OpenAI — GPT-5.6 (Sol / Terra / Luna)

Shipped July 9, 2026 (C5). Sol: 52.7 on Agents' Last Exam, 80 on the AA Coding Agent Index (self-reported), 90.4 on BrowseComp, 73.5 on ExploitBench, 88.8 on Terminal-Bench 2.1 (C4). Context roughly 256K–512K tokens; reasoning exposed as an effort setting (standard/max/ultra). Pricing: Sol $5/$30, Terra $2.50/$15, Luna $1/$6 per million tokens (C4). The prior GPT-5.5 sits at #15 on the index cited throughout, at 56.3 (C3).

Anthropic — Fable 5.1 / Mythos 5.1 / Opus 5

Fable 5.1 and the smaller Mythos 5.1 launched September 1, 2026, initially to vetted organizations under "Project Glasswing" (C5). Reported figures: TB-Science 0.1 at 52.6 (versus 24.7 for Fable 5, 29.0 for Opus 5); Terminal-Bench 4.0 at 55.8 (Mythos 5.1 scores higher, 60.9); CursorBench 3.2.0 at 73.4; HLE 60.9 without tools, 65.0 with tools (C4). Context 1M tokens in, 128K out. List price is unchanged from Fable 5 at $10/$50 per million tokens, cached reads discounted 75% to $0.25, batch pricing $5/$25 (C4). On the index snapshot cited throughout, Fable 5.1 sits at #1 with 65.7 (Sept 3, via benchlm.ai, C3).

Opus 5 sits at #2 with 63.0, and TB-Science 29.0; one source (netalith, C3) credits it with roughly 103 agentic steps before failure, versus roughly 53 for Grok 4.6 — Opus 5's own pricing was not found in these reports. The prior Fable 5 sits at #4 (62.1); Opus 4.8 at #13 (57.3).

Google — Gemini 3.5 Pro / 3.8 Flash

Gemini 3.5 Pro reached general availability around late June 2026 (C3): 2M-token context, 64K output, a four-level "Deep Think" effort setting. On the index, the smaller Gemini 3.8 Flash sits at #10 with 58.7 (C3). A Gemini 3.8 Pro/Ultra tier is discussed in coverage but its specs are UNVERIFIED here.

xAI — Grok 4.6

Launched August 12, 2026 (C3): a 1.5-trillion-parameter base model, priced $2/$6 per million tokens under a 200K-token context window and $4/$12 above it, cached input $0.50, cached output $1. It scores 60.9–61 on the index (C3), and lab-provided summaries credit it with leading CursorBench, FrontierCode and "AA-Briefcase," with roughly 53 agentic steps before failure. xAI is reported to be targeting Grok 4.7 "within weeks" of this snapshot and Grok 5 before the end of 2026 (C3).

Kimi K3 (Moonshot)

Announced July 16, weights released July 27. 2.8 trillion total parameters across 896 experts, 16 active per token (roughly 104B active parameters per Artificial Analysis) — reported as the largest open-weight model released to date. 1M context, roughly $2.30 blended price. How to read the leaderboards

The harness a model runs in, and the index a score comes from, do as much work as the model itself.

Harness versus model

Two cases make the same point from two different labs. First: Nvidia's own "AI Village Olympics" (AVO) benchmark, published August 21 (Nvidia Developer Blog, corroborated by Forbes and The New Stack, C4), found Opus 5 scoring roughly 30% on the ARC-AGI-3 public set alone at High reasoning effort (a figure attributed to ARC Prize, C3) — but reaching 100.00 relative-human-adjusted-effort (RHAE) across 25 environments and all 183 levels once wrapped in Nvidia's own agent harness. Nvidia's own framing: "A frontier language model is only one component of an AI agent... the harness determines whether that capability translates into sustained real-world performance." Second: Astra shows the identical pattern from OpenAI's side — 98.6–99.9% on ARC-AGI-3 through OpenAI's own provider adapter, 62.7% on ARC Prize's neutral harness (see Section 2). Read together, both say the same thing: the model is not the whole system, and self-graded harnesses inflate scores that neutral ones do not reproduce.

What the index actually is

The Artificial Analysis Index v4.1.1 cited throughout this piece composites nine evaluations — GDPval-AA v2, τ³-Banking, Terminal-Bench v2.1, SciCode, HLE, GPQA Diamond, CritPt, AA-Omniscience and AA-LCR — across 643 models (C5 for that composition claim). The live top-10 leaderboard itself was not extracted for this piece, since it renders client-side; every ranking cited here instead comes through a secondary aggregator's snapshot of it (C3).

What was not retrieved

LMArena standings; METR's time-horizon series (a 1.1 update from January 29, 2026 exists; a roughly seven-month capability-doubling time is widely repeated and a compression to roughly four months is rumored — both UNVERIFIED here); SWE-bench Verified/Pro leaderboards; HLE's top 10; and the top of the "ECI" index (its own page title claims "14 points per year since reasoning models," C3) — none of these were reached for this piece.

Token share versus revenue

Gavin Baker (Atreides Management) is widely cited for a claim that open-weight models carry 15–25% of inference volume against 60–90% of revenue value for closed models — but that exact line was not confirmed as his in these reports. What was found: a Baker post citing open-source share of tokens on the Vercel AI Gateway rising from 28% to 62% over roughly two months in mid-2026 (crediting @rauchg, C3), while OpenAI's and Anthropic's own revenue kept accelerating over the same period. Anthropic's annualized revenue reached $65 billion in July 2026 (CNBC and TechCrunch, Aug 17, C5); OpenAI's is reported around $40 billion (single aggregator, C3; a separate Axios "revenue chasm" piece on this is referenced in the underlying research but was not itself fetched).

Other viewsThe token-share number (open models carrying an ever-larger fraction of raw usage) and the revenue number (closed labs, especially Anthropic, still growing dollars fastest) are both real and both true at once on the sources found here — they describe volume and value, not the same thing, and no source in these reports reconciles them into one line.

The convergence theme

Stanford HAI's 2026 AI Index is confirmed to discuss a "Converging Frontier Model Performance" theme alongside "Jagged Intelligence" (C3) — models bunching near the top on aggregate scores while still failing unevenly on specific tasks. A more specific claim of "98% pathway overlap" between frontier models could not be confirmed — UNVERIFIED.

Stepping back

The direction of the errors, read off this one run. Every gap this piece could check ran the same way: a lab's own harness scored its own model higher than a neutral one did (Astra on ARC-AGI-3, by more than 35 points; Opus 5 on Nvidia's AVO, from ~30% to 100%); a lab's "best at X" self-report did not survive contact with an aggregated index (Sol's coding claim; Grok 4.6's breadth-of-wins framing against its own independently-sourced step-length number); and at least one specific figure that circulated in commentary (Nemotron 4's training budget) did not match the primary number once traced back. This is not evidence any one lab is dishonest — it is what happens whenever the entity being measured also controls the measurement.

The messenger, every time

There is no single author to profile in this piece — every lab quoted here is grading itself, in public, on evaluations it had a hand in choosing or running. That is the structural condition of this whole beat, not a flaw specific to one company.
