Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole surprise blindsides markets pricing a hold

Last edition closed with two catalysts still pending — Nvidia's fiscal Q2 report and Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote — and both resolved inside this short four-day window. Nvidia crushed its own guidance on Wednesday evening, sending the stock to its best single session since April 2025 on Thursday. Then Warsh, whom fund managers had expected to strike a neutral tone, told the Jackson Hole symposium on Friday that "the Fed's predominant focus right now should be on prices" — an explicit inflation warning that flipped September rate-hike odds from roughly a third to close to a coin flip within hours, dragging equities, bitcoin and the September-hold consensus back the other way before the weekend.

Fed · Jackson HoleFri 28 Aug

Warsh delivers a hawkish inflation warning, not the neutral tone markets had priced

In his first Jackson Hole keynote as Fed chair on Friday 28 August, Kevin Warsh told the Kansas City Fed's symposium that inflation remains "too high," citing 12-month PCE running at 3.7% and noting that 49% of PCE components have shown annualized increases above 3% over the past six months. That is a sharp break from the neutral tone roughly 69% of fund managers had expected — flagged as the consensus read in last edition — and Warsh said explicitly that "the Fed's predominant focus right now should be on prices." Markets repriced immediately: CME-tracked September rate-hike odds jumped to 57% from 35% the day before, while Polymarket's own "Fed decision in September" contract swung to a 53.5%-hold/47.5%-hike split (odds board, above) from last week's 67.5%/32.5%.

Context & background: last edition built its entire framing around this speech landing after its coverage window closed, calling the Nvidia-Warsh pairing "the clearest possible unresolved thread" and noting the market had already moved hard toward a hold ahead of it. It is now resolved, and the crowd was wrong-footed on the tone even as it correctly saw the event as pivotal.

The details: Warsh also used the speech to formally step back from forward guidance and the dot plot itself, arguing that "survey and market expectations adjust too slowly" to Fed projections and that pre-committing to a rate path "risks creating ambiguity in the name of clarity." He framed Fed independence around delivering results rather than communications transparency — "at the moment of truth, there are either reasons or results" — a different frame from the dot-plot-anchored guidance markets have priced off for over a decade. He separately flagged AI specifically, noting token sales for leading labs have reached over $100 billion annualized, and named the productivity and labor-substitution questions as still unresolved.

Why it still matters: not every outlet read the speech identically — CNN's own headline framed Warsh as having "stayed quiet on interest rates" while calling inflation "concerning," a softer read than CNBC's and Bloomberg's "signals possible rate hike" framing of the same remarks. The market's own repricing — a genuine, immediate move in both CME and Polymarket odds — is the more reliable signal than any single outlet's interpretive headline, and it is the basis for this card's framing rather than any one outlet's spin.

lightbulbEditor's view

Both pending catalysts resolved this week, and the crowd only got one of them right

1. The market had this exactly backwards on tone. Nvidia's beat crushed the compressed-beat-margin worry this briefing flagged last edition, while Warsh's expected "neutral" tone turned out to be the market's sharpest miss in weeks — a genuinely useful reminder that a well-flagged binary event can still surprise on direction.

2. Warsh chose results over guidance, and markets are now flying without the dot plot. His explicit break from forward guidance means future data prints, not projections, will move pricing going forward — expect sharper single-session moves around each release, not fewer.

3. Bitcoin and Treasury-yield-sensitive assets just demonstrated how fast the rate-path trade can reverse. Bitcoin gave back 3.3% in a single day the moment hike odds jumped, while gold's post-speech reaction is still an open thread this briefing could not confirm before publish.

4. Nvidia's demand story is intact, but on narrower footing than Thursday's rally implied. Under half of S&P 500 stocks advanced even as the index jumped on the earnings beat — a concentrated, not broad-based, relief rally.

5. Europe is not one trade. The DAX and CAC 40 diverged sharply within the same week on entirely different drivers — industrial/auto strength and cheaper oil for Germany, French fiscal concerns for the CAC — and the DAX even rose on the same session Warsh's hawkish tone hit US equities.

6. Oil's "de-escalation" story is not resolving cleanly. Brent ticked back up toward $90 this window even as a reported Hormuz "breakthrough" pointed the other way — worth treating as unsettled rather than closed.

This was a short window bridging two resolutions rather than a full trading week, and it delivered a clean split: the AI-capex catalyst resolved bullish, the Fed catalyst resolved hawkish, and the two collided on Friday to leave markets essentially flat for the stretch despite two of the largest single-session moves this briefing has recorded in months. Next edition should open by checking whether gold and Treasury yields have caught up to Friday's repricing, and whether the DAX's divergence from Wall Street held into the following week.
