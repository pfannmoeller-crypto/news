Weekly · 30 October–5 November 2026

Fishmeal keeps climbing as bird flu hits eggs, not pet food

A quiet week for factories and brands. The movement is on the input side: fish protein is still getting dearer after Peru called off its second fishing round, energy costs are rising faster than food ingredient prices, and the one new deal is a minority stake in Argentina. Two items that were being passed around as recent announcements turned out to be older, and we say so below.

LEADFISHMEALweek 44 · 26 Oct–1 Nov 2026

Peruvian fishmeal offers rise more than 4% for a third week running

Undercurrent News, a seafood trade publication, reported that sellers' asking prices for the top grade of Peruvian fishmeal rose by more than 4% for the third week in a row in the 44th week of 2026, with prices inside China up by as much as 5.4%. The run follows Peru's confirmation on 16 October that it will not open a second anchovy fishing round this year.

The full article is subscriber-only, so the exact price level is not confirmed here.

Context & background.Fishmeal is a protein powder made from small fish, mainly Peruvian anchovies. It goes into feed for farmed fish and into some pet foods, especially premium and cat recipes. Peru fishes anchovies in set periods, normally one in spring and one in autumn. Last week's edition (29 October) reported the cancellation and a record price reading for the 43rd week.

The details.Week 44 is the third consecutive weekly rise above 4%, so offers have climbed roughly 13% or more compounded over three weeks (our arithmetic from the reported figures, not a published number). The cause given by the Peruvian Production Ministry was a survey in which 79% of the anchovies counted were juveniles, which made further fishing harmful to future stocks. What is not publicly available is a dated price in dollars per tonne, any statement from a pet food maker about changing recipes, or any sign of buyers switching to other fish or to insect protein.

Why it still matters.With no new Peruvian catch before spring 2027, buyers draw on stored stock and on fishmeal from Chile, Norway and Europe, so prices are likely to stay elevated until the spring season is clearer. Whether this reaches pet food prices depends on how much fishmeal a recipe contains, which is not public.

POULTRYBIRD FLUconfirmed 19 Oct · reported 27 Oct 2026

Bird flu removes almost 935,000 egg-laying hens in Illinois; no pet food supply effect is reported

The United States Department of Agriculture confirmed highly pathogenic avian influenza, a fast-killing form of bird flu, in a commercial egg-laying flock of 934,600 hens in Clinton County, Illinois on 19 October. A relay of the report by Baller Alert on 27 October calls it the largest active poultry outbreak in the United States and names the owner as Rose Acre Farms.

No report links the outbreak to the supply or price of chicken meal or chicken by-products used in pet food.

Context & background.Chicken meal and by-product meal are made mostly from the parts of meat-producing chickens that people do not eat. Egg-laying hens are a different flock, so a layer outbreak mainly affects egg supply. Culled birds can in principle be sent to rendering (the process that turns animal material into meal), but the only source for that claim in the United States is an advocacy website, so it is not treated as established.

The details.The source for the numbers is the animal health agency of the United States Department of Agriculture (the Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service), quoted by a general news site rather than taken from the agency directly. A market-insight company claimed regional outbreaks can cut protein supply by almost 30% for feather meal; it is a commercial page and we do not rely on it. Earlier in 2026, a Pennsylvania layer flock lost 722,100 birds (3 February), according to Feedstuffs.

Why it still matters.The honest finding on poultry supply is that the public record shows an egg-sector shock, not a pet food ingredient shock. The thing to watch is an outbreak in meat-chicken processing regions, or an official change to the rules for sending culled birds to rendering. Neither has been reported in this window.

GAS / OIL27 Oct 2026 · September data

French statistics office: European gas price jumped 21% in September, food raw materials did not follow

INSEE (France's national statistics institute) reported on 27 October that the price of gas in euros rose 21.2% in September after a 15.1% rise in August, while oil in euros rose 16.5%. The prices of food commodities in euros rose only 0.2% over the month and were 1.3% lower than a year earlier.

For pet food makers, that means energy costs, which drive drying and cooking, have risen faster than the cost of most food ingredients so far.

Context & background.The European gas benchmark is the Title Transfer Facility (TTF) price, which most European plants' energy contracts track directly or indirectly. The French statistics institute's figures are monthly averages in euros, so they smooth out day-to-day swings. The release is a measurement of what happened in September, published during this week.

The details.Imported non-energy raw materials rose 0.6% in the month and 8.0% over a year, and industrial raw materials 20.5% over a year, so the squeeze is on metals and industrial inputs more than on food. There is no verified late-October gas price, so we cannot say whether September was a peak. A bank forecast from June expected much lower gas prices in the second half, which shows how far the outcome departed from expectations; that source is unverified and not relied on here.

Why it still matters.Pet food is energy-intensive to dry and cook, and plants sign annual energy and customer price contracts, so a September jump tends to reach margins and price lists with a delay of months. No pet food maker has publicly attributed a price rise to this this week.

INVESTMENTARGENTINAlate Oct–early Nov 2026

Distribution group Pilar Innova buys 35% of Argentine pet food maker Vitalcan

Argentine business press reported that Grupo Pilar Innova has acquired 35% of Vitalcan, becoming its largest shareholder, and will inject capital over the next four years. Vitalcan was already using Pilar Innova's network to distribute its products; that network covers more than 40,000 sales points and 22 logistics hubs.

Petfood Industry, a trade publication, adds that Vitalcan's new wet food plant in Tierra del Fuego is reported to run at only 20–25% of its installed capacity.

Context & background.Argentina is one of the larger pet food markets in Latin America, and Vitalcan is a privately held maker founded in 2001. The deal is a minority purchase by a distributor, not a takeover by a pet food group, and the price was not disclosed in the reports.

The details.The Petfood Industry piece describes dry food production as outsourced and nearing its limits, while the new wet plant has spare capacity. If accurate, the stake gives Vitalcan funding and shelf access to fill a plant that is largely idle. The capacity figures come from the Petfood Industry piece alone, and the full article could not be read, so they should be treated with caution. A different Argentine company, Grupo Pilar (Gepsa Pet Foods), is a separate business; nothing links it to this deal.

Why it still matters.It is the only newly announced deal this week, and it shows a pattern: capital going into capacity that already exists, rather than into new buildings. It is also a reminder that Latin America is where several large groups are currently building wet food capacity.

Editor's view

The pressure this week is on inputs, not on deals

  1. Fish protein is the live story. Three straight weekly rises above 4% after a cancelled fishing round mean that prices have not yet found a ceiling; the next evidence is the spring 2027 season.
  2. Bird flu is a watch item, not a finding. The Illinois outbreak is an egg-farm event. Nothing public ties it to pet food ingredients, and we have not suggested otherwise.
  3. Energy is the slower-burning cost. A 21% monthly jump in gas, with food raw material prices flat, squeezes processors before it shows in shelf prices.
  4. Verify dates before repeating a headline. Two announcements circulating this week were older than described. A short, accurate week is better than a long one.

Coverage is limited to what trade and business press and official releases made public; where an article was subscriber-only, the card says so.
