LEADPRODUCTION9–10 Sept 2026

Nestlé adds CHF 157m Thai plant, taking 2026 Purina capex past $1.4bn

Nestlé Purina PetCare announced a CHF 157 million (roughly $193 million) expansion of its Rayong, Thailand manufacturing site on 9–10 September, adding capacity for Felix, Friskies, Pro Plan and Purina One aimed at Asia, Oceania and Africa. It is the third large Purina capacity announcement of 2026, after a CHF 520 million integrated plant in Italy and a $550 million new-build factory in Batavia, Ohio that opened on 1 September.

Context & background.Purina's 2026 capex trail now runs across three continents: a CHF 520 million ($641 million) integrated wet- and dry-food plant under construction in Italy, the $550 million Batavia, Ohio factory that opened 1 September — Purina's first ground-up US plant in over 50 years, its 24th US site, producing Pro Plan, Purina ONE and Dog Chow at roughly 8 million pets' worth of output a year — and now Rayong. Nestlé Zone AOA CEO Rémy Ejel called the Thailand move a reflection of "confidence in its long-term potential across Asia, Oceania and Africa"; Purina CEO Hubert Wieser tied it to rising pet ownership and a shift toward premium packaged food.

The details.Rayong will supply Felix, Friskies, Pro Plan and Purina One both locally and for export across a region Nestlé says has more than 350 million domestic cats and dogs, and the company says the expansion will draw on locally sourced Thai raw materials. No completion date, added capacity in tonnes, or job-creation figure was disclosed in the announcement as reported.

Why it still matters.Three separate nine-figure-plus commitments inside a single year, spread across the US, southern Europe and Southeast Asia, is a heavier and more geographically deliberate build-out than Purina's usual cadence — it reads as capacity positioning ahead of demand growth in Asia specifically, not a one-off plant refresh, and DACH/EU competitors will be watching the Italian site most closely for what it signals about premium wet-food capacity in Europe.

FISHMEAL9 Sept 2026 · Peru / India

Peru's anchovy shortage pushes India's fishmeal export value up 145% in H1 2026

India's fishmeal exporters posted a 63% rise in volume and a 145% jump in value for January–June 2026, trade press reported on 9 September, as buyers priced out of Peruvian supply by an extended anchovy fishing suspension turn to Indian product instead. Peru normally supplies roughly a fifth of the world's fishmeal, and pet food formulators using marine protein are exposed to the same supply squeeze as aquafeed.

Context & background.Peru's Production Ministry has repeatedly extended an anchovy fishing suspension through 2026, citing warming ocean conditions tied to a coastal El Niño event and abundant juvenile fish in the catch. The 2026 total allowable catch was set at 1.9 million metric tons — the lowest in a decade and 36% below 2025's 3 million ton quota — and by early June only about a quarter of that had been landed. IFFO reported April 2026 fishmeal production down 21% year-on-year and fish oil down 19%, both led by the Peruvian shortfall.

The details.India exported 208,706 tonnes of fishmeal in the first half of 2026 worth $362.4 million, a 63% volume and 145% value increase on the same period in 2025, according to trade data reported by Undercurrent News (the article is paywalled beyond the headline figures). The publication frames the jump directly as displacement demand from buyers unable to secure Peruvian supply.

Why it still matters.This is a geography shift, not just a price story: pet food formulators — wet cat food and marine-protein treat lines especially — that built sourcing around Peruvian fishmeal now have a live alternative-origin data point in India, at a moment when Peru's supply constraint shows no confirmed end date. Whether Indian volumes can scale to replace a meaningful share of Peru's ~20% global position, or whether this is a temporary arbitrage, is the open question for procurement teams to watch into Q4.

BRAND MOVEMENTS9 Sept 2026 · FY2026 results

Blue Buffalo Wilderness needs 18-24 months as cat food and fresh carry General Mills

Reporting fiscal 2026 results on 9 September, General Mills said its Blue Buffalo Wilderness line faces "acute challenges" and needs 18 to 24 months of product renovation to return to growth, even as North America Pet segment net sales rose 6% to $2.61 billion for the year. The growth is coming from cat food and fresh formats, not from Wilderness or the core kibble line.

Context & background.General Mills bought Blue Buffalo for $8 billion in 2018 and added Tiki Pets and Cloud Star in December 2024 by acquiring Whitebridge Pet Brands for $1.45 billion. Wilderness, Blue Buffalo's grain-free sub-line, is the piece of that portfolio now singled out for a multi-year fix, while the original Blue Buffalo Life Protection Formula line is holding up better.

The details.Cat food is the clearer growth engine: Tiki Cat is growing at double-digit rates and Blue Buffalo Tastefuls at mid-single digits. Fresh dog food is accelerating fast off a small base — Love Made Fresh sales rose 80% in fiscal Q4 and a further 30% in fiscal Q1 2027, now stocked in roughly 6,000 refrigerated retail displays after earlier supply-chain and distribution delays. Total North America Pet net sales growth (+6%) was flattered by the Whitebridge acquisition and calendar effects; organic sales actually fell 3%, and segment operating profit rose 14% to $160 million.

Why it still matters.This is one of the clearest public data points yet on where premiumisation demand is actually moving inside a large multi-brand portfolio: toward cat food and fresh formats, away from legacy grain-free dry dog food, even for a brand as established as Blue Buffalo. General Mills is naming the shift itself as the reason Wilderness needs renovation, not just marketing — a signal worth watching against how competitors' grain-free lines perform over the same stretch.

Editor's view

Capacity is being built for Asia; protein sourcing is quietly shifting east too

  1. Nestlé's three-site build-out isn't random.Ohio, Italy and now Thailand inside one calendar year reads as deliberate positioning — a US refresh, a European premium-wet-food bet, and an Asia-Oceania-Africa capacity add specifically justified by pet-ownership growth in that region. Watch whether competitors match the Asia move rather than the US or EU ones.
  2. Fishmeal's geography is moving before its price fully does.Peru's shortage has been the story all year; what's new this week is the first hard evidence that buyers are actually re-routing to India at scale, not just paying more for Peruvian meal. If that persists past this quota year, it changes where marine-protein sourcing teams build relationships, not just what they budget.

A quiet week for deals, an honest one for everything else: two real capacity and sourcing stories plus one large public company naming a two-year fix for part of its own portfolio. Nothing here required reading past what companies and named trade outlets put on the record.
