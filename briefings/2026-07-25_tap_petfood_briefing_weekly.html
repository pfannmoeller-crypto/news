Feed Business MEA reported on 13 July that Italian-Serbian manufacturer Farmina Pet Foods is investing $23.5 million (roughly €20 million) to expand its Inđija, Serbia manufacturing hub — the company's largest single production facility globally, currently employing 415 people and generating over €170 million in annual revenue, exporting to 40+ countries. This briefing could not retrieve the full article text (access was blocked), so the figures below are limited to the headline amount and known context from Farmina's own prior public statements about the same site.

Farmina has operated in Inđija since 2010 and has invested over €50 million there cumulatively. In October 2025, Serbia's Agriculture Ministry and Farmina's majority owner Angelo Russo publicly announced a €20 million investment over two years to expand production capacity and introduce new lines — dental snacks for dogs and creams for cats — with Russo crediting "bilateral agreements... with more than 50 countries" for the export reach. The €20 million figure from that October 2025 announcement converts to almost exactly the $23.5 million figure in the 13 July report at prevailing EUR/USD rates.

Disclosed via the accessible reporting: $23.5 million investment figure, Inđija, Serbia location, "largest pet food manufacturing hub" framing. Not independently confirmed from the primary article: whether this is fresh incremental capital beyond the October 2025 €20 million plan, a currency-adjusted restatement of it as construction proceeds, or a distinct new tranche. Farmina's export intensity (97% of production shipped abroad per its own prior statements) makes Inđija a genuinely globally relevant node, not just a regional plant.

LEADFISHMEAL — UPDATE20 Jul
Update: Chinese fishmeal inventories hit a two-year low as hope of Peru's season reopening "fades"
Undercurrent News reported on 20 July that Peruvian fishmeal inventories in China have depleted to their lowest level in over two years, with prices at record highs, and that resumption of Peru's north-central anchovy season is becoming "increasingly unlikely." This is a direct, more pessimistic update to last edition's story, which as of 22 July had no fresher confirmation beyond Peru's 12 June open-ended suspension. Undercurrent's own weekly reporting series shows the same price-record narrative building continuously through May, June and July.
chevron_rightMehr lesen
Kontext & Vorgeschichte

Last edition tracked Peru's 12 June extension of its anchovy suspension (citing Coastal El Niño conditions and high juvenile-fish presence) and IFFO's April 2026 data showing fishmeal output down 21% year-on-year. Undercurrent News — a specialist seafood/marine-ingredients trade outlet — has run a near-weekly series on the same squeeze since at least early May: "fresh highs" on 18 May amid a 15-day "mega ban," a "fifth straight record" week in China on 8 June, further records on 29 June and a stabilization report on 3 July before the 20 July report of inventories hitting a two-year low. This is a continuously worsening (or at best plateauing-at-a-high-level) supply picture across more than two months of consistent reporting, not a one-off data point.

Die Details

Confirmed from the accessible portion of the 20 July article: Chinese fishmeal inventories at their lowest level in over two years; Peruvian and Chilean "super prime" fishmeal prices at record highs; resumption of the north-central Peru season described as "increasingly unlikely." The article sits behind a paywall, so specific price figures (USD/tonne) and an explicit ban-status statement could not be extracted — this briefing does not fabricate numbers it could not verify. No new IFFO monthly bulletin (which would carry updated year-on-year production percentages) was found more recent than the April 2026 data cited last edition.

Einordnung

The shift in tone from last edition ("open-ended ban, no fixed end date") to this update ("reopening increasingly unlikely") is itself the news: a supply event that formulators might have planned around as temporary is being reported by a dedicated trade outlet as looking more structural. Combined with China's inventory depletion — China being a major intermediary buyer/processor for marine ingredients — this raises the odds of the tightness reaching finished-product cost conversations later in the year, though this briefing again makes no price-pass-through claim of its own (that is the separate COGS briefing's territory).

Watch-Items

(1) Whether IFFO publishes a May/June 2026 bulletin with updated year-on-year figures. (2) Whether Peru's fisheries authority (PRODUCE/IMARPE) issues any formal statement on the north-central season's status. (3) Whether petfood formulators publicly reference fishmeal/fish oil substitution (e.g., toward algal oil — see below) specifically in response to this deepening shortage.

Analysis

Four reads from a thinner second week

  1. The fishmeal story got worse, not better, in seven days. Last edition described an "open-ended" Peru ban with no fixed end date. This week's update — Chinese inventories at a two-year low, reopening "increasingly unlikely" — is a genuine escalation in tone, not just a repeated data point. Nothing in this cycle's searches connected that tightening directly to petfood formulation decisions, which remains the gap worth watching.
  2. PE-backed bolt-on platforms are now running the same playbook in three different national markets at once. AlphaPet in Germany (last edition), Assisi Pet Care in the UK (this edition's Forthglade deal, its eighth since 2020) and The Nutriment Company across Germany and the UK simultaneously (this edition's pattern item) are structurally identical: PE-backed platforms doing small, price-undisclosed bolt-ons at a cadence of roughly one every one to two months. This looks less like isolated deal-making and more like a category-wide financial-sponsor strategy that happens to be visible in parallel across markets.
  3. Private label is following premium brands into new formats, not just competing on price. Fressnapf's fresh-food private-label launch (DACH, retailer-led) sits alongside Nestlé's wet-food capital investment (last edition, branded-manufacturer-led) — two different actors making the same bet on where premium demand is heading. That is a more interesting private-label signal than the discount-channel share-growth data already covered, which is why it is included here despite being outside the window.
  4. Production-investment news genuinely dried up this week — and that itself is worth noting rather than disguising. Per the brief's instruction to say so honestly rather than pad, this section is thinner than last edition's on purpose: no new plant announcement of Mantova's scale surfaced, so the section carries a smaller, less certain Serbian item and an older Croatian item rather than manufacturing false freshness.

Second-edition note: as flagged going in, this is a slower week on this beat than the first. Roughly half of this cycle's items are older than their section's standard window, each explicitly dated and labeled. That trade-off — a shorter, honest briefing over a padded one — is the same discipline applied last edition and will likely recur most weeks on a niche beat like this one.
