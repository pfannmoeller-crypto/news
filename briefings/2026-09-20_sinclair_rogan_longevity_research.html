David Sinclair on Joe Rogan: the longevity list, checked

Harvard geneticist David Sinclair's return to JRE — his last visit was 2019, by his own reckoning on air — ran roughly two hours fifty minutes, across his gene-therapy trial, a secret cocktail, GLP-1 drugs, supplements, red light and hyperbaric oxygen. This piece lists every intervention mentioned, records both men's views, checks the factual claims against primary sources, and adds the compiler's own evidence grade to each item.

What they say, in brief

Steelman first, verdicts later. Both positions as each man would state them himself.

Sinclair: information loss is reversible; the cheap levers still buy the most time

Ageing, in Sinclair's frame, is a loss of epigenetic information — and that loss is reversible: partial reprogramming with the OSK factors restored function in old and injured mouse tissue, and his company's first human trial, a gene therapy for glaucoma and NAION, dosed its first patient in 2026. He says he has to be "a good boy" while the world watches for a bad outcome. Short of reprogramming, he argues the cheap, boring levers — no smoking, little alcohol, sleep, movement, relationships, a plant-forward diet — buy "another 15 years" on their own, and that supplements and low-dose drugs like NMN, tadalafil and metformin are hormesis mimics worth taking meanwhile. On GLP-1 drugs he holds both a life-saving case and an eye-safety warning at once.

Rogan: n=1 hardware, scepticism toward sellers, a coach's veto

Rogan's frame is experimental and personal: a red-light bed, a hyperbaric chamber, a hypoxia-and-heat room installed the day of taping, two rings — each judged by feel, not by a trial. He is openly sceptical of anyone he suspects is selling something, whether a GLP-1 promoter or the Blue Zones brand, and notes that the loudest GLP-1 promoters often hold stakes in the companies. The one place he will not defer to n=1 experimentation is the gym: he insists resistance training is non-negotiable, and tells Sinclair — who admits he skips it — that he is getting him a trainer.

ER-100 / OSK gene therapy

"The first potential human age reversal technology is in people right now." — Sinclair

Life Biosciences' ER-100, an AAV-delivered dose of OSK, is now in Phase 1 for glaucoma and NAION. Sinclair cites mice and monkeys regaining vision, OSK-treated mice living "21 months eye, 19 months body" with fewer tumours, hedges the trial itself — "I'm not allowed to say… Nothing bad to report" — and admits going too young risks dysplasia. Rogan calls it a "horror movie," half-joking.

CheckMostly true C 5/5 — first patient dosed, Phase 1, glaucoma+NAION, NCT07290244 (9 Jun 2026); no readout yet. "Nothing's gone wrong" is the co-founder's word, not a published result.
CheckMostly true C 3/5 — mice: Lu, Nature 2020; the NHP data is a 2023 ARVO abstract only, not peer-reviewed.
CheckTrue C 4/5 — Yang, Cell 2023 (ICE mice); countered by Brenner's 2024 Cell "Matters Arising."
CheckWrong date C 5/5 — iPSC paper 2006; Nobel Prize (with Gurdon) 2012.
Grade: PLAUSIBLE, UNTESTED Rx — real trial, zero human efficacy data yet.

GLP-1s and NAION

"sudden blindness has doubled seemingly because of these meds" — Sinclair

Sinclair's hedge — "seemingly" — vanishes in Rogan's echo: "Sudden blindness has doubled because of GLP1s." Rogan adds, unprompted, "obviously correlation does not equal causation" — the caveat this piece's own grade turns on — then reads a blurb: "68.6 times more likely"; the on-air reply is "We only got the blurb." Sinclair counters that the drugs "probably saved millions of lives already."

CheckMostly true relative risk / Misleading framing C 4/5 — five estimates cluster near a doubling, not "68.6×": Grauslund 2.19×; Simonsen ≈2.8×; EMA "very rare," ≈1/10,000 patient-years; Hathaway/Rizzo 4.28–7.64× (high); Cai ≈1.3× (low). Usually partial, one-eye loss — not "blindness."
CheckUnverifiable — "68.6 times" is in no study or regulator record found.
CheckUnverifiable "saved millions" / Mostly true usage C 4/5 — no lives-saved count exists; SELECT (2023, n=17,604): 20% fewer cardiac events, ≈19% lower mortality over 3.3 yrs. KFF: ≈12% of US adults have used one.
Grade: ESTABLISHED cardiovascular benefit + very-rare NAION risk Rx — both half right; small absolute risk.

NMN, resveratrol, metformin

"one of the supplements that I take, which everyone knows about, NMN" — Sinclair

Sinclair cites a "2012" blood-vessel paper on NMN and his father's stack — NMN, resveratrol, metformin, exercise — without restating his own use of the latter two. He co-founded MetroBiotech, whose NMN candidate triggered the FDA's 2022 supplement exclusion.

CheckWrong date / Misleading mechanism C 4/5 — actual paper: Das, Cell 2018 — NAD+/SIRT1/H2S restoration, not removal.
CheckWEAK C 4/5 — Yoshino, Science 2021, n=25: insulin sensitivity up, no hard endpoint. FDA excluded NMN Nov 2022 after MetroBiotech's own drug filing, declared it lawful again Sept 2025.
CheckWEAK resveratrol / PLAUSIBLE, UNTESTED metformin C 3/5 — resveratrol's trials mostly null (Sirtris: $720M to GSK 2008, shut 2013); metformin's TAME trial has never run.
Grade: NMN WEAK, metformin PLAUSIBLE, UNTESTED — surrogates only; the recommender's company sells one.

Tadalafil 7 mg/day

"That is true. It's low dose. 7 milligrams a day of tadalafil." — Sinclair

Sinclair says he takes low-dose tadalafil for his hair (Rogan's echo — "That's why I take it for my hair" — reads as a joke in the captions) and for the prostate; the endurance line ("it's good for a lot of things… I know athletes who take it for endurance") follows a speaker change and is likely Rogan's. Sinclair cites a cancer paper "last week" that turns out to be two studies from two different years.

CheckMostly true, two papers conflated C 3/5 — the new one (Jul 2026) is cell/mouse-only on cholesterol trafficking; the head-and-neck mechanism is Califano's 2015 trial plus a 2025 survival association.
CheckMostly true prostate / Unverifiable hair C 4/5 — 5mg/day is FDA-approved for BPH; no hair trial found.
Grade: ESTABLISHED BPH/ED under a physician, NOT EVIDENCE hair/endurance Rx — a melanoma signal here is separately debated.

Red light and the eyes

"I don't need reading glasses anymore." — Rogan
"there's a lot of science now for red light" — Sinclair

Rogan credits red/NIR light — Joovv panel, red-light bed, Sinclair's shower brick and hair cap — with going from readers to none, though "in the beginning… I thought it was making things blurrier" and his eyes are just "way better than they were when I was 50." Joovv "were our sponsor at one point in time." Sinclair, on lutein: "I haven't done an experiment"; on red-light glasses: "I haven't found a big difference."

CheckWEAK contrast / NOT EVIDENCE presbyopia C 3/5 — Jeffery, UCL, Sci Rep 2021: single 3-min exposure, n≈20–24, one group, no independent replication found. Case reports (2023–25) note harm from unregulated devices.
CheckMostly true, narrower C 5/5 — AREDS2 (2013, n=4,203): slows progression in existing AMD only.
CheckMostly true for children C 4/5 — Torii/Tsubota 2017; outdoor-time trials robust in children; myopia is childhood axial growth, so there is no read-across to adult eyes.
CheckMostly true, modest C 3/5 — FDA-cleared laser caps: modest, industry-funded gains.
Grade: WEAK contrast, NOT EVIDENCE presbyopia, ESTABLISHED AMD and children's myopia.

Hyperbaric oxygen and hypoxia

"it's not so much the high pressure that's good for you, it's the coming down that mimics hypoxia" — Sinclair

Rogan has done 60 hyperbaric sessions and owns a chamber; Sinclair sat in the Israeli chamber himself. One of the two — the captions do not settle which — calls the wound-healing, memory and telomere data "incontrovertible"; Sinclair credits the hypoxia-like decompression, not the pressure.

CheckMisleading C 4/5 — Hachmo/Efrati, Aging 2020: n=35, single-arm, no control, no replication. Efrati co-founded the Aviv Clinics selling this protocol.
CheckOpen C 3/5 — the "hyperoxic-hypoxic paradox" is Efrati's own hypothesis, not established.
Grade: WEAK Rx — established uses are wounds, CO poisoning, decompression.
Where it is contestedBrenner — ChromaDex co-founder, a competing NAD+ product; wrote the 2024 counter. Kaeberlein — called the 2024 dog-supplement claim "the textbook definition of snake oil" and quit the Academy for Health & Lifespan Research; Sinclair then resigned as its president. Newman vs. Buettner — Newman's critique is an unpublished preprint; Buettner, who has a commercial stake, disputes it; Sinclair himself said "fraud" and then corrected to "records are hard" in the same breath. Efrati — Aviv Clinics co-founder, sells the HBOT protocol above. Independent support: Ocampo/Izpisúa Belmonte, Cell 2016; Sarkar/Hochedlinger, eLife 2020 — neither his lab — and EMA's NAION acceptance vindicates his eye warning.

Stepping back

The error direction in this run. Of 24 scorecard rows: 1 True, 12 Mostly true, 5 Misleading / wrong date / wrong figure, 2 Unverifiable, 1 Open, and 3 that are evidence grades rather than verdicts. The factual slips point one way — dates and figures rounded toward the more dramatic version (40 years for 88, 2012 for 2018, 117 for 122, "68.6×" for roughly 2×) — while the underlying science claims mostly hold up. The bigger gap is in the grading layer, not the fact-check: ten of the 23 listed items rate WEAK or NOT EVIDENCE for the main effect claimed on air. One run is not a base rate.

What survives

  • ER-100's first patient is real and dosed, Phase 1, NCT07290244.
  • The NAION risk is regulator-confirmed — EMA lists it as very rare, not a podcast theory.
  • The cheap levers — training, relationships, less alcohol, diet, coffee — are the best-evidenced items here.
  • The alcohol and relationships claims hold up against independent cohorts.
  • The National Geographic feature is real and names the cocktail candidate.

What to discount

  • Sinclair: NMN's mechanism and date, HBOT's "incontrovertible," tadalafil for hair.
  • Rogan: "68.6 times," a presbyopia cure, Blue Zones as "actual fraud" settled.
  • Critics: Brenner sells a competing NAD+ product, Buettner sells Blue Zones, Efrati sells HBOT.
  • Compiler: these grades are tiers, not effect sizes; one run is not a base rate.
