Elon Musk's companies · 30 October – 5 November 2026

Democrats won Congress and Texas after Musk's record election spending

Elon Musk runs Tesla (electric cars and robots), SpaceX (rockets, the Starlink satellite-internet service, and the former xAI, now its artificial-intelligence division), X (formerly Twitter), the Boring Company (tunnels) and Neuralink (brain implants). The week was set by the 3 November US midterm elections, which went against the side he funded, and by SpaceX moving into mobile phone networks. Most of the pending items from the previous edition, including Tesla's sworn answers to the safety regulator, produced no public news.

TESLARegulation3 Nov 2026

Europe cut how far above the speed limit Tesla's driver-assistance software may go, from 50% to 10%

On 3 November the Dutch vehicle authority approved changes Tesla asked for to its supervised self-driving feature in Europe. The feature is renamed "Tesla Assisted Driving", and the amount by which a driver can let the car exceed the speed limit falls from up to 50% to a maximum of 10%.

The setting also resets to zero every time the car is started. The existing approval stays valid, and the change arrives as a software update sent to the cars over the internet.

Context & background. Tesla's paid driver-assistance software, sold in the US as Full Self-Driving, still needs a human watching. In Europe the Netherlands' vehicle authority holds the approval, and nine other countries (Belgium, Denmark, Estonia, Croatia, Latvia, Lithuania, Slovenia, Slovakia and Czechia) have accepted it. A wider approval across the whole European Union (EU) is still under discussion in a confidential committee of EU governments, on which the Dutch authority declined to comment.

The details. Tesla itself asked for the review of what the authority treated as "minor changes". The authority found no unintended effects, so no new approval document was needed. German authorities had argued earlier for a clearer name, so that drivers are not led to think the car drives itself, and for a tighter speed margin; the new rules match those points. The change applies to cars with the newer generation of Tesla's onboard computer. This account comes from one specialist Tesla site that links the authority's notice; the notice itself has not been checked directly.

Why it still matters. Europe is the market where Tesla has been trying to widen use of its driver-assistance software, and a stricter speed rule shows what regulators demand in return. Tesla's American strategy leans heavily on this software (see the subscription figures in the previous edition), so Europe's conditions affect how much money that software can earn abroad.

Watch items. Whether other EU countries accept the approval; whether the wider EU committee imposes the same speed limit; how many owners switch the feature off.

TESLASalesOctober 2026

Tesla sales in Germany nearly quadrupled in October, but it still sold fewer cars there than a Chinese rival

Germany's federal motor transport authority (KBA) counted 2,932 new Tesla cars registered in October, 290.9% more than a year earlier. For the first ten months the total is 47,742, up 206.1%.

Tesla still had only 1.2% of the German car market in October, and a Chinese rival registered 5,173 cars, more than Tesla in the month, and is narrowly ahead for the year so far.

Context & background. Tesla's European sales collapsed in 2025 as buyers turned to rivals and some boycotted the brand. This year's increases are measured against that very weak starting point, so the percentages look bigger than the actual numbers of cars justify. Tesla's September in Germany was strong too, at 12,552 cars, or roughly 269% more than a year earlier.

The details. Germany registered 94,086 new battery-electric cars in October, 79.5% more than a year earlier, and they were 37% of all 254,492 cars registered. Behind the Chinese rival in October came Leapmotor with 3,661 cars (1.4%), and Xpeng had 0.5%. Tesla's factory near Berlin supplies the region. The figures come from the German authority via one specialist site.

Why it still matters. Germany is Europe's largest car market, so a recovery there matters for Tesla's income from car sales, which fell in the July–September results. But the whole electric-car market is growing faster than Tesla, and Chinese brands are catching up in the number of cars sold.

SPACEXTelecoms4 Nov 2026

SpaceX chose Qualcomm, the chip company, to supply the equipment for its own US mobile phone network

SpaceX announced on 4 November a multi-year deal with Qualcomm to supply radio equipment, signal-processing chips and software for Starlink Mobile, a planned nationwide network of ground-based 5G (fifth-generation mobile) towers. Qualcomm calls it its biggest 5G deal yet.

Building would start across tens of thousands of US cell sites in 2027. The aim is to compete directly with the three big US carriers rather than only fill gaps with satellites.

Context & background. "Spectrum" means the radio frequencies a network is licensed to use; megahertz (MHz) measures how much of it there is. SpaceX bought roughly 65 megahertz of middle-range frequencies from EchoStar (a US satellite and telecoms company) and up to 14 megahertz of low frequencies, which travel further and pass through walls better, from Grain Management (an investment firm). Satellite-to-phone service already exists; the new part is ground towers that give capacity where many people live.

The details. The Qualcomm equipment uses advanced antennas with 64 channels for sending and 64 for receiving, designed to carry more traffic at lower cost per user. SpaceX's president, Gwynne Shotwell, has described a hybrid design: satellites remove outdoor dead zones and towers add capacity where demand is highest. Earlier comments suggested small boxes near Starlink dishes on roofs; later regulatory filings also mention conventional towers. The report does not give the deal terms or the price.

Why it still matters. It turns SpaceX from a supplier of satellite links into a would-be fourth national phone company, a business that brings in very large sums each year but needs very heavy up-front spending on equipment. The three existing phone companies have been lobbying the Federal Communications Commission (the US telecoms regulator) against SpaceX's spectrum plans, and SpaceX still holds less spectrum than they do.

Watch items. The Federal Communications Commission vote on the spectrum; the cost of the build; whether Starlink Mobile signs a big shop or brand to sell its service.

SPACEXSpending1 Nov 2026

SpaceX's own forecast implies its sales more than doubling in a quarter while it borrows money to buy computer chips

On 1 November Musk defended SpaceX's spending plans on X, saying that computing done by satellites in orbit would eventually dwarf computing done on the ground: "Terrestrial compute will be peanuts compared to orbital compute" (that is, ground-based computing will be tiny next to orbital computing). The context was an analyst estimate that SpaceX must spend roughly $470 billion over five quarters.

Forecasts SpaceX gave on its earnings call, as reported: $35 billion to $40 billion of spending on equipment in October–December, and $33 billion to $37 billion of sales in January–March 2027, against $14.58 billion in July–September. Musk said SpaceX had just taken on a new $40 billion of loans to buy computer chips.

Context & background. SpaceX's results for July–September were covered in the previous edition; this card concerns only the follow-up. The company has said it wants data centres drawing 13 gigawatts or more of electricity (enough for several large cities) by the end of 2027, up from 1.7 gigawatts at the end of September. A gigawatt is a billion watts. Most of the equipment spending goes to one chip supplier, Nvidia; the analyst puts its share at 83% to 89%.

The details. The analyst Kyle Reidhead estimates about $230 billion could come from the money its business generates and about $60 billion from SpaceX's roughly $90 billion of cash, leaving about $180 billion that would have to be borrowed. A separate estimate from a partner at the investment firm Sequoia has spending of $88 billion this year, $290 billion in 2027 and $369 billion in 2028, with SpaceX using up $77.8 billion of cash this year before it starts bringing in more cash than it spends in 2028. SpaceX's finance chief said new computing equipment earns back its cost in under a year at current prices and Musk said that could fall to six months. All of this is secondhand: forecasts by outside analysts and statements on a call, not figures from an official company filing that has been checked.

Why it still matters. The plan assumes demand for AI computing stays strong enough to pay back the chips fast, and that SpaceX can launch the hardware into orbit (the estimate assumes 250 flights of its Starship rocket in 2028). If either fails, even a company with $90 billion in cash could run short quickly at this rate of spending.

Watch items. Whether the new loans show up in an official filing; the next quarterly report; any change to the computing-power target.

SPACEXGift30 Oct 2026

The first SpaceX shares promised to children are moving into savings accounts; Musk's own gift is still only a promise

SpaceX's president, Gwynne Shotwell, said on 30 October that more than 2 million SpaceX shares she and her husband own are going into children's "Trump Accounts" (government-started savings accounts) in December, and were "transferring to escrow as we speak" (that is, being placed with a neutral third party until handed over).

Musk's own pledge of one SpaceX share to every child in America in 2027 is unchanged: neither SpaceX nor the US Treasury (the government's finance ministry) has published a start date or method.

Context & background. Trump Accounts are savings accounts for children that the US government launched on 4 July; babies born from 2025 to 2028 get a $1,000 government deposit, but older children get nothing, so for the first recipients this stock is the first money in the account. The accounts turn into retirement accounts at age 18. Shotwell first announced the gift on 6 July.

The details. The first round goes to children aged 11 to 17 in postal-code areas where the typical (median) family income is $57,000 or less, plus areas too small for the national population count (the Census) to report. Families should have received a notice by Monday 2 November. A second gift, to be settled within weeks, covers children of military families abroad, children in foster care, and children in Texas counties near the Shotwells' home with median family income of $90,000 or less. Musk replied "Cool" to her post. SpaceX shares were priced at $135 when they were first sold to the public in June and were trading at about $135 on 4 November, so the value of the pledge has fallen from the $146 used in October.

Why it still matters. Shotwell's gift is a real-world test of whether shares can be placed in these accounts at all, which had been unclear since July. Musk's far larger pledge would depend on the same route and on tax rules that have not been explained.

Watch items. The December transfer; the second round; any Treasury or SpaceX statement on Musk's own gift.

SPACEXStarlink / UkraineReported 20–21 Oct 2026

Reuters and others report that Musk ordered a slowdown of the Starlink internet used by Ukraine's military, then it was restored

The Wall Street Journal reported on 20 October, according to Reuters, that Musk had ordered a slowdown the week before of the military version of Starlink, called Starshield, which Ukraine uses on attack and air-defence drones. Reuters could not confirm the report independently.

Ukraine's defence ministry later said speeds were back above 400 kilometres an hour (the cap affected how fast a drone could fly and still stay connected). This item was not in the previous edition.

Context & background. Starlink (SpaceX's satellite internet) dishes were first used in Ukraine in 2022. Starshield is the military version, sold under US government contracts. Musk had said earlier that the use of Starshield was ultimately up to President Donald Trump, according to Reuters.

The details. According to the WSJ via Reuters, Musk and Trump discussed the decision both before the slowdown and soon after it became public. Trump said on Friday 17 October that he did not think he or Musk was responsible for Ukraine losing Starlink access in a way that affected its operations. Other outlets, whose accounts could not be checked against the WSJ report itself, say Trump asked Musk on 21 October to lift the limit and that the White House denied ordering the slowdown. The reason for the cap has not been established. It came at a time when Trump turned on Ukraine's president and told Kyiv to stop attacking Russian refineries.

Why it still matters. It shows how much of a combat network a private company can switch on or off, and who answers for it when a government says it did not order it. It also bears on the possible merger: a combined Tesla–SpaceX would be both a military supplier and a carmaker under one owner, and would now face a Congress controlled by Democrats (see Politics).

Watch items. Congressional requests for documents on Starshield; any SpaceX statement; whether service stays unrestricted.

SPACEX AIProduct1–3 Nov 2026

Musk says Grok Bot costs too much to run, then pushes a phone-call feature that costs more

On 1 November Musk replied to a customer who had hit a weekly usage limit early: "The cost of serving Grok Bot was unsustainably high relative to revenue". Grok Bot is SpaceX's AI assistant, a program that carries out multi-step tasks for a user on its own. The weekly usage limits have been cut.

Two days later he told users to try a new function that lets the bot phone US businesses for them. Phone calls that last 25 to 50 minutes are exactly the kind of long task that uses up a weekly allowance quickly.

Context & background. Grok Bot launched around August as an assistant that runs around the clock on its own remote computer and is included in X's paid subscription tiers and some plans of Cursor (a tool for writing software), which SpaceX bought. On the results call in late October Musk called it "the fastest growth of a product" he had ever seen, and said there is no free version.

The details. The calling feature was announced on 25 October and reached all users on 3 November. In one demonstration a call lasted about 25 minutes and cut a customer's internet bill from $84 to $45 a month; another user recovered a $100 gift card after 51 minutes. Each call must say it is a virtual assistant and warns that it may be recorded; sales calls, mass calling and impersonation are banned, and only US numbers work. One user reported a representative refusing to cancel a subscription once it was clear an AI was on the line. SpaceX has not said how calls count against the weekly limit. All of this comes from one specialist site and Musk's posts on X.

Why it still matters. This is the first public number on what an agent product costs to run: a company with 800,000 paying users says serving them costs more than they pay, in the same week it adds a feature that needs even more computing power. It matters for SpaceX's claim that its AI division can earn enough to pay for the computers described in the spending card above.

Watch items. A price change or metered billing for Grok Bot; whether calling is capped; new versions of the underlying AI (none reported this week).

Context & background. Before the vote, Bloomberg reporting relayed by Mediaite put Musk's giving above $445 million, including $77 million to America PAC (a political action committee, a group that raises money to back candidates) in early October. America PAC (political action committee) had spent about $71 million supporting Republicans, much of it in Senate races in Texas, Ohio, Michigan, Iowa and Maine, and a separate group backing Paxton had spent about $181 million, funded partly by a $158 million Musk gift to the Senate Leadership Fund (a Republican group for Senate races). Why it still matters. In Texas, where Tesla's Austin factory, the Cybercab service and SpaceX's Starbase sit, Musk's companies now face a Democratic governor who, according to Teslarati, campaigned against some public subsidies for companies and floated a pause on building new data centres (the large buildings that house computers). These are interpretations from one outlet, not decisions.

Watch items. Official certified results; hearings or document requests from the new Congress; any statement from Musk on future political giving; Texas data-centre rules.

edit_noteEditor's view

A week when the politics Musk paid for turned into risk for the companies he runs

1. The election result is now a business risk, not just a political story. A Democratic Congress and Texas government can ask for documents, slow reviews and change state rules where Tesla and SpaceX are based. The share falls on 4 November are small, but the direction is clear.

2. SpaceX is turning into a telecoms and computing company. A phone network built with Qualcomm equipment and a spending plan in the hundreds of billions both point away from rockets. The funding case rests on company forecasts and analyst estimates, not on an official filing.

3. The AI product is popular and expensive. Musk saying Grok Bot costs too much to run is a rare public hint of what AI assistants cost, and it sits awkwardly next to a feature that can run for 50 minutes a call.

4. Silence is also news. Tesla's sworn Cybercab answers were due on 30 October and nothing has been made public; Starship Flight 15 has no date; the merger committees have no named members. Each is likely to surface in an official filing or a post before the end of November.

5. The Ukraine episode and the election point to the same question. How much does a government depend on one man's company, and how much does that company depend on the government? Congress now has the power to ask.

Most items this week rest on one specialist site and Musk's own posts; the C-Scores say so.
