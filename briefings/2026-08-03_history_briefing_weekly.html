10 YEARS AGO2016 Dubai crash-landing · Brexit rate shock · Rio opens
August 3, 2016WorldC-Score 5/5

Emirates jet crash-lands and burns out at Dubai airport

Emirates Flight 521, a Boeing 777 arriving from Thiruvananthapuram, India, touches down long, aborts the landing too late and slams back onto the runway at Dubai International Airport, sliding almost a kilometre before catching fire. All 300 passengers and crew get out alive; an airport firefighter dies tackling the blaze.

Why it still matters: Investigators trace the crash to a missed cockpit procedure during a late go-around, and the case becomes a standard teaching example in airline training on go-around decision-making.

August 4, 2016EuropeC-Score 5/5

Bank of England cuts its rate for the first time since 2009

Six weeks after the Brexit referendum, the Bank of England, Britain's central bank, cuts its key rate from 0.5 to 0.25 percent — its first move since March 2009 — and adds a bond-buying package worth up to 70 billion pounds along with cheap funding for banks.

Why it still matters: The package becomes the template for Britain's low-rate years after the EU exit vote and shows how fast a central bank can respond to a political shock.

August 5, 2016WorldC-Score 5/5

Rio de Janeiro opens South America's first Olympic Games

At the Maracanã Stadium, the 2016 Summer Olympics open with a ceremony that puts climate change at its centre; former marathon runner Vanderlei de Lima lights the flame. The Games arrive against the backdrop of Brazil's impeachment crisis around President Dilma Rousseff and a Zika outbreak.

Why it still matters: It remains the only Olympics held in South America, and the political turbulence surrounding it foreshadows the instability of Brazil's following decade.

25 YEARS AGO2001 Ealing car bomb · Genesis launch · Sbarro bombing
August 3, 2001EuropeC-Score 4/5

Real IRA car bomb explodes in Ealing, west London

Around midnight, a car bomb containing roughly 45 kilograms of explosives detonates in Ealing Broadway, injuring seven people. The Real IRA, a splinter group that rejects the Northern Ireland peace process, carries out the attack — one of three it stages in Britain within a year, alongside bombings at BBC Television Centre and in Birmingham.

Why it still matters: The bombing shows that dissident republican violence continues three years after the 1998 Good Friday Agreement — a pattern of splinter-group attacks that persists into the 2010s. The three men convicted are jailed in 2003 at the Old Bailey.
Wikipedia →
August 8, 2001WorldC-Score 5/5

NASA launches the Genesis probe to capture solar wind

A Delta II rocket lifts the Genesis spacecraft off from Cape Canaveral toward the Sun-Earth L1 point, where it will collect solar wind particles to bring back to Earth — NASA's first sample-return mission since the Apollo moon landings.

Why it still matters: Despite a parachute failure that damages the return capsule in 2004, the mission yields usable samples and becomes the direct forerunner of later sample-return missions such as Stardust and OSIRIS-REx.

August 9, 2001WorldC-Score 5/5

Suicide bombing at a Jerusalem pizzeria kills 15

A bomber sent by Hamas detonates an explosive device inside a crowded branch of the Sbarro chain at a busy Jerusalem intersection. Fifteen people die, including several children, and around 130 are wounded — one of the deadliest attacks of the Second Intifada.

Why it still matters: Accomplice Ahlam Tamimi, who scouted the target, is released in 2011 in the prisoner exchange for Israeli soldier Gilad Shalit; Jordan continues to refuse her extradition to the United States to this day.

50 YEARS AGO1976 Post-Franco amnesty · Big Ben breaks down · Viking 2 at Mars
August 4, 1976EuropeC-Score 5/5

Spain publishes its first post-Franco amnesty decree

The Spanish government under Prime Minister Adolfo Suárez publishes a decree-law, signed by King Juan Carlos I, granting amnesty for politically motivated offences from the dictatorship era; 287 prisoners are eventually freed. It is one of the first concrete steps of the Transición, the democratic transition that began after Franco's death in November 1975.

Why it still matters: The decree builds trust with the opposition and paves the way for the far broader amnesty law of 1977 and the Spanish Constitution of 1978.
Spanish Official Gazette (BOE) →
August 5, 1976EuropeC-Score 4/5

Big Ben's chiming mechanism breaks down for the first time in 117 years

At around 3:45 in the morning, metal fatigue fractures the flywheel arbor of the Great Clock's chiming train inside the Palace of Westminster's Elizabeth Tower, sending fragments flying through the clockroom and stopping the bells. A guard on duty first suspects a bomb.

Why it still matters: The repair takes nine months; the bells go silent until May 1977 — the longest stoppage in the clock's history to that point, and a recurring reference point every time Big Ben later falls silent for maintenance.

August 7, 1976WorldC-Score 5/5

Viking 2 enters orbit around Mars

The US probe Viking 2 fires its engine and settles into Mars orbit to scout a landing site for its lander, which will touch down on the planet the following month. Together with its sister craft Viking 1, it delivers the first long-duration American missions on the Martian surface.

Why it still matters: The Viking landers' biology experiments searching for life on Mars are still debated by scientists today, and the mission remains the technical foundation for every later Mars lander.
NASA NSSDCA →
Editor's view: Two of this week's nine events are NASA sample-and-survey missions arriving almost exactly 25 years apart — Viking 2 reaches Mars orbit in 1976, Genesis launches toward the solar wind in 2001. Both stand at the start of a lineage that runs through Stardust, OSIRIS-REx and today's Mars programmes.
