Iran de-escalation

Geopolitics · 5 underlying markets · A Hormuz deal reaches "final stage" drafting, but the touted signature date passes without one
sync_altWhipsawed again — real drafting progress on a Hormuz-specific agreement, but no signature by this edition and fresh attacks kept landing through the week

Iran and Oman moved into what Iranian officials called the "final stage" of drafting an interim agreement to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, and on August 5 President Trump said an announcement could come "as early as Wednesday." It did not: the deal is reportedly held up by a dispute over fees, with the proposed structure having ships enter the Persian Gulf through an Iranian-controlled route and exit through an Omani-controlled one, and Iran wanting to charge transit and security fees that the US is "strongly opposed" to on precedent grounds. CNN's own analysis called the emerging shape of the deal "not one Trump wants" — a framing that suggests real US concessions are on the table, not just Iranian ones. Axios: US nears Iran deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz CNN: an agreement is taking shape — but not one Trump wants

Meanwhile the shooting track kept running in parallel with the drafting track, exactly as last edition's Israel-axis point anticipated: Iran-backed Houthi militants in Yemen claimed an attack on Saudi Arabia's Jazan refinery, and a tanker operated by Abu Dhabi National Oil Co. came under attack in the strait over the weekend of August 8–9. Brent crude nonetheless drifted lower across the week — from $87.38 on August 3 to a low near $82 on August 7, settling around $84.18 on August 10 — a real, if partial, de-escalation discount building into the price even without a signed deal. Away from Polymarket's own pricing, actual market capitalization data this week shows Nvidia still on top at roughly $5.09 trillion, but with Alphabet's surge pushing it to about $4.55 trillion — ahead of Apple at roughly $4.49 trillion, meaning Alphabet has overtaken Apple for the #2 spot. That's a genuinely new development this cluster hadn't tracked before: last edition framed Alphabet as an emerging "third leg" behind an Apple-Nvidia race; this week Alphabet is outright ahead of Apple. Nvidia itself remains well off its own May 14 peak of $5.7 trillion, having shed roughly $900 billion since. Yahoo Finance: Alphabet overtakes Apple as world's second-most-valuable company

Polymarket's own "largest company, end of December 2026" market, however, is still not giving a clean, current read: this edition's search turned up a cached figure of Nvidia 69.5% / Alphabet 14.5% / Apple 10.1% — the same pre-re-mark split flagged as stale two editions ago — sitting alongside last edition's fresher capture of roughly Nvidia 54.5% / Apple 31.7% / Alphabet 10.5%. Neither figure reflects this week's actual market-cap flip of Alphabet past Apple, which is now the second consecutive edition this discrepancy has gone unresolved — a genuine limitation of news-mediated pricing this dashboard's standing caveat exists to flag. Context & background

Seven editions at a stale 69.5% gave way to a re-mark down to 61%, then to roughly 54.5% last edition, tracking Nvidia's real slide from its May peak. This edition's actual market-cap data confirms the underlying trend has continued and, in Alphabet's case, accelerated past what last edition's odds even captured — Alphabet wasn't priced above 10.5% last week, and this week it's the real #2 company in the world.

Price trajectory

Nvidia's real market cap: ~$5.7T (May 14 peak) → ~$5.09T (this edition), a roughly $600 billion further decline from the ~$900B-off-peak figure cited this week, consistent with a continuing, not one-off, slide. Alphabet's real cap has now visibly overtaken Apple's, a rank change this board had not previously captured.

The signals in detail

The stale-vs-fresh discrepancy persisting: this is the second consecutive edition this board has flagged conflicting Polymarket sources for the same underlying market, and this time the gap is arguably wider given actual market caps have moved even further than either cached number reflects — a concrete illustration of why this dashboard's standing caveat about news-mediated, possibly-stale pricing exists. Alphabet's durability question: last edition flagged the risk that Alphabet's earnings-week pop might prove a one-week move rather than a durable re-rating; this week's data, showing Alphabet still ahead of Apple several days later, is early evidence against the one-week-pop read, though a longer observation window is still needed.

Scenarios

(1) Nvidia stabilizes around the low-$5T level through year-end (~35%): the AI-buildout demand story continues to outweigh Apple and Alphabet's recent gains, consistent with Nvidia still holding a clear #1 by a wide absolute margin. (2) Alphabet's #2 position proves durable, becoming a genuine year-end contender (~35%): would represent a real re-rating of the "largest company" race's framing beyond the Nvidia-Apple duopoly this board used through mid-2026. (3) Apple reclaims #2 within the next few weeks (~30%): the gap between Alphabet and Apple's real caps (~$4.55T vs. ~$4.49T) is narrow enough that either could lead again on ordinary volatility.

Watch items

Whether Alphabet holds the #2 spot past this week or Apple reclaims it; Nvidia's next earnings date; and, specifically, a clean single-sourced update to the Polymarket "largest company" odds that actually reflects the current real-world ranking.
