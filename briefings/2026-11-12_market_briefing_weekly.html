US shares post their best day since April as oil prices fall
On Monday 9 November the S&P 500 rose 2.35% to 7,549.94, its best day since April. The jump came as oil prices dropped almost 5%.
●●●○○ 3/5
The details

The S&P 500 (an index of 500 large US companies) closed Monday 9 November at 7,549.94, up 2.35%. The 9 November edition had ended with the index 5.7% below its record after the worst day since April 2025 (Thursday 5 November); Monday recovered a large part of that fall. It is still about 3.4% below the 6 October record of 7,818.93. Over Monday and Tuesday together, the S&P 500 gained about 1.95% from Friday’s 7,376.86, the Nasdaq about 1.98% from 24,579.42 and the Dow about 1.84% from 51,285.73 (our arithmetic from the published closes). On Monday itself the Nasdaq Composite (mostly technology shares) rose 2.74% and the Dow Jones Industrial Average (30 large US companies) 2.15%. Monday’s rise came with an oil fall of almost 5%, which lowers the inflation worry. Even so, one report notes that Nvidia, one of the largest shares in the index, fell that day.

What to watch

Whether the oil-led recovery holds after the inflation figure (see the next story); Wednesday 11 November’s closes were not found.

Nvidia falls 5.5% and drags the S&P 500 down after the inflation figure
On Tuesday 10 November the S&P 500 lost 0.38% to 7,520.93, with technology the weak spot. Nvidia, the AI-chip maker, fell 5.5% and pulled the index down more than any other share.
●●●○○ 3/5
The details

After the inflation figure, the S&P 500 closed Tuesday at 7,520.93, down 0.38%; the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.74% to 25,065.87 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average 0.30% to 52,231.89. The technology sector fell 1.4%, the worst of the index’s 11 sectors, and Nvidia lost 5.5%. The index is about 3.8% below its 6 October record. The report we read ties the move to the higher-than-forecast inflation figure and to concern about the price of AI shares, and mentions “scrutiny” involving Nvidia’s chief executive Jensen Huang; we could not establish what that scrutiny is, so we do not treat it as a cause. Another report says the ten-year government bond yield (the interest rate the government pays to borrow for ten years) moved back above 5% on Tuesday, against 4.84% on Thursday 5 November. Nvidia has now fallen sharply on two of the last four trading days.

What to watch

Nvidia’s results, due later this month (date not confirmed in our sources).

Rates & inflationTOP10 Nov
US consumer prices rise 0.4% in October, above forecasts, and put a larger December rate rise in play
US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October from September, and 0.3% excluding food and energy, the third month in a row at that pace. Forecasts were for about 0.3% and about 0.2%.
●●●●○ 4/5
The details

The US Bureau of Labor Statistics (the government statistics agency) reported on Tuesday 10 November that prices rose 0.4% in October, down from September’s 0.6% but still high; excluding food and energy the rise was 0.3%. MUFG (a Japanese bank) put the middle of analysts’ forecasts collected by Bloomberg for the latter at 0.23%, so 0.3% is a clear step above. One news-collection site gives components: shelter +0.3%, food +0.5%, energy +0.7% (its gasoline figure conflicted with another table in the same release, so we omit it). No official year-on-year figure exists for October because the 2025 US government shutdown left a gap in last year’s data; the last one, for September, was 3.7%. One outlet citing the Associated Press reports that Federal Reserve governor Christopher Waller had said beforehand that a stronger response could be needed if the figure excluding food and energy reached 0.3%. It did. A news-collection site’s headline says the result raises the prospect of a larger December rise, meaning 0.50 percentage points instead of 0.25. The Fed holds its rate at 3.75%–4.00% with inflation well above its 2% target; the 74% December-rise probability from after the 6 November jobs report is the last confirmed one, and we found none after this report.

The other reading

One market-news site calls the report “in line” (as forecast); most others describe it as higher than expected. Another site’s headline quotes the Minneapolis Federal Reserve president, Neel Kashkari, as saying the direction is right; we could not read either piece in full.

What to watch

Minutes of the Federal Reserve’s 28 October meeting, around 18 November; the Fed meeting on 8–9 December.

Oil · Iran warTOP9 Nov
Oil falls almost 5% to a two-month low after Trump signals restraint on Iran
Brent crude, the standard international oil price, fell almost 5% on Monday 9 November, to its lowest since early September. US President Donald Trump had said he was unlikely to order new attacks on Iran while talks continue.
●●●○○ 3/5
The details

Brent was down about 1.5% at $96.91 early on Monday and later fell almost 5%; one report puts the low at $93.22. West Texas Intermediate (the US benchmark) was at $89.85, down 1%, early in the day. The restraint remark came from Trump on Sunday 8 November; he was due to meet the prime minister of Qatar, who is mediating, on Monday. Reuters reported that senior US and Gulf officials doubted a deal was close, and ships were still being attacked near the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow passage for much of the world’s oil, yet the price fell anyway. The 9 November edition had Brent at $98.29 on Thursday 5 November; reports differ on last week’s fall, from 12% to 16%. Some analysts also say the oil market is returning to more supply than demand because high prices are cutting demand. The war between the US and Iran is in its ninth month, and energy prices are why central banks, including the Fed, lean toward higher rates. Cheaper oil is the one force that could ease that inflation, and Monday’s fall helps explain the share rise the same day. Levels during the day differ between outlets, so each carries its reading time.

The other reading

The move rests on a statement, not on a change in supply, so it can reverse as quickly as it did on 5 November.

What to watch

Talks with Iran and shipping near the Strait of Hormuz; no closing price for Monday, Tuesday or Wednesday was found. The Geopolitics briefing covers the war and the strait.

Editor’s view

  1. The inflation figure is the story of the three days. Prices excluding food and energy rose 0.3% for the third month, above the 0.23% middle forecast, and the Federal Reserve now has a larger December rise to weigh. The honest gap: we have no post-report futures probability.
  2. Two forces pulled in opposite directions. Oil at a two-month low is good for inflation; the October figure shows prices have not yet followed. Shares rose on the first and fell on the second.
  3. Nvidia is carrying the market again. A 5.5% fall in one share pulled the index down more than any other on Tuesday. Its results, due later this month, are the next test, alongside the Federal Reserve minutes around 18 November.
  4. The oil move rests on diplomacy. Monday’s fall followed a remark, not a change in supply; see the Geopolitics briefing for the war and the Strait of Hormuz.
