Markets & Macro · Catch-up edition

Hot CPI and a widening Gulf oil shock push Fed hike odds past 90%, cracking the rally

This is a ten-day catch-up edition covering 6–15 September, closing the gap since the 5 September stack. Last edition's Wall Street recovery didn't survive the window: an August CPI print that ran hotter than forecast, a Saudi-pipeline-and-Hormuz escalation that pushed Brent above $107, and a still-solid jobs backdrop combined to flip Fed odds from a coin-flip to a near-certain 25-basis-point hike at Wednesday's meeting. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq each gave back roughly 1.5% of the window, the ten-year Treasury yield hit its highest level since 2007, and the DAX kept underperforming for a third straight stretch. Oracle beat cleanly on cloud and earnings but got sold on capex worries, gold gave back its early-September gain, bitcoin held close to flat, and a Senate cloture vote on the CLARITY Act — needing seven Democratic crossovers — lands today.

Wall Street drops three straight sessions as the 10-year yield tops 5%, highest since 2007

The S&P 500, Nasdaq and Dow each fell for three consecutive sessions into Monday's close as a hotter-than-forecast August CPI print (macro section, below) and a fast-escalating Gulf oil shock (energy section, below) combined to push Fed hike odds from roughly 66% to above 90% in under a week. The 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 5.02% by Tuesday morning — its highest level since 2007 — squeezing equity valuations directly. Semiconductor names added a second headwind: Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's public call to slow frontier AI development (covered in Frontier Tech this edition) hit Nvidia, AMD and SanDisk hardest on Monday, a company-story angle cross-referenced rather than repeated here.

Context & background: The prior edition closed with equities having shrugged off the first Hormuz strikes entirely, recovering to fresh highs by 4 September on a strong jobs report and a dovish signal from Fed Governor Waller. That recovery assumed the rate story was settling toward a hold. It wasn't: this window's CPI print and a second, larger oil shock reversed the rate calculus in the opposite direction, and equities are now repricing for a hike rather than a pause.

The details: Monday 14 September's session saw the S&P 500 fall 0.48% to 7,619.95, the Nasdaq 0.56% to 26,186.41 and the Dow 0.29% to 52,421.28, with the 2-year, 7-year and 10-year Treasury yields all hitting 52-week or multi-year highs the same day. By Tuesday morning, futures were sliding further as Brent pushed through $107 and the 10-year yield added another four basis points to 5.02%. Roughly 85–90.7% odds of a quarter-point hike are now priced for Wednesday's FOMC decision, versus under 50% two weeks ago. Semiconductor stocks bore a disproportionate share of Monday's losses after Amodei's AI-safety remarks; that story and its market read belongs to Frontier Tech, but the price impact on Nvidia, AMD and SanDisk is real and compounds this section's decline.

Why it still matters: A 25-basis-point hike would be the Fed's first rate increase since 2023, ending nearly three years of easing-or-holding bias and validating the hawkish drift this stack has tracked since the Jackson Hole surprise in late August. If Wednesday's decision confirms the market's pricing, the next question is guidance — whether Chair Warsh signals this is a one-and-done move against an oil-driven inflation spike, or the start of a longer tightening campaign.

Hot August CPI sends Fed hike odds above 90%, setting up a first hike since 2023

August's CPI report, released Friday 11 September, ran hotter than forecast on both counts that matter to the Fed: headline inflation hit 3.4% year-over-year (versus a 3.3% consensus) and core CPI held at 2.4% annually with a monthly print a tenth above expectations, driven by a 3.9% jump in gasoline prices. Combined with August payrolls' clean beat (+162,000 versus the roughly 30,000 monthly average) and producer prices up 5.4% year-over-year, the report erased any case for a hold: CME FedWatch now prices a 90.7% probability of a quarter-point hike at Wednesday's meeting, with a Reuters poll separately putting 85% of economists in the same camp.

Context & background: The last edition closed with CME's September hike odds at 49.5% and Fed Governor Waller on record saying it "would not take much acceleration in inflation" to push him toward a hike. That acceleration arrived within the window: gasoline alone accounted for more than a third of August's CPI gain, and the broader energy index rose 16.3% year-over-year as the Gulf oil shock (energy section, below) fed directly into the inflation print the Fed watches most closely.

The details: The monthly headline print (+0.4%) matched consensus, but the annual figure's overshoot and the tenth-of-a-point core miss were enough to move markets: Treasury yields jumped, equities sold off (equities section, above), and Fed-dated futures repriced from a genuine toss-up to near-certainty within the same trading session. A quarter-point hike would lift the federal funds target range to roughly 3.75%–4.00%.

Why it still matters: This would be the Fed's first rate increase since 2023, a reversal of the market's early-2026 expectation of cuts and the clearest sign yet that an oil-driven inflation shock, not just a resilient labor market, is now setting policy. Wednesday's actual decision and Chair Warsh's guidance on whether this is a one-off move fall just after this window closes and will lead next edition.

lightbulbEditor's view

Two shocks that offset last time compounded this time — and the Fed capitulated to both

1. The hawkish flip was data-driven, not rhetorical. Unlike the Warsh-speech-triggered repricing in late August, this window's move to 90%+ hike odds came from hard prints — CPI, payrolls, PPI, and an oil shock feeding straight into the inflation numbers the Fed watches. That makes it harder to argue the market overreacted.

2. Two shocks that offset last time compounded this time. The 5 September edition's story was equities absorbing a geopolitical shock and a hawkish drift simultaneously without much net damage. This window, the oil shock got bigger and the CPI print landed hot in the same days — and the S&P and Nasdaq each gave back roughly 1.5%.

3. Europe's underperformance is now a three-window pattern. The DAX first diverged from a recovering Wall Street on 4 September; it diverged again this window. A German index more exposed to bank funding costs and energy prices than the S&P is starting to look like a structural, not incidental, laggard.

4. Beats keep getting sold on cash flow, not growth. Oracle's quarter mirrors Broadcom's from the prior edition: a clean estimate beat, still-torrid AI-demand growth, and a stock that falls anyway because investors are now pricing the capex-to-cash-flow math, not just the top line.

5. Gold's safety bid lost to the rates story. A widening Middle East oil shock would normally support gold; instead it fell 5.4% on the window because the same shock also fed a hawkish Fed repricing that dominated. Watch whether Wednesday's actual decision reverses that.

6. Crypto's next catalyst is a Senate roll call, not a CPI print. Bitcoin is nearly flat through a week that moved every other asset class; today's CLARITY Act cloture vote — and the seven-Democrat math behind it — is the more consequential event for the space than anything macro this window produced.

Next edition should open by checking Wednesday's actual FOMC decision and Chair Warsh's guidance on whether this is a one-off hike, today's CLARITY Act cloture result and what it implies for 2026 passage odds (Galaxy Research's 10% estimate is the number to beat), and whether the Gulf oil shock stabilizes or a third escalation arrives before the next stack.
