Grok 4.7 misses its window; Boring Co. raises $3B; xAI appeals to the 8th Circuit

This is a widened, ten-day catch-up edition covering 6–15 September, closing the gap since the previous 5 September edition. The biggest thread is a prediction closing badly: the "3–4 weeks," surpass-Opus timeline for Grok 4.7 that the Moonshots podcast relayed in early September has now fully expired, and Musk has both missed a fifth deadline and quietly downgraded the model's own billing. Tesla's Cybercab audit and SpaceX's Starship Flight 14 both sit exactly where they were — open and unconfirmed, respectively — with no material movement to report beyond a status line. Neuralink stayed silent for a third edition running.

Grok 4.7 misses the "3–4 weeks" window Musk promised in early September, then gets downgraded

The claim the Moonshots podcast relayed in early September — that Grok 4.7 would ship within three to four weeks and surpass Anthropic's Claude Opus — has not held up. That window closed around 9 September with no release; on 11 September Musk posted that the model "needs a few more days to cook," attributing the delay to reinforcement-learning (RL) tuning that "penalized response length too heavily," causing the model to abandon hard tasks prematurely. Grok 4.6 (12 August) remains xAI's official flagship, priced at $2/$6 per million tokens; as of 14 September, Grok 4.7 appears on no official xAI product page, API listing or release note.

Context & background. This closes a specific loop this briefing flagged for checking: the prior edition relayed, from the Moonshots podcast, that Grok 4.7 would land within "3–4 weeks" of early September and would surpass Opus. It is the latest in a documented pattern of slips — Musk said "4 weeks" on 24 July (implying ~21 August), then "3–4 weeks" again on 12 August (implying ~2–9 September), then "10 days" on 2 September (implying ~12 September). Each target passed without a release.

The details. On 13 September, rather than shipping 4.7, Musk instead named Grok 4.8 — a 2.5-trillion-parameter model — as what's coming next, which two independent trackers both describe as effectively sidelining 4.7 rather than replacing it outright; xAI has still not put Grok 4.7 into the market under any name. Separately, and single-sourced only, one tracker reports Musk describing 4.7 on 14 September as "roughly on par with Opus 5.0, not 5.1 — better in some ways, worse in others," a marked retreat from the original "exceed all current models" framing; that specific comparison was not independently corroborated by a second outlet as this briefing closed.

Why it still matters. A prediction that does not land is worth reporting precisely because it was reported with confidence: this is the fifth missed internal deadline in roughly seven weeks, and the flagship model Grok users actually get remains August's Grok 4.6. Microsoft Copilot picked up Grok model selection inside Word, Excel and PowerPoint this window — xAI's distribution kept expanding even as its next-model timeline kept slipping, a split this briefing will keep tracking separately from the model-release thread.

Starship Flight 14's target slips past this window's close, from 15 to 18 September

The 15 September target this briefing reported on 5 September — sourced to an FCC filing and already flagged as "aggressive" — has passed without a launch. The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) added Flight 14 to its Air Traffic Control System Command Center operational planning on 11 September, listing a primary window of 18 September (12:15–14:14 UTC) with a 19 September backup; SpaceX has not publicly confirmed either date. Flight 14 would be SpaceX's first genuine orbital-insertion attempt for Starship, rather than the suborbital arcs flown to date, carrying roughly 20 production Starlink V3 satellites.

xAI appeals its Minnesota nudification-law loss to the 8th Circuit, seeking an injunction pending appeal

After Judge Donovan Frank declined to block Minnesota's AI-nudification law on 4 September, xAI took the fight to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the 8th Circuit, docketed as No. 26-2806 (X.AI LLC v. Ellison), asking for an injunction pending appeal to pause enforcement while its First Amendment challenge proceeds. xAI argues developers should not be liable for outputs "they cannot fully control." No briefing schedule or hearing date has been set as of 14 September, so Minnesota's law remains fully enforceable — fines of up to $500,000 per violation — in the meantime.

Context & background. This is the direct next step on the thread the prior edition closed with Frank's 4 September ruling: xAI did not accept the district court's denial as final and moved to the appellate level within roughly ten days, while the underlying district-court case (including Minnesota's own pending motion to dismiss) continues in parallel.

The details. Frank's own language undercuts xAI's urgency argument on appeal: he wrote that xAI "is a sophisticated and well-resourced litigant with the means to move quickly when its interests are at stake," and that a genuine fear of irreparable harm would have produced faster action than xAI took before the law's 1 August effective date. The 8th Circuit appeal does not reach the First Amendment merits directly — it is a request to pause enforcement while those merits are litigated.

Why it still matters. Minnesota's attorney general retains enforcement authority right now, unless and until the 8th Circuit grants a stay; no such stay has been reported. Separately, and without movement this window, Musk v. Altman remains dormant on appeal to the Ninth Circuit following Musk's May 2026 trial loss — a continuing thread, not a resolved one.

The Boring Company raises a further $3 billion, now valued at $23 billion, led by the UAE

Days after the prior edition reported the Boring Company's $20 billion round as closed, the company disclosed a further $3 billion Series D raise at a new $23 billion valuation — roughly four times its 2022 valuation of about $5.7 billion — led by the United Arab Emirates (UAE) and its affiliated investment entities, alongside returning backers including Sequoia Capital, Andreessen Horowitz and Temasek. edit_noteEditor's view

A promise that didn't land, and two threads that kept moving without it

1. Grok 4.7 is this window's headline precisely because it's a failure to deliver. The specific claim this briefing flagged for checking — a "3–4 weeks," surpass-Opus release relayed from the Moonshots podcast — expired without a release, and Musk's own framing softened from "exceed all current models" to, on one account, "roughly on par with Opus 5.0." A prediction that doesn't land is worth reporting as plainly as one that does.

2. Model releases and product distribution are decoupling at xAI. While Grok 4.7 stalled, Grok Bot gained Microsoft Copilot integration and its own dedicated conference this week — two different measures of company momentum that moved in opposite directions simultaneously.

3. Capital keeps outrunning operations at the Boring Company. A $23 billion valuation arrived barely a week after a $20 billion round was reported closed, for a company whose flagship product is still a single operating loop — the UAE's 150km tunnel pledge is the latest instance of investor capital tied to a non-financial obligation rather than treated as a passive stake.

4. xAI's legal exposure escalated procedurally, not substantively. The 8th Circuit appeal is a request to pause enforcement, not a ruling on the First Amendment question underneath it — Minnesota's law stays enforceable at up to $500,000 per violation regardless of how the appeal proceeds.

5. Two threads sat exactly still. Tesla's Cybercab audit and Starship Flight 14 both remain open questions rather than resolved ones — the audit with no findings after ten days, the flight with a target date that has now slipped twice since this briefing first picked it up.

6. A genuinely quiet company stayed quiet. Neuralink produced no new dated development for a third consecutive edition — stated here plainly, as instructed, rather than padded into a card that wouldn't hold up.

The next edition should carry: whether Starship Flight 14 actually flies near 18–19 September and reaches orbit; any findings or timeline from NHTSA's AQ26002 audit; whether the 8th Circuit grants or denies xAI's injunction pending appeal; and whether Grok 4.7 ships under any name, or is quietly superseded entirely by Grok 4.8.
