Cybercab's RSVP deadline lands today, and reporting says the 3 September reveal is smaller than the hype suggests

Tesla confirmed the Cybercab launch as an invite-only event in Austin on 3 September, with a public livestream. Invitations went to two groups — the highest-frequency riders of Tesla's existing Austin robotaxi service, plus mail-in sweepstakes entrants — with winners announced 26 August and the RSVP deadline landing 30 August, the final day of this window. Reporting published alongside the invite news frames the event more modestly than "Cybercab launch" implies: the vehicle will simply join the existing small Model Y robotaxi fleet, runs on the same FSD (Full Self-Driving) software stack as those Model Ys rather than new autonomy hardware or software, and no public sale price or wider-availability timeline has been announced. TSLA closed 28 August at $348.75, roughly flat against the Buy rating and $390.09 target from a 46-analyst consensus reported this window — both essentially unchanged from the $350.25 close and $390.09 target this briefing reported on 25 August.

Eleven days after the Minnesota hearing, Judge Frank still hasn't ruled on xAI's nudification-law challenge

The preliminary-injunction hearing in X.AI LLC v. Ellison (No. 0:26-cv-03425, D. Minn.) was held on 19 August before Judge Donovan W. Frank; as of 30 August, this briefing's research — including a targeted check of this window's news and an attempted direct docket check on CourtListener, which was blocked by that site's robots rules rather than returning a result either way — found no reported ruling. Eleven days is longer than the seven-plus days this briefing flagged on 26 August, and now exceeds the roughly two-day gap between Minnesota Attorney General Keith Ellison's 18 August opposition brief and the hearing itself by a wide margin, reinforcing that the absence of a decision is itself this window's notable fact.

Context & background. This is the second consecutive edition to report the ruling as still pending, and the gap has grown from "seven-plus days" to a confirmed eleven. This briefing attempted to move from an inferred absence (no news coverage found) to a direct primary check of the docket itself; CourtListener refused the automated request via its robots.txt rules, so this window's evidence remains inference from news coverage, not direct docket confirmation.

The details. Ellison's opposition brief gave Judge Frank two independent grounds to deny the injunction — procedural delay undercutting any claim of irreparable harm, and unlikelihood of success on the First Amendment merits — either of which he could rule on without addressing xAI's substantive drafting-flaw argument about the law's imported "intimate part" definition. An eleven-day gap is not unusual for a federal preliminary-injunction ruling on a novel First Amendment question, but it is notably longer than this case's own prior pace (a same-week TRO denial in July).

Why it still matters. Every additional day without a ruling is a day Minnesota's $500,000-per-violation law remains fully enforceable with no injunction in place — the practical stakes keep favoring Minnesota's position by default the longer Judge Frank takes, independent of how he eventually rules on the merits.
