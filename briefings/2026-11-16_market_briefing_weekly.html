US sharesTOP11–13 Nov
Nvidia’s chief executive resigns and shares first plunge 14%, then recover about half
Jensen Huang left Nvidia on 11 November; the chief financial officer is interim chief executive. The shares fell as much as 14% on Thursday, recovered about half of that, and fell again on Friday.
●●●●○ 4/5
The details

Nvidia told the US Securities and Exchange Commission (the market regulator) in a filing dated 12 November that its board accepted the resignation of Jen-Hsun (Jensen) Huang as president, chief executive and board member on 11 November, effective immediately. Colette Kress, chief financial officer since 2013, is interim chief executive and keeps that job; Harvey Jones, a board member since 1993, became chair. The filing gives no reason. On Thursday 12 November the shares initially plunged 14%, then cut the loss roughly in half while other AI chip shares rose (Associated Press). On Friday Nvidia fell again, and technology shares were the main weight on the market; no percentage was given in the report we read. The board has said no date for choosing a permanent chief executive.

The other reading

Earlier reporting (Fortune, 9 November) linked the board’s independent investigation, opened 8 November, to an alleged relationship between Huang and an executive suspected of ties to Beijing. We could not confirm this and the filing does not say it was the reason.

What to watch

Nvidia reports quarterly results on Tue 17 Nov (about 1:20 pm Pacific time, per a search summary), the first under the interim chief executive.

Sources

SEC filing (8-K), 12 Nov Associated Press via La Nación, 12 Nov Fortune, 9 Nov

Why 4/5: primary filing read in full plus the Associated Press market report; the reason for the resignation rests on a single outlet and is not confirmed. Friday’s Nvidia move was not quantified.

Traders put a December US interest-rate rise at about 85% after the October inflation figure
Interest-rate futures (contracts that bet on the Fed’s rate) now see a quarter-point rise on 9 December as likely. A half-point rise has about 13% odds.
●●●○○ 3/5
The details

After the consumer price index (the monthly inflation measure) showed prices up 0.4% in October, with 0.3% excluding food and energy, the CME FedWatch tool (a gauge built from interest-rate futures) put the chance of a December rise at 84.7%, down from 98.6% earlier that morning, one report says. FXStreet puts a quarter-point rise at 86% and a half-point at 13%; another report gives 13.6% for the half-point. The Fed is on hold at 3.75%–4.00% with inflation above its 2% target. The 74% reported after the 6 November jobs report was the last figure in our previous edition. No figure after Tuesday was found, and the October producer price report due Fri 13 Nov (what companies get paid) was not found.

The other reading

One analyst quoted says the Fed is unlikely to go for a half-point rise in December.

What to watch

Fed minutes (the written record) of its 28 October meeting, about 18 Nov; Fed meeting 8–9 Dec.

Sources

FXStreet, 10 Nov InvestmentNews, 10 Nov

Why 3/5: several outlets agree on the direction but give different percentages, and all figures come from search summaries, not from the futures exchange itself.

Oil12–13 Nov
Oil rises after Trump says he is no longer interested in an Iran deal
Oil climbed on Thursday 12 November and was little changed on Friday. No closing price for Brent, the benchmark oil price, was found for Wednesday to Friday.
●●●○○ 3/5
The details

The Associated Press market report for Thursday says oil prices climbed after US President Donald Trump said he is no longer interested in a deal with Iran; its Friday report says oil was little changed. Last time Brent had fallen almost 5% on Monday 9 November, to a two-month low, after Trump signalled restraint on Iran. So the move since then has been a partial reversal of that fall, and the war between the US and Iran is now in its ninth month. Oil matters to the Fed because higher prices feed inflation. We have no price level for the end of the week.

The other reading

Reports from 10 November quote Trump as saying large strikes on Iran are highly unlikely if Iran signs a very strong deal, which reads as less final.

What to watch

Any US–Iran talks and ship attacks near the Strait of Hormuz (the narrow passage for much of the world’s oil).

Sources

Associated Press via La Nación, Thu 12 Nov Associated Press via La Nación, Fri 13 Nov

Why 3/5: one wire report in two daily summaries; no price level and no second source for Trump’s remark.
