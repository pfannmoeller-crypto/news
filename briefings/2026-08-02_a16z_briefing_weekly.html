PORTFOLIO IPOBIOTECH30 Jul
a16z-backed cardiovascular biotech Braveheart Bio sets terms for a Nasdaq IPO, seeking up to $318.8 million
Braveheart Bio, a clinical-stage cardiovascular biotech backed by Andreessen Horowitz, set terms for its Nasdaq initial public offering on 30 July: 18.8 million shares priced at $15–17 each, aiming to raise as much as $318.8 million under the ticker BRVE. The company develops BHB-1893, an oral cardiac myosin inhibitor for hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) — a genetic heart disease in which the heart muscle abnormally thickens, raising the risk of arrhythmia and sudden cardiac death. Braveheart originally filed its S-1 registration on 15 July; the 30 July update set the actual price range and share count, which is what makes this dated within the current window. It is the one clearly new, on-the-record a16z item found this week.

Context & background

Braveheart is barely nine months old: it was founded in November 2025 by CEO Travis Murdoch, who previously led HI-Bio through its ~$1.15 billion sale to Biogen in 2024, with the board chaired by former Biogen CEO Chris Viehbacher. The company licensed BHB-1893 from China's Jiangsu Hengrui Pharmaceuticals in a roughly $1 billion biobucks deal in 2025 — the same Hengrui pipeline that also underpins obesity-drug developer Kailera's earlier IPO this year. Braveheart followed the licensing deal with a $185 million Series A backed by Andreessen Horowitz alongside Forbion, OrbiMed, Enavate Sciences and Frazier Life Sciences; a16z's exact stake and whether it is participating further in the IPO have not been disclosed in the filing as summarized in press coverage.

The details

The 30 July filing sets 18.8 million shares at $15–17, implying proceeds of up to $318.8 million before underwriter discounts. BHB-1893 already has positive Phase 2 data in both obstructive HCM (42-patient study, reported March 2026: "rapid and substantial reductions" in left ventricular outflow tract gradient) and non-obstructive HCM (84-patient study, reported May 2026: improvements in cardiac biomarkers, diastolic function and exercise capacity). Braveheart plans global Phase 3 trials — oHCM starting in the second half of 2026, nHCM in the first half of 2027 — competing against Bristol Myers Squibb's approved drug Camzyos and Cytokinetics' Myqorzo, which also reported positive Phase 3 nHCM data in May. More than 250 patients have received BHB-1893 to date across Braveheart's and Hengrui's programs; Hengrui is separately running its own Phase 3 trial of the same molecule in China.

Context

This is a biotech listing, not an a16z thesis move, and the a16z angle here is narrower than the deal-driven cards this beat usually carries: it confirms a16z as an early institutional backer of a life-sciences company reaching the public markets, consistent with the firm's broader bio investing arm, but the IPO itself is being driven by Braveheart's own clinical timeline and capital needs rather than any new a16z capital commitment. It lands amid a wider 2026 wave of biotech IPOs (Scribe Therapeutics, Apnimed, Attovia Therapeutics all filed in the same ten-day window in mid-July), suggesting a receptive listings market rather than anything specific to a16z's portfolio.
