UkraineTOP10–12 Nov
Abu Dhabi talks end with a prisoner swap agreed but no breakthrough on ending the war
Russia and Ukraine agreed a large prisoner exchange after two days of US-mediated talks on 10–11 November. Territory, the hardest issue, was not settled, and US envoys flew on to Moscow to brief President Putin.
●●●●○ 4/5
The details

Russia's chief negotiator Vladimir Medinsky said both sides agreed a large exchange, and Ukraine's Rustem Umerov confirmed it; numbers and date were still open, with final figures expected on 12 November. President Zelensky had floated swaps of 1,000-for-1,000 or 1,500-for-1,500 prisoners, and Kyiv hopes for a record swap and a deal by New Year, a Ukrainian hope, not an agreed timetable. The first day ran about four and a half hours (Kyiv Post) and covered humanitarian steps, prisoner releases and security guarantees. Ukraine's team said no broader framework could be built before a full ceasefire. US envoy Jared Kushner called the meeting “very tough” but productive and said territory would be hardest; he and Steve Witkoff then flew to Moscow. Britain, France, Germany, Italy, NATO (the North Atlantic Treaty Organization) and the European Commission met the Ukrainian and US teams separately, never with Russia.

The other reading

Reuters, as relayed by Meduza (a Russian news site based abroad), reported that Russia wanted the talks centred on postwar European security, with Ukraine only “one aspect”. Reports differ on whether the envoys would then meet Zelensky; one says in Dublin. We read only short summaries, not the full articles.

What to watch

The swap date and size, the official account of the Putin meeting, and any date for a next round.

TerritoryTOP11–12 Nov
Kyiv says it will not hand over Donbas, and the Kremlin presses wider European security demands
Zelensky said on 11 November that Ukraine will not surrender sovereign territory to end the war. Whether the US floated a specific border plan is disputed; Moscow still demands Ukraine leave Donbas, the eastern industrial region it has not fully captured.
●●●○○ 3/5
The details

The British newspaper The Telegraph reported that the US floated redrawing the borders of Donetsk and Luhansk along the current front line; a Ukrainian source said Kyiv would never recognise Russian occupation. The New Voice of Ukraine, citing the Institute for the Study of War (a US think tank), reported that the Kremlin revived demands it first made in 2021 about NATO (the North Atlantic Treaty Organization). The Kyiv Post reported that Zelensky rejected Russian demands for security guarantees, saying no one has attacked Russia for decades. Putin has repeatedly demanded a Ukrainian withdrawal from Donbas; freezing the line instead has appeared in earlier peace drafts. Our 9 November edition quoted Trump saying Ukraine would “end up with a little bit different shape”; this is the first reporting of a concrete border idea, and it is second-hand. All of this is from short summaries and headlines.

The other reading

A different Ukrainian source told Ukrainska Pravda that no formal US initiative existed.

What to watch

Whether Washington confirms or denies the front-line idea, and what Putin tells Witkoff and Kushner.

Ukraine11–12 Nov
Russia hit Kyiv with ballistic missiles hours after the talks ended, then a drone hit an apartment block
The Kyiv Independent reported ballistic missiles on Kyiv hours after the Abu Dhabi talks closed. On the morning of 12 November a Russian drone hit a nine-storey apartment building, injuring two people and starting a fire, per the Kyiv Post.
●●●○○ 3/5
The details

This repeats the pattern from our last edition: heavy strikes and diplomacy inside the same days. On 6–7 November Russia fired 36 ballistic missiles at Kyiv before the talks were confirmed. We have headlines and a short summary for these two reports, not the full text, so damage and casualty figures beyond “two injured” are unknown to us. Strikes continuing through a negotiation means the swap is not leading to a wider pause in fighting, at least for now. The Kyiv Post also carried a report of a Russian colonel killed in a bombing in St. Petersburg, part of a series of attacks on Russian military figures; we did not verify it.

What to watch

Whether a pause on energy strikes, one of the reported agenda items, appears in any official account.

IranTOP9–12 Nov
Iran denies any talks and says Hormuz stays shut; Trump says the US won but keeps the blockade
Iran's security official Mohsen Rezaei said on 11 November there are no talks with the US and Hormuz stays closed until seven conditions are met. Israeli media report that Israel is stepping up preparations to attack Iran.
●●●●○ 4/5
The details

Rezaei said the Strait of Hormuz will not reopen until the US meets Tehran's seven conditions. Trump said the US won the war and the naval blockade continues, after calling the war “effectively over” following a meeting with Qatar's prime minister on 9 November. Israeli media reported that Israel's security cabinet ordered the military to step up preparations for an attack on Iran, while Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu reportedly pushes Trump to resume the war; his office denied the call was hostile. Rezaei also warned of strikes on US bases in Europe if Trump resumes the war. The Times of Israel reported that Vice President Vance is weighing direct talks with a top Iranian security official, which Rezaei says is not planned. Kepler (an energy-data firm) puts regional oil exports at about 90% of pre-war levels, per NPR (US National Public Radio), which fits a stalemate with leaks. Correction to our 9 November edition: Fox News quotes Trump calling US attacks on Qeshm island “minor strikes” on Iranian air defences and launchers, so a US action did occur. Our Markets briefing covers oil prices.

The other reading

CNN (a US television news network) described US and Iranian officials as negotiating quietly, with Qatar mediating; Iran publicly denies it. Fox News and the Times of Israel disagree on how heated the Trump–Netanyahu call was, and the Israeli military chief is reported to have warned against ill-advised action. We could not confirm the cabinet order beyond Israeli media; all of this is known from summaries.

What to watch

Any Israeli strike, any Vance meeting, and the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit conversations with Xi Jinping and Putin that Trump mentioned last week.

Israel 1 story

Israel9–12 Nov
Eisenkot's road to 61 seats runs through ultra-Orthodox parties that want draft exemptions he refuses
Gadi Eisenkot has 28 days from 8 November to form a government and needs 61 of 120 seats. United Torah Judaism will not join unless he grants military-service exemptions, which he refuses.
●●●●○ 4/5
The details

The deadline can be extended by 14 days. United Torah Judaism also demands funding for its schools and the religious status quo; Shas, the other ultra-Orthodox party, had told its members to recommend Netanyahu. Avigdor Liberman, whose party sits in Eisenkot's bloc, rejected relying on Arab-led parties and warned a minority government would quickly bring Netanyahu back. Eisenkot's four-party alliance holds 48 seats. The bloc agreed to nominate Matan Kahana of Eisenkot's Yashar party for speaker of the Knesset (Israel's parliament), a first test of unity; United Torah Judaism said it would not back replacing the current speaker. Netanyahu calls for a national unity government; sources close to him floated a plan with Eisenkot, which the bloc rejects.

The other reading

Yair Golan reportedly prefers a minority government backed from outside by Arab-led parties, and there are reported doubts about former prime minister Naftali Bennett's commitment. A former Netanyahu aide said Netanyahu is in “panic”, one person's view. Most of this is from short summaries.

What to watch

The speaker vote and any signed deal with United Torah Judaism.

Editor’s view

  1. Abu Dhabi produced a swap, not a framework. Both sides called it useful, and the only concrete result is prisoners. Territory is where it stops.
  2. The Putin meeting is now the real test. If the US envoys come back with a date for another round, the track survives. We have no official account yet.
  3. Iran's public line and the reporting do not match. Tehran says no talks; CNN (a US television news network) says quiet ones. Both can be true, but we cannot tell which parts are theatre.
  4. Israel is the unpredictable factor on Iran. The strike-preparation reports are Israeli media only, but came the same week Trump called the war effectively over.

Next: the Putin account of the meeting, the swap date, whether Israel acts alone, and the Knesset speaker vote.
