Briefing summary — eighth edition

Fishmeal prices reverse as European gas pushes past €66/MWh on Hormuz risk

This cycle's clearest story is protein inputs pulling in two different directions at once. Chinese superprime fishmeal prices have fallen for the first time since this briefing began tracking the commodity's record run, easing on rising Chinese stocks and stronger-than-expected Peruvian production even before the main season has formally reopened — while a separate, primary-sourced USDA print shows US rendered protein meal (meat-and-bone meal, blood meal, feathermeal) still climbing sharply year-on-year. On the energy side, European gas has pushed past €66/MWh, a fresh high on Strait of Hormuz risk and the lowest storage level for the date in fifteen years, continuing rather than reversing the trajectory the 19 August edition reported. Industry side carries one dated, named acquisition — The Farmer's Dog buying enrichment brand Woof — but no new capacity investment or PE-fund-level deal surfaced inside this window, and packaging carries no fresh print since the 19 August edition's resin data; both are named as gaps rather than padded.

US porcine meat-and-bone meal prices climb roughly 46% year-on-year in USDA's latest weekly print

The USDA's Agricultural Marketing Service weekly Livestock, Poultry and Grain Market News feedstuff bundle, released 21 August 2026 and covering the week ending 14 August, put average US porcine meat-and-bone meal at $478.75 per ton, up from $328.75/ton a year earlier — a roughly 46% year-on-year increase. Porcine blood meal averaged $1,225/ton (range $1,100–1,350), up $200/ton year-on-year, ruminant blood meal averaged $1,450/ton, and 80% feathermeal averaged $1,250/ton.

FISHMEALwk33, 12–18 Aug 2026

Chinese superprime fishmeal prices fall for the first time since this briefing's record run began

Superprime fishmeal at Shanghai port eased to 14,950 yuan/tonne (roughly $2,086/MT) in week 33 (12–18 August 2026) — down 200 yuan from the previous week and down 1,400 yuan since a mid-June peak — reversing the run of records the 19 August edition reported through early June. The decline is attributed to rising Chinese stocks and stronger Peruvian production (approximately 582,080 tonnes from the north-central and southern regions as of 4 August), even though Peru's main-season quota assessment cruise does not begin until early September, with a formal season start not expected until late October.

Context & background. The 19 August edition reported five consecutive weekly record highs in China through week 23 (early June), with Peru's "mega ban" still running and no reopening date named. This is the first sustained price decline this briefing has tracked for this commodity since it began covering the beat — a genuine trend reversal, not just a pause.

The details. The mechanism is a supply-and-inventory story rather than a quota story: Chinese warehouse stocks are rising and Peruvian output from the already-active north-central and southern fisheries has come in stronger than expected, even though the separately-tracked main-season closure (the "mega ban" area) remains shut. IMARPE, Peru's fisheries research institute, does not begin its stock-assessment cruise — which sets the quota the industry hopes will land near 1.7–2 million tonnes — until early September, with the formal season opening not expected before late October.

Why it still matters. Price relief is running ahead of the actual quota decision, which means it could reverse quickly if IMARPE's assessment comes in below the 1.7Mt industry floor. Undercurrent News's own 14 August headline independently corroborates the same "prices drop" direction, though this briefing's specific figures trace to a secondary aggregator repackaging that (paywalled) reporting rather than Undercurrent's article directly.

GAS / TTF26 Aug 2026

European gas pushes past €66/MWh as storage falls to its lowest for the date in 15 years

TTF (Europe's Dutch benchmark gas price) traded above €66/MWh on 26 August 2026, having touched €68 earlier the same day, up from the €62.50/MWh the 19 August edition reported a week earlier and more than double the roughly €29/MWh level TTF opened 2026 at. EU gas storage stood at 62.99% full as of 24 August, against a five-year average of 79% — Germany at 51%, the Netherlands at 44.3% — with growing investor concern that the Strait of Hormuz could remain effectively closed into winter.

Context & background. The 19 August edition already had TTF at a three-week high on Gulf LNG disruption and heatwave demand; this print extends the same trajectory rather than reversing it, adding roughly €4/MWh over the intervening week.

The details. The storage figure is the more structurally significant new number: 62.99% full against a 79% five-year average is, per Euronews, the lowest level for the date in fifteen years — the only comparable precedent being 2021. Consumer pass-through is uneven across the EU: near-immediate in the Netherlands, but taking close to a year to reach households in Germany and Austria, meaning today's wholesale spike will show up in different EU markets' delivered costs on very different timelines.

Why it still matters. Gas is a direct cost input for extrusion, drying and rendering rather than a pet food raw material itself. Goldman Sachs' own scenario band — a €50/MWh base case against an upside above €100/MWh by December — is wide enough that EU producers budgeting winter energy costs right now are working with a genuinely unresolved range, not a forecast.

Analysis

Three reads from a cycle where protein inputs split in two directions at once

  1. Fishmeal and rendered protein are now moving opposite ways, which this briefing has not seen before. Superprime fishmeal's first sustained price decline since this beat began tracking it sits alongside a primary USDA print showing US rendered meat-and-bone meal up roughly 46% year-on-year — two protein-input classes a formulator might treat as loose substitutes, now diverging sharply on cost.
  2. The fishmeal reversal is running ahead of the actual quota decision, not behind it. Prices are already easing on rising stocks and early-season Peruvian output, weeks before IMARPE's assessment cruise even begins — worth treating as provisional relief rather than a resolved story until the cruise results and formal quota land in the coming weeks.
  3. Three straight editions, three different consolidation logics. Ever After Foods/Fishway bought protein-platform capability; VAFO/Pets Deli bought D2C scale; The Farmer's Dog/Woof buys brand-portfolio breadth outside pet food entirely. None of the three fund-level PE platform deals the "2026 as a new M&A cycle" trade-press narrative keeps predicting has yet surfaced with a name and a date in this briefing's own sourcing.
