It happened — but as of filing, no outcome has been reported.

Grok 4.6 ships publicly and gets its first independent benchmark score — a real jump, with a real latency catch

xAI publicly launched Grok 4.6 on 12 August 2026 — five days later than the 7 August ship date this briefing previously reported — priced the same as Grok 4.5 at $2/$6 per million input/output tokens with a 500K-token context window, and live immediately in Grok Build, Cursor, Grok Bot, and the xAI API. For the first time, an independent evaluator has scored it: Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index put Grok 4.6 at 61, a five-point jump from Grok 4.5's 56, putting it roughly level with GPT-5.6 Sol's max-reasoning configuration and just behind Claude Fable 5 (62) and Claude Opus 5 (63). The same benchmark flagged a real weakness: Grok 4.6 took roughly 42 seconds to produce a first token, against a roughly 2.9-second median for its price tier, and ran below-average output throughput at about 68 tokens per second.

The preliminary-injunction hearing in X.AI LLC v. Ellison (No. 0:26-cv-03425, D. Minn.) is scheduled for today, 19 August 2026, in St. Paul, before Judge Donovan W. Frank — the same judge who denied xAI's TRO (temporary restraining order) request on 31 July, ruling that "such a delay in bringing the action and the motion suggests that harm is not immediate." Minnesota's law, in effect since 1 August, carries penalties of up to $500,000 per violation; the law's author, State Senator Erin Maye Quade, and defendant Attorney General Keith Ellison have both publicly defended it, with Ellison stating it "does not inhibit free speech" but "puts guardrails in place."

XAINew case, venue lossruled 13 Aug 2026

xAI loses a separate venue fight as a federal judge finds its Grok terms "too faint" to bind users

In a Grok privacy class action distinct from the Ashley St. Clair case — Skaggs v. X.AI LLC (No. 26-cv-04550-BLF, N.D. Cal.), alleging ECPA (Electronic Communications Privacy Act) and CIPA (California Invasion of Privacy Act) violations — Judge Beth Labson Freeman denied xAI's motion to transfer the case to Texas on 13 August, ruling that neither Grok's sign-up screen nor its chat-input screen gave "reasonably conspicuous notice" of xAI's forum-selection clause. Both were so-called "sign-in wrap" agreements presented in muted gray-and-white text with no checkbox consent required. The order distinguished the case from the June 2026 St. Clair ruling (which enforced the same forum clause against St. Clair because she had conceded seeing the terms), meaning xAI now has one enforced and one rejected venue clause on its recent record.

Context & background. This case was entirely new to this briefing's tracking as of this window — it is not the St. Clair countersuit, and no prior edition has covered it. The order itself references St. Clair's June 2026 ruling as one of five precedents xAI tried to use, which is how this briefing confirmed the two cases are legally connected but factually distinct.

The details. The court's reasoning turned specifically on interface design: a forum-selection clause presented in low-contrast text with no affirmative click-to-accept mechanism does not, per this ruling, provide the "reasonably conspicuous notice" required to bind a user to a distant forum — a design-law intersection that could affect how xAI (and other consumer AI products) structure onboarding flows generally, not just this case.

Why it still matters. xAI now has a split record on forum-clause enforcement within weeks of each other — enforced against St. Clair, rejected against the Skaggs plaintiffs — meaning its litigation strategy of consolidating consumer claims in Texas is not a reliable tool, and the underlying privacy claims (ECPA/CIPA) in Skaggs will now proceed in California rather than Texas, a venue generally seen as less favorable to corporate defendants on consumer-privacy claims.

Watch items. (1) Substantive developments in the Skaggs privacy claims now that venue is settled. (2) Whether xAI redesigns its sign-up/consent flow in response. (3) Whether the St. Clair venue countersuit (still no new filings this window) produces any activity referencing this ruling.

The quiet week ended — a $60 billion acquisition, a hard legal date, and two product launches all landed in the same window

1. SpaceX's Cursor acquisition is the week's single most consequential fact. A $60 billion, stock-funded purchase of an AI coding startup by a launch-and-satellite company is not a natural extension of SpaceX's stated business, and neither company has yet offered a public rationale this briefing could independently verify beyond the filing itself.

2. Today's Minnesota hearing happened; its outcome did not arrive in time for this edition. That is expected given the timing, not a gap in this briefing's research — the ruling is the single most trackable open item heading into the next edition.

3. Tesla's narrative flipped from split to broadly bullish, but on execution promises rather than new financial disclosure. The improved $395.34 consensus target arrived alongside a Cybercab launch tease and a 500-truck Semi order — real commercial signals, though this briefing's sourcing on both stayed thinner than it would like.

4. Grok 4.6 is the first Grok release this briefing can score against an independent benchmark — and the two available scores disagree with each other. Artificial Analysis shows a real capability jump; LMArena's human-preference Elo is flat to down. That gap, not either number alone, is the story.

5. xAI's legal exposure widened, not narrowed, this window. A second, previously untracked venue fight (Skaggs) was lost even as the marquee Minnesota case reached its hearing date without resolution — two live fronts instead of the expected one.

6. A theme worth naming: Musk's companies are increasingly sharing capital and data across entity lines. SpaceX buying an AI startup, Grok 4.7 training on SpaceX engineering data, and renewed Tesla-SpaceX merger speculation are three separate, imperfectly-sourced signals pointing the same direction within a single week.
