China's superprime fishmeal price falls for a second straight week on rising inventories

Undercurrent's 28 August figures for the following week sit behind its subscriber paywall; this briefing reports the confirmed direction and drivers rather than an unverified number.

Context & background. The 26 August edition treated the week-33 decline as a genuine trend reversal after five consecutive weekly record highs through early June, but flagged it as running ahead of Peru's actual quota decision — IMARPE's stock-assessment cruise was not due to start until early September. This 28 August report is the first confirmation that the decline held into a second week rather than bouncing back, strengthening (not settling) that reversal read.

The details. Undercurrent's own headline and publicly visible summary attribute the continued fall to the same two mechanisms as before — rising Chinese port inventories and stronger imported Peruvian supply — rather than to any change in the quota outlook, which remains unresolved. This briefing deliberately does not carry forward or estimate a CNY/tonne figure for this later week; only the direction and stated drivers are confirmed from public information.

Why it still matters. Two consecutive weeks of decline is a firmer signal than one, but the underlying supply-and-inventory mechanism identified in the 26 August edition is still ahead of Peru's formal quota-setting process, due to open in early September with a season start not expected before late October — the reversal remains provisional until that process lands.

GAS / TTF27–29 Aug 2026

European gas eases from its record run as traders weigh a possible Hormuz reopening deal

TTF, which the 26 August edition put above €66/MWh (intraday to €68), settled at €66.47/MWh on 28 August 2026 — down 2.63% on the day, per Trading Economics daily data. The pullback lines up with the Al Attiyah Foundation's weekly energy review for the week ending 29 August, which put TTF's weekly settlement at $22.48/MMBtu (down 1.3% on the week) and cited "considerable speculation that a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz could be reached over the weekend," even while calling the underlying Hormuz transit disruption from the US–Iran conflict an unresolved upside risk.

Context & background. The 26 August edition reported TTF pushing to a fresh high on Strait of Hormuz risk and EU storage at its lowest level for the date in fifteen years. This is the first pullback this briefing has recorded in that run, not a reversal of the underlying risk premium — the Al Attiyah Foundation review explicitly still calls the Hormuz transit disruption an unresolved upside risk even as it notes reopening-deal speculation.

The details. The two figures cited (€66.47/MWh daily, $22.48/MMBtu weekly) come from different providers using different units and reference periods — a EUR/MWh daily print and a USD/MMBtu weekly settlement average — and are reported here as two independent, corroborating reads of the same direction (prices easing off the peak) rather than as a single reconciled number. This briefing did not locate a specific, sourced figure for 27 or 29 August individually.

Why it still matters. Gas is a direct extrusion, drying and rendering cost input rather than a raw material. A pullback on reopening speculation, with the underlying disruption still unresolved per Al Attiyah's own framing, means EU producers budgeting winter energy costs are watching a headline risk that could reverse quickly either way depending on whether that weekend speculation firms into an actual deal.
