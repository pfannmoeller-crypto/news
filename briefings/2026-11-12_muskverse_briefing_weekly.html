TESLAReversal7–11 Nov 2026

Within days of announcing the end of Cybertruck, Musk says the line will run until late summer 2027

Five days after saying Tesla would wind down the Cybertruck at its Texas factory around mid-2027, Musk now says production continues "through the end of summer 2027", according to the news site Electrek, which describes the reasons he has given as shifting. Local Texas news had already reported on 7 November that Musk would add a shift to the line to fill existing orders.

Electrek, a site that is often critical of Tesla, also disputes his claim that orders "far exceed" production, and a car-tuning firm has publicly offered to take the line over.

Context & background. The Cybertruck is Tesla's steel pickup, on sale since 2023. The previous edition covered Musk's 6 November post: production would end around the middle of next year to free floor space and scarce skilled staff for the Cybercab. The reasoning was labour and space, not weak sales.

The details. All of this week's new detail comes from Electrek headlines and search summaries of its articles; we could not open the articles themselves, and Tesla has published nothing. Electrek's 9 November pieces say Musk admitted Tesla lacks the people to build more than one car line at a time, that half the executives he called Tesla's strength in 2023 have left, and that Tesla's own figures show the line at roughly a quarter of capacity, which it argues contradicts the demand claim. An earlier Electrek headline (6 November) put sales at about a tenth of plan. Separately, a Stocktwits item (an investor social site) reports Musk saying Tesla will refund deposits to “a lot of people” as production winds down; we did not open it, so treat the wording as unverified. The News4 San Antonio report of the extra shift is a local TV station relaying Musk's post, so the extension itself rests on Electrek alone.

Why it still matters. The wind-down was Tesla's clearest sign that it is trading a product with buyers for a driverless taxi built in small numbers. A date that moves by roughly two months within five days suggests the plan is being worked out in public. What would settle it: a Tesla filing, or the 3 December shareholder meeting, naming a date and a deposit policy.

TESLAExpansion9 Nov 2026

Tesla opened driverless taxi rides in San Antonio, its eighth city, with a very large service area

Tesla began rides with no safety driver in San Antonio on 9 November, from 5 a.m. local time. The service area covers about 274 square miles, which Teslarati, a Tesla fan news site, calls Tesla's largest on a first day; Electrek's headline adds that the area exists "on paper", meaning the area is the permitted zone, not proof of how many cars operate in it.

Context & background. Tesla runs a robotaxi service, a taxi without a human driver, in Texas and Florida, using ordinary Model Y cars and, since September, the purpose-built Cybercab in Austin. The previous edition noted that a Cybercab start in Dallas had been promised for November but not reported.

The details. A summary built on Teslarati's report lists the eight cities as Austin, Dallas, Fort Worth, Houston, Miami, Orlando, Tampa and now San Antonio. That list shows Dallas already served, which fits a Model Y service there but does not answer whether Cybercab rides have started in Dallas, so that item stays open. We have no figure for how many cars run in San Antonio. Both accounts reached us as headlines and summaries; neither article was opened.

Why it still matters. A large area with few cars is a statement of permission, not of capacity. The number to watch is fleet size per city, which Tesla does not publish; the Texas vehicle register (573 Cybercabs in late October, per the previous edition) is the only outside count.

Context & background. Starship is SpaceX's fully reusable rocket. The previous edition covered the plan: catch the booster, then try the first ever catch of the upper stage with the tower's arms after about 22 hours in orbit. Earlier planning documents had pointed to 12 November, so the date has moved by a day.

The details. NASASpaceflight, a specialist spaceflight news site, in a report about five days old, said the ship's return to the tower at Starbase, SpaceX's Texas launch site, involves flying over populated areas on the way back and therefore needs a licence modification; until it is granted, the double catch cannot fly as planned. It adds that regulatory steps, above all the ship catch, could still push the date later. Space.com's article, updated on 11 November, still gives Friday as the plan. The regulator's general statements page carries a 9 November entry about a licence condition and permission for the ship to return to Earth, but the excerpt we saw does not say it concerns Flight 15, so we do not count it. Our attempts to open the regulator's Starship page and SpaceX's own mission page failed, so this status rests on two secondary reports and on the absence of a listing, which is weaker than a confirmation. If the licence arrives without the ship-return clause, SpaceX could fly the booster catch alone; nothing we found says that is planned.

Why it still matters. A launch date that depends on a regulator is a hope until the licence is published. Check the regulator's Starship page and SpaceX's mission page on launch day; this edition will not say how the flight went.

edit_noteEditor's view

Three quiet days, one open licence

1. The Cybertruck date is soft. A wind-down said to be mid-2027 became late summer 2027 in five days. Until Tesla writes it in a filing, treat any date as Musk's mood of the week.

2. Driverless-taxi growth is announced faster than it is counted. Eight cities and a 274-square-mile zone say where Tesla may operate, not how many cars do.

3. Starship's date depends on paperwork, not hardware. The vehicles are ready; the ship-catch licence is the open item, and we could not verify it from a primary page.

4. Source concentration remains a weakness. This edition leans on Electrek and two Starship reports, and on summaries we could not open. Nothing here is a filing except the Tesla proxy.
