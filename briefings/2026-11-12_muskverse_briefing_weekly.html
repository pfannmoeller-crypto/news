TeslaTOP7–11 Nov
Days after announcing the Cybertruck wind-down, Musk says the line runs until late summer 2027
On 6 November Musk said Tesla would wind down the Cybertruck pickup around mid-2027; five days later he said production continues through the end of summer 2027, per Electrek. Local news had already reported an extra shift.
●●●○○ 3/5
The details

Tesla’s Cybertruck is a steel pickup on sale since 2023. Electrek (a news site often critical of Tesla) reports the new date of 11 November and disputes Musk’s claim that orders “far exceed” production. Its 9 November pieces say Musk admitted Tesla lacks the people to build more than one car line at a time, that half the executives he called Tesla’s strength in 2023 have left, and that Tesla’s own figures show the line at about a quarter of capacity. An earlier Electrek headline (6 November) put sales at about a tenth of plan. News4 San Antonio, a local TV station, relayed on 7 November that Musk would add a shift to fill existing orders, so the extension itself rests on Electrek alone. On 10 November the car-tuning firm Unplugged Performance publicly offered to take the line over; Tesla has not replied. A Stocktwits item (an investor social site) reports Musk saying Tesla will refund deposits to “a lot of people” as production winds down; we did not open it, so treat the wording as unverified. All new detail comes from headlines and search summaries of articles we could not open; Tesla has published nothing.

The other reading

Electrek is the only outlet behind the extension and the capacity figure. The earlier reasoning, given on 6 November, was labour and floor space for the Cybercab (Tesla’s driverless two-seat taxi), not weak sales.

What to watch

Tesla’s annual shareholder meeting on 3 December; a Tesla filing or that meeting would need to name a date and a deposit policy.

Tesla9 Nov
Tesla opened driverless taxi rides in San Antonio, its eighth city, with a very large service area
Rides with no safety driver began on 9 November from 5 a.m. local time. The service area covers about 274 square miles, which Teslarati (a Tesla fan news site) calls Tesla’s largest on a first day.
●●●●○ 4/5
The details

Electrek’s headline adds that the area exists “on paper”: it is the permitted zone, not proof of how many cars run in it. Airport trips were not available at launch. A summary built on Teslarati’s report lists the eight cities as Austin, Dallas, Fort Worth, Houston, Miami, Orlando, Tampa and now San Antonio. Tesla runs ordinary Model Y cars and, since September, the purpose-built Cybercab in Austin. The list shows Dallas already served, which fits a Model Y service there but does not answer whether Cybercab rides have started in Dallas, promised for November in the previous edition; that item stays open. We have no figure for cars in San Antonio, and both accounts reached us as headlines and summaries; neither article was opened. A large area with few cars is a statement of permission, not of capacity.

What to watch

Fleet size per city, which Tesla does not publish; the only outside count is the Texas vehicle register (573 Cybercabs in late October, per the previous edition).

SpaceXTOPTarget 13 Nov
On the eve of Starship Flight 15 the date stands, but the licence for catching the ship is not confirmed
SpaceX still aims at Friday 13 November, with a 75-minute window opening at 4:45 p.m. Texas time. A specialist report says the US aviation regulator still had to approve flying the upper stage back to the tower; we found no confirmation today.
●●●●○ 4/5
The details

Starship is SpaceX’s fully reusable rocket. Flight 15 would catch the booster (Booster 22, the lower part) and then try the first ever catch of the ship (Ship 42, the upper part) with the launch tower’s arms, after about 22 hours in orbit; earlier planning documents had pointed to 12 November. NASASpaceflight, a specialist spaceflight news site, in a report about five days old, said the ship’s return to the tower at Starbase, SpaceX’s Texas launch site, flies over populated areas and therefore needs a licence modification; until it is granted, the double catch cannot fly as planned. It adds that regulatory steps, above all the ship catch, could still push the date later. Space.com, updated 11 November, still gives Friday as the plan, window 4:45 p.m. Texas time (5:45 p.m. US Eastern). The regulator’s general statements page carries a 9 November entry about a licence condition and permission for the ship to return to Earth, but the excerpt we saw does not say it concerns Flight 15, so we do not count it. Attempts to open the regulator’s Starship page and SpaceX’s mission page failed, so the status rests on two secondary reports and on the absence of a listing, which is weaker than a confirmation. If the licence arrives without the ship-return clause, SpaceX could fly the booster catch alone; nothing we found says that is planned.

What to watch

Friday 13 November: check the regulator’s Starship page and SpaceX’s mission page on launch day. This edition does not say how the flight went.

Editor’s view

  1. The Cybertruck date is soft. A wind-down said to be mid-2027 became late summer 2027 in five days. Until Tesla writes it in a filing, treat any date as Musk’s mood of the week.
  2. Driverless-taxi growth is announced faster than it is counted. Eight cities and a 274-square-mile zone say where Tesla may operate, not how many cars do.
  3. Starship’s date depends on paperwork, not hardware. The vehicles are ready; the ship-catch licence is the open item, and we could not verify it from a primary page.
  4. Source concentration remains a weakness. This edition leans on Electrek and two Starship reports, and on summaries we could not open. Nothing here is a filing except the Tesla proxy. Next edition: what Starship Flight 15 did or did not do, once it has happened.
