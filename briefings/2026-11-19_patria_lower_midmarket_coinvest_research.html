The argument in brief

Co-investment has become a deliberate way to pick assets. The most comprehensive study (Patria's phrase) finds no systematic adverse selection and a meaningful net edge over funds once fee savings are taken into account. Europe's lower mid-market offers depth, value-creation levers that do not need leverage, wider exit routes and entry prices near a decade low, and smaller funds have outperformed. Slower fundraising pushes GPs to offer more co-investment. But access runs through GP relationships and outcomes are widely dispersed, so what you select matters more than what you can access. This restates Patria's claims in its own terms.

The five load-bearing points

Scale: 5/5 primary source · 4/5 two independent sources · 3/5 single credible source · 2/5 aggregator only.

I. Co-investments beat funds, with no adverse selection 1981–2010 sample

"found no systematic adverse selection in the deals GPs offer to their investors" — Patria on Braun et al.

If GPs hand LPs their weaker deals, nothing else matters. The paper cites a 0.10–0.29 net PME edge over buyout funds.

CheckOpen C 5/5 conclusion · C 3/5 figures — The abstract confirms "no evidence of adverse selection"; simulated portfolios "significantly outperform fund returns". The PME figures conflict, and the gap runs against Patria (its numbers are lower).
Side by side (net PME, buyout): Patria fn 1: 1.46 (0/20) · 1.65 (fee-free) vs 1.36 for funds. Accepted manuscript, read by a research agent via a summarising fetch (C 3/5): 1.56 (0/20) · 1.59 (1/10) · 1.76 (fee-free) vs 1.36.

The conclusion holds; the exact margin is unresolved, and the discrepancy does not flatter the author.

II. Smaller funds outperform

"outperformed larger funds by 266 basis points at the median and by as much as 975 basis points" (at the top quartile) — Patria

The segment is defined as funds below €2bn and EV below €500m (Patria p. 2); the evidence uses funds below $5bn, and the top-30 list (fn 2) funds below €7bn.

CheckUnverifiable — Patria's own Preqin analysis, no data published. Footnote 4 concedes that "Much of the available market data is cut at €1 billion or $1 billion" and adds that the dynamics "apply with greater force at the smaller end of that range"; Patria states the threshold wherever it uses such data. Still, three different thresholds are in play. Patria also uses the spread for caution: "The dispersion data presented earlier cut both ways."

The direction has academic support; the magnitudes cannot be checked.

III. Prices are near a decade low, and the small-deal discount is structural

"The discount for smaller deals is persistent rather than cyclical" — Patria

Paper's own hedge: Q4 2025 was 8.3x, so 8.6x is a rebound.

CheckMostly true C 4/5 — 8.6x is the Argos Q1 2026 median across all buyers. Investment funds paid 10.0x; strategic buyers paid 7.8x. Argos's own report defines its scope as €15–500m in value (Patria fn 8 describes it as equity value; Argos's report reads as book value) and publishes no size bands, so it cannot show a structural size discount. The "three to four turns" is Patria's own Preqin analysis (unverified).

The multiple is right; the index does not show that financial buyers pay it, nor a size discount.

IV. Why now: liquidity and GP demand H1 2026

"Liquidity is showing green shoots rather than a full recovery." — Patria

The paper adds that the pick-up is "concentrated in larger transactions and distributions to LPs remain subdued".

CheckOpen C 4/5 for 23.2% — Patria: European exit value +59% YoY in H1 2026 (PitchBook data as of 30 Jun 2026), while US exit value declined. PitchBook's own Q2 2026 European PE Breakdown says H1 exit value was "pacing for 23.2%" YoY growth. No US comparison found. Both figures are shown; not resolved.
CheckTrue C 4/5 — Bain: buyout fundraising down 16% to $395bn; distributions 14% of NAV, below 15% four years running.
CheckMostly true C 4/5 — Bain/StepStone: median 33 cents per $1; quartiles 22c and 70c, upper 110c. "Most active" is undefined.

The squeeze is documented; the strongest exit number is the weakest-supported one.

V. Hard to access, hard to select, and Patria's programme

"what you select matters more than what you can access" — Patria

The paper concedes "More Opportunity Does Not Mean Lower Risk" and "None of this is as simple as it can appear from the outside", then presents Patria's own programme.

CheckUnverifiable C 2/5 — Inconsistent: text says 87% went to companies "above €1bn in enterprise value"; footnote 19 says "enterprise value above $500 million". No PitchBook source found.
CheckUnverifiable — 100+ deals seen and 70% co-underwritten are repeated on Patria's own website. The 0.3x TVPI is self-reported; the comparison is against the "broader set reviewed", which appears to include deals Patria declined. Scope per fn 22: co-investments since 2013 where Patria has a primary relationship; the 70% refers to deals since 2020 below €1bn EV.

The access and selection logic is plausible; every number offered for Patria's own skill is self-reported.

Where it is contested

Other views Adverse selection: Fang, Ivashina & Lerner (JFE 2015) found co-investments performed poorly, tied to GPs offering large deals. Lerner, Schoar, Mao & Zhang (NBER w24941): discretionary vehicles about −0.016 PME vs main funds, weak in the 2000s, strong in the 2010s; GP-directed about −0.10. Fees: Patria writes "reduced or no fees"; Torys (Q3 2026) lists what remains: admin charges ($50–150k), broken-deal costs (~20% of tracked deals), monitoring and transaction fees. Support: GSAM XIG (Sep 2026): 236 co-invests 2015–22 at 2.11x net MOIC vs 1.80x for parent funds (seller's own data, 3/5). Size: Braun, Dorau, Jenkinson & Urban (2022, 942 funds: doubling size cuts PME ~0.09) and Bhardwaj et al. (NBER w33596) support it; Harris, Jenkinson, Kaplan & Stucke (NBER w28109) find no significant buyout size effect after 2001. Skill: Harris et al.: weak buyout persistence post-2000; Braun et al. 2022: skill sits with managers (14.7% of deal-return variance) more than firms (4.0%); Lerner et al.: better LPs match better GPs (~0.38 PME), fund-of-funds weakest. Why GPs offer deals: GSAM asks whether co-invest is value-accretive or fills GP equity gaps in large deals; in the sources found, the need is documented mostly for large deals, which may sit uneasily with an EV below €500m focus (our inference; Patria points to co-underwrite and midstream demand).

What survives

  • Net-of-fee logic holds in the best study; a size effect has academic support; the fundraising squeeze and co-invest supply are documented.

What to discount

  • Specific magnitudes: 266/975 bp, 59%, 0.3x, 87%.
  • The "persistent rather than cyclical" discount, since the index cited shows no size bands and funds paid 10.0x; and the fee saving: Patria says "reduced or no fees", Torys documents costs that remain.
  • Both sides' evidence ages: Braun et al. (Patria's anchor) covers 1981–2010; Fang et al. also predates the 2010s, where Lerner et al. find co-invest strong; Harris et al.'s null size result does not test small funds separately.
