LEADCAPACITY31 Aug 2026

Purina opens its first ground-up US factory since 1975, a $550 million Ohio plant

Nestlé Purina PetCare cut the ribbon on 31 August 2026 on a $550 million, 1.3 million-square-foot factory in Batavia, Ohio (Clermont County) — its first facility built entirely from the ground up since 1975 and its 24th US manufacturing site. The plant will produce Purina Pro Plan, Purina ONE and Dog Chow, reaching an estimated 8 million pets a year, and currently employs 430+ people, scaling toward 500+ at full capacity.

It is described by the company as Purina's largest single manufacturing investment to date, built with automated storage and retrieval systems, robotics and digital manufacturing tools — a nine-figure capacity commitment from the sector's largest US player even as the broader industry's expansion pace has been reported slowing.

Context & background. Purina's most recent ground-up US build predates this briefing's entire run by five decades — every other US facility in its 24-plant network has come from an expansion, acquisition or retrofit rather than a new site built from scratch. The company frames Batavia as its largest single manufacturing investment on record, a scale that stands out against a wider industry backdrop of slowing operational expansions reported earlier this year.

The details. CEO Nina Leigh Krueger called it "the beginning of an exciting new chapter for Purina in Ohio," pointing to "a long-term commitment to American manufacturing, innovation and the communities where we operate." Factory manager Mike Ferritto credited "the combination of cutting-edge technology and an exceptional team." The site runs an Automated Storage and Retrieval System alongside robotics and digital manufacturing tools, and includes a Manufacturing Experience Center for community and workforce outreach — features aimed as much at labor availability and throughput as at raw capacity.

Why it still matters. A $550 million greenfield commitment from the category's largest US manufacturer, opening the same week as a mid-market private-equity exit elsewhere in the sector, is a useful contrast: strategics with balance-sheet scale are still willing to build new US capacity from scratch, even as deal activity lower down the market runs through financial sponsors rather than new construction. Worth watching whether competitors answer with capacity announcements of their own in the next few cycles.

FISHMEAL / PERU31 Aug 2026

Trade press frames Peru's 2026 anchovy collapse as a "systemic alert" for fishmeal supply

Aquaculture Magazine's 31 August 2026 review pulled together a season this briefing has already tracked piece by piece: Peru's Ministry of Production opened the 2026 first anchovy season on 1 April at a maximum 1.91 million tonnes — down 36% year-on-year and the lowest decadal quota outside 2023's outright cancellation — after IMARPE's stock assessment found juvenile-fish incidence above 50% in the 6–14°S zone. A mid-season suspension from 12 May left only around 451,000 tonnes (23.6% of quota) landed by 27 May, and the closure was extended a further fifteen days to 10 June.

The piece puts spot fishmeal prices at roughly $2,500/tonne by May 2026, about 25% above the 2023–24 El Niño peak, and names recurring Coastal El Niño-driven warm-water intrusion — not a one-off event — as the mechanism behind three constrained seasons in a row. Nothing in the underlying data is new to this window; the news value is the synthesis itself, published inside it.

Context & background. Earlier editions of this briefing tracked the price side of this story week by week — Chinese superprime fishmeal hitting record highs through June, then easing for two consecutive weeks by late August on rising Peruvian supply and Chinese port inventories. This 31 August piece is the first this briefing has seen step back from the weekly price print to treat the underlying quota compression as a multi-season structural risk rather than a single bad quarter.

The details. The quota timeline: 1 April season open at 1.91m tonnes; 12 May suspension across 6–14°S after juvenile incidence exceeded 50%; by 27 May only 451,000 tonnes landed, roughly a quarter of the total; suspension extended to 10 June. Prior-year comparison in the same piece: ~2.5m tonnes in 2024, 3.0m tonnes in 2025 — meaning 2026's quota is the lowest of the past decade outside the 2023 cancellation. Spot fishmeal reportedly reached $2,500/tonne by May, about a quarter above the prior El Niño-driven peak.

Why it still matters. Peru supplies the majority of the world's prime fishmeal, so a third consecutive constrained season reshapes formulators' input cost and availability planning across multiple quarters rather than one. The open question for the next cycle is whether the second 2026 season, typically opening around October–November, reopens at a similarly reduced level — that data point, when it lands, belongs in a future edition rather than being estimated here.

EXIT31 Aug 2026

Align Capital sells pet-wellness platform CompletePet to Blue Sea Capital

Align Capital Partners announced on 31 August 2026 the sale of CompletePet — a Miami-based developer, formulator and manufacturer of pet wellness products (soft chews, topical liquids, sprays, dental solutions, oils and powders) — to growth-equity firm Blue Sea Capital. Financial terms were not disclosed. The platform originated as Custom Veterinary Services, which Align backed starting in 2021, and became the sector's only dual-facility formulator after its 2025 acquisition of Green Mountain Animal.

Align says the business grew organically at 2–3x market rates over the hold, expanding from a single 55,000 sq ft site with 125 employees to 350,000+ sq ft and 500+ team members across two facilities — a five-year platform build now changing hands to a new financial sponsor rather than a strategic buyer.

Context & background. The release frames this as a five-year, single-sponsor buy-and-build: Align partnered with Custom Veterinary Services in 2021, then grew the platform primarily organically before the 2025 Green Mountain Animal acquisition added a second manufacturing facility and cross-selling reach, at which point the business took the CompletePet name.

The details. ACP co-founder Rob Langley credited "the talented team that embraced an ambitious growth strategy"; CompletePet CEO Worth Turner pointed to Align's "consistent strategic support and commitment to investing in our people." No purchase price, revenue or EBITDA figures were disclosed in the release, and this briefing does not estimate them.

Why it still matters. A clean sponsor-to-sponsor exit — rather than a strategic acquirer or a distressed sale — is a data point for deal-flow health in the mid-market pet-wellness segment even without a disclosed multiple; it fits the spec's framing of "platform buy-and-build, fund activity...exits" more cleanly than a brand-level acquisition would.
