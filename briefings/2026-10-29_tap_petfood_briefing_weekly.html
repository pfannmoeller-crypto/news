TOP STORYFISHMEAL16–23 Oct 2026

Peru bars a second round of anchovy fishing, and fishmeal prices hit a record

Peru's Production Ministry announced on 16 October that it will not allow a second round of anchovy fishing this year in the north-centre zone (fishing is only allowed in set periods). A survey by the Peruvian Institute of the Sea found young fish made up 79% of the anchovies counted, so fishing was judged harmful to future stocks.

Fishmeal, a powder made from anchovies, goes into pet food and food for farmed fish. Trade press reported on 23 October that sellers' asking prices for the highest quality grade of Peruvian fishmeal in China rose more than 4% in a week to an all-time high, as stored supplies shrink.

Context & background.Peru is the largest single source of fishmeal worldwide. Its anchovy fishing is allowed in set periods: normally one in spring and one in autumn, each stopped early if too many young fish are caught. This year's spring period opened on 1 April with a catch limit of 1,914,049 tonnes, lower than in earlier years, and fishmeal output fell sharply from January to March. Reports blame warmer ocean water, which drives fish away.

The details.The survey put the total weight of anchovies in the sea at about 5.12 million tonnes: 27.9% below the summer 2026 survey and 22.8% below the usual level for this time of year over the past 30 years (1996–2025). Undercurrent News, a trade publication, had reported on 12 October that a middleman who arranges sales was telling buyers the second round would not open, so the official announcement confirmed what the market expected.

Why it still matters.Pet food makers that use fish in premium or cat recipes buy against this supply. With no autumn fishing, there is no new Peruvian fishmeal until spring 2027, so buyers must use stored supplies or substitutes such as powder made from fish leftovers from Europe, Chile and Norway. An industry conference on ingredients from the sea was held in Buenos Aires on 19–21 October, with attention on the 2027 season. Insect protein is sometimes suggested as a substitute, but there was no news on it.

TAKEOVERDOG TREATS26 Oct 2026

Thai Union's pet-food arm completes a $160 million purchase of US treat maker Phelps

Thai Union, a Thai food company, announced on 26 October through its pet-food business i-Tail that it has completed buying Phelps Pet Products for $160 million, including the debts it took on.

Phelps, founded in 1966, makes protein-rich pet treats and air-dried foods for other companies, which sell them under their own names. It has plants in Illinois and Wisconsin.

Context & background.i-Tail has said it wants to grow in pet treats and in United States pet food. Buying a company that makes products for others gives it factories in the US and existing customers in the United States and Canada, among them retailers and other pet food brands.

The details.The company statement gives Phelps' 2025 revenue as $70.3 million, and says it grew about 25.6% a year from 2023 to 2025. The price is about 2.3 times a year of Phelps' revenue; that ratio is our arithmetic. The statement does not give profit, so we cannot judge whether that is cheap or expensive. It also did not say who sold the company or when the deal closed. Phelps' chief executive, Rick Ruffolo, called it a new chapter; i-Tail's chief executive, Roy Chan, called it a strong strategic fit.

Why it still matters.It is an example of an Asian pet-food group buying US manufacturing rather than building it, in a product category (air-dried and jerky-style products) that is growing faster than conventional dry food. One industry tally counted 23 pet-industry deals in 2025, down from 26 in 2024, and trade analysts say activity is picking up in 2026.

DEALSPRIVATE EQUITY15 Oct 2026

Mostly small deals: an investment firm buys CompletePet, The Farmer's Dog finishes buying Woof, Scales takes full ownership of Esro Petfood

A trade round-up published on 15 October lists recent deals. Blue Sea Capital, a private equity firm (an investment firm that buys companies to sell them later), bought more than half of CompletePet, a maker of pet health products, from another investment firm, Align Capital Partners; the current managers also put their own money in.

The dog-food company The Farmer's Dog finished buying Woof, a maker of dog toys and activities, announced in August. Scales Corporation of New Zealand bought the other half of pet-food ingredients firm Esro Petfood from its partner, Esro Food Group. Prices were not published for any of them.

Context & background.Investment firms usually sell what they buy after several years, and many purchases made in 2020–2022 are now due to be sold. Align Capital sold its stake in CompletePet in this deal.

The details.Prices were not published, so no comparison with other deals is possible. The round-up covers deals from September to early October.

Why it still matters.The mix of an investment-firm sale, a brand started by its founder being absorbed into a larger company, and a partner buying out the other half of a business shows deals running through mid-sized manufacturing and ingredient companies. The big company sales that analysts expected in 2026 have not happened yet.

FACTORYSTORE-BRAND PET FOOD24 Sep – 14 Oct 2026

United Petfood buys a third North American plant, in Arkansas, from Wellness Pet

United Petfood, a Belgian maker of store-brand pet food (sold under supermarkets' own names), agreed to buy a dry pet food plant in Decatur, Arkansas, from Wellness Pet, a US pet-food brand. It will keep buying food made there for years. Terms were not disclosed.

A separate plant of more than $30 million in Jonesboro, Arkansas is expected to create more than 150 jobs over three years, according to a 14 October industry update.

Context & background.This is United Petfood's second purchase of a Wellness Pet plant; it bought one in Mishawaka, Indiana two years earlier. It has bought several plants this year, in Canada and Turkey, and also runs plants in the UK, France and Poland.

The details.Reports differ on whether this is the third or fourth North American plant. Chief executive Dries Eeckhout said it lets the company make more food, more flexibly. Wellness may now pay United Petfood to make its food.

Why it still matters.Demand for store-brand pet food in North America is leading a European company to buy factories there.
