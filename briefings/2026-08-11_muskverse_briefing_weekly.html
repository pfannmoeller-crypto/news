Across 28 analysts the consensus splits 10 Buy, 15 Hold, 3 Sell, with a consensus target of $380.47 — about 16% above the current price.

TESLAMargin detail10-Q filed, covering Q2 2026

Tesla's own Q2 filing shows operating income cut in half and commits the company to more than $25B in 2026 capital spending

Granular detail from Tesla's Form 10-Q quarterly filing, not previously broken out in this briefing's Q2 coverage, sharpens the margin story behind the recent price-target cuts: operating income fell 56.9% year-on-year to $398 million, down from $923 million, while research and development (R&D) spending rose 49.2% to $2.371 billion. First-half 2026 capital expenditure (capex) — spending on physical assets like factories and equipment — reached $8.28 billion, and Tesla's own filing guides to more than $25 billion for the full year, a figure this briefing had not previously had in hand.

xAI ships a hands-free Grok Voice Connector that turns spoken requests into actions across a user's connected apps

xAI shipped a new "Voice connector" feature on 10 August, merging its existing Voice Mode with its Connectors framework so spoken requests can trigger actions in linked apps directly, free on iOS, Android, and web with no separate toggle required. Launch use cases include routing voice memos into connected apps, generating personalized podcast-style summaries of a user's day, and delivering automated recurring "daily brief" voice summaries pulled from calendar and email data. The feature runs on Grok Voice Think Fast 2.0, a speech-to-speech model xAI announced 29 July supporting more than 25 languages and tuned for noisy environments and varied accents, paired with Grok 4.5 for reasoning; the underlying grok-voice-latest application programming interface (API) alias began routing to the new model on 5 August, priced at $0.08 per audio minute.

XAIArgument sharpensfiled 27 Jul · analysis 1 Aug · hearing 19 Aug

As Minnesota's 19 August hearing nears, xAI's case zeroes in on the nudification law's missing intent requirement

No new filings or rulings emerged in the Minnesota nudification case this window — the preliminary-injunction hearing remains set for 19 August in St. Paul, exactly where the 10 August edition left it. But this window's research surfaced sharper detail on xAI's underlying legal argument: the case is formally captioned X.AI LLC v. Ellison, No. 0:26-cv-03425 (D. Minn.), filed 27 July, and xAI's core drafting-flaw argument is that Minnesota imported its "intimate part" definition from the state's criminal sexual-conduct code (Minn. Stat. § 609.341, subd. 5) rather than from its own narrower, pre-existing deepfake statute — and that the resulting law imposes strict liability with no scienter requirement, meaning no need to prove a defendant knew or intended the violation.

Context & background. The 10 August edition covered the TRO denial and the $50 billion worst-case exposure figure xAI's attorneys cited, but had not yet surfaced the specific statutory-drafting theory underlying xAI's constitutional challenge. This window's research fills that gap with legal-analysis detail rather than new docket activity — the hearing date, and the fact of no new filings, are unchanged from the prior edition.

The details. The "intimate part" definition being borrowed from Minnesota's criminal sexual-conduct statute, rather than its own deepfake-specific law, is the specific textual hook xAI's filing uses to argue the statute sweeps more broadly than the legislature intended for AI-generated content specifically. Pairing that with a strict-liability, no-scienter structure is a First Amendment-adjacent argument distinct from the $50 billion exposure figure already reported: the exposure number is about penalty scale, while the scienter argument is about whether the law can constitutionally apply to conduct without proven intent at all.

Why it still matters. A sharper legal theory ahead of a preliminary-injunction hearing does not change the law's current in-force status or predict the hearing's outcome, but it does mean the 19 August hearing is more likely to turn on this specific drafting question — how the "intimate part" definition was sourced and whether scienter is constitutionally required — than on the broader public debate about nudification bans generally.

Watch items. (1) The 19 August hearing itself. (2) Whether Minnesota's Attorney General's office directly rebuts the scienter and cross-referenced-statute arguments. (3) Whether any other state's pending nudification legislation is amended in response to this specific drafting critique. (4) Whether St. Clair's separate suit against xAI (still with no new filings this window, and its own venue countersuit unresolved) or Musk v. edit_noteEditor's view

The quietest window this briefing has tracked in months — the real news this week is detail, not events

1. No earnings, no lock-up, no ship launch, no ruling. Every open thread from the 10 August edition is either unchanged or has been filled in with sharper detail rather than resolved — a genuinely slow news week across a portfolio that rarely has one.

2. Tesla's story split further rather than clarifying. Retail investors nearly tripled their weekly buying at the same time the company's own SEC filing confirmed operating income fell by more than half — both are true of the same stock simultaneously.

3. xAI shipped a real product while leaving its harder questions open. The Grok Voice Connector is a genuine, priced, dated release; Grok 4.6's missing independent benchmarks and Grok 4.7's exact ship date are not.

4. The week's single sharpest fact came from a government filing, not a press release. America PAC's own FEC paperwork shows $274,171 in cash on hand against a publicly reported $100–120M pledge — a gap the pledge's own coverage never surfaced.

5. Two of three open legal threads produced nothing; the third produced argument, not resolution. St. Clair and Musk v. Altman sit exactly where they did; Minnesota's case gained a specific statutory theory (no scienter requirement) ahead of its 19 August hearing, without changing the law's current in-force status.

A week defined by absence of catalysts more than by any single story — which made the discrepancies (Tesla's retail-versus-fundamentals split, America PAC's cash gap) more visible than they might otherwise have been. The next edition should have the 19 August Minnesota hearing outcome, whichever way Grok 4.7's timeline resolves, and — if SpaceX finally announces one — an actual Starship Flight 14 launch window.
